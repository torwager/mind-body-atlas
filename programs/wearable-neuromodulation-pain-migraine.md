## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)

All four claim to act centrally by stimulating peripheral nerves, but the routes differ.

- **Nerivio (Remote Electrical Neuromodulation, REN).** A perceptible but non-painful stimulus on the upper arm (lateral/medial cutaneous nerves) is said to trigger **conditioned pain modulation (CPM)**. CPM is "pain inhibits pain": a brainstem-mediated descending inhibitory system (PAG / rostral ventromedial medulla, "diffuse noxious inhibitory controls") that reduces pain in remote body regions, including trigeminovascular head pain. The company contrasts this with TENS, which acts locally. Yarnitsky's CPM research programme, and his finding that migraineurs often have impaired CPM, is the theoretical basis. The odd part is that a classically noxious conditioning stimulus is replaced with one the patient perceives as non-painful. A company-authored review (Birlea et al., *Curr Pain Headache Rep* 2025) says the parameters are tuned to activate cutaneous A-delta and C nociceptive fibres, so that CPM releases noradrenaline and serotonin. The same review frames deficient CPM as common to "certain idiopathic pain disorders," a partly transdiagnostic claim.
- **Cefaly (external trigeminal nerve stimulation, e-TNS / tSNS).** Stimulation of the supratrochlear and supraorbital branches of V1 is said to "calm the trigeminal nerve" and reduce sensitivity of the trigeminal system. The proposed central effect is neuromodulation of the trigeminal nucleus caudalis and higher pain areas. Schoenen's group reported normalisation of orbitofrontal/anterior cingulate hypometabolism with prolonged use; that study was not re-verified here.
- **Relivion (external concurrent occipital and trigeminal neurostimulation, ExCOTS).** It stimulates six branches (bilateral supraorbital, supratrochlear and greater occipital) at once, aiming at the trigemino-cervical complex. The site says this releases brainstem neurotransmitters and "normalises the brain's electrical activity."
- **Quell (high-frequency TENS, worn on the calf).** It is framed as a **gate-control** plus **descending inhibition / central sensitization** mechanism. Activating large-diameter afferents is said to release endogenous opioids and engage descending pain inhibition that reduces widespread pain. For fibromyalgia, the company and trialists argue that people with higher pain sensitivity on quantitative sensory testing, i.e. more central sensitization, respond best.

The model of *disease* implied is a sensitized or under-inhibited central pain-processing system (trigeminovascular sensitization in migraine; central sensitization and deficient endogenous inhibition in fibromyalgia). These are mainstream neurology/pain-science constructs. There is no psychological, emotional or "limbic retraining" causal story.

## 2. One mechanism or many?

**Mostly unified, with condition-specific emphasis.** Each device is cleared for specific indications, and the mechanism is pitched at a specific node: trigeminal (Cefaly), trigemino-cervical (Relivion), remote CPM (Nerivio), widespread descending inhibition (Quell). The shared idea is to modulate central pain-modulatory circuits through a peripheral nerve input. None of the companies claims a transdiagnostic "one illness" model like ANS Rewire or DNRS. Each is regulated condition by condition. Nerivio's CPM framing is in principle generic to any pain, and the company has studied menstrual migraine and adolescent migraine, but it is cleared only for migraine. Quell is the only one with a central-sensitization framing aimed at a "contested" overlap condition (fibromyalgia).

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies

- **Nerivio:** 45-min arm stimulation at the start of a migraine (ideally within 1 h, earlier is better). Intensity is set to "strong but not painful." For prevention, use every other day. If repeating a treatment, wait 30 min and switch arms; more than 6 treatments a day adds no benefit. Each unit gives 18 treatments. The app includes an optional **GIER (Guided Intervention of Education and Relaxation)** module, with guided imagery, relaxation and education, to use during stimulation. This is an explicit mind-body add-on, studied by Buse et al. 2022.
- **Cefaly:** forehead electrode. "Acute" program 60 min during an attack; "prevent" program 20 min daily. Cefaly Dual/Connected switches between them with app tracking. The electrodes are reusable.
- **Relivion:** headset, 20–60 min per attack, with no stated limit during an attack. A 60-day trial program precedes purchase.
- **Quell / Quell-FM:** worn on the upper calf for hours a day, including overnight, with automated high-frequency TENS sessions (typically 60 min on, 60 min off). The app does sleep and activity tracking and adjusts intensity, and weather-adaptive settings were offered. Fibromyalgia use in the trial lasted 3 months.

None requires behavioural work, though Nerivio's GIER and sleep/activity tracking nod to self-management.

## 4. Roots and influences

- **Gate-control theory** (Melzack & Wall 1965) and **TENS** (1970s), the basis of Quell and Cefaly.
- **Diffuse noxious inhibitory controls / CPM** (Le Bars 1979; Yarnitsky's CPM work in the 2000s–2010s), the basis of Nerivio.
- **Invasive occipital / supraorbital nerve stimulation** for headache (2000s), the basis of Cefaly and Relivion.
- Electro-analgesia history: from the 18th–19th-century "electrotherapy" vogue to modern bioelectronic medicine ("electroceuticals," GSK 2013).
- **Biofeedback-era wearable self-monitoring** and app-based digital health, seen in app control, diaries and GIER relaxation.
- GIER draws on **relaxation and guided imagery** (Relaxation Response tradition) and migraine behavioural therapy.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance

- **Regulatory:** all four are FDA-authorised. Cefaly was the first device ever authorised for migraine (De Novo 2014), followed by 6 Cefaly 510(k)s through 2024. Nerivio has had seven FDA actions (De Novo DEN180059 plus 510(k)s K201824, K203181, K223169, K232152, K241756 and K250405) from 2019 to 2025, extending to adolescents (2021), prevention (2023), the rechargeable Infinity (2024) and ages 8 and up. Quell-FM (2022) created a new device type for fibromyalgia symptom relief.
- **Guidelines:** the AHS 2021 consensus statement (Ailani et al.) lists neuromodulation devices among acute and preventive options for migraine. Coverage of non-drug options is uneven.
- **Health systems:** Nerivio is available to US veterans, with a published sponsor-co-authored real-world cohort (Brooks-Horrar et al., *Mil Med* 2026, 2020–2025). DoD-wide use was not verified. Relivion's site says the VA "covers access to Relivion for Veterans." US headache specialists commonly offer these devices to patients with medication overuse, contraindications to triptans, pregnancy concerns or a preference for non-drug care.
- **Insurance:** Nerivio has built the most payer coverage. Its FAQ says it is "eligible for insurance coverage"; claims of Medicaid coverage in some states were not verified on the current site. with a sponsor-funded cost-savings analysis (Cowan et al. 2023, *J Med Econ*). Cefaly and Quell have largely been cash/OTC. Relivion says commercial coverage is not yet available.

## 6. Evidence base

Brand-specific, peer-reviewed, sham-controlled trials (verified via Europe PMC/PubMed):

| Device | Citation | Design / N | Key result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cefaly | Schoenen J et al. *Neurology* 2013 (PREMICE) | Sham RCT, prevention, 3 mo, N=67 | Monthly migraine days fell 6.94→4.88 with active vs 6.54→6.22 with sham. 50% responders 38.1% vs 12.1% (p=.023). |
| Cefaly | Chou DE et al. *Cephalalgia* 2019 (ACME) | Sham RCT, acute 1-h, N=106 | VAS pain −3.46 vs −1.78 (−59% vs −30%), p<.0001. |
| Cefaly | Kuruvilla DE et al. *Sci Rep* 2022;12:5110 (TEAM, phase 3) | Sham RCT, acute 2-h, N=538 | 2-h pain freedom 25.5% vs 18.3% (p=.043). Most bothersome symptom (MBS) resolved 56.4% vs 42.3%. Modest effect sizes. |
| Nerivio | Yarnitsky D et al. *Neurology* 2017 | Randomized double-blind sham-controlled **crossover** pilot, N=71 evaluable (299 treatments) | 50% pain reduction in 64% vs 26% with sham. Earlier use worked better. |
| Nerivio | Yarnitsky D et al. *Headache* 2019 | Pivotal sham RCT, acute, N=252 randomized (202 mITT: 99 vs 103) | 2-h pain relief 66.7% vs 38.8%. Pain-free 37.4% vs 18.4%. MBS relief 46.3% vs 22.2%. |
| Nerivio | Tepper SJ et al. *Headache* 2023 | Sham RCT, prevention every other day, 8 wk, N=248 (mITT 179) | Migraine days −4.0 vs −1.3 (gain 2.7 days/mo). Significant in both episodic and chronic migraine. About 28% excluded from mITT. |
| Relivion | Tepper SJ et al. *Headache* 2022 | Sham RCT, acute, N=131 randomized (109 analysed: 50 vs 59) | 2-h pain relief 60% vs 37% (p=.018). Pain-free 46% vs 12%. |
| Relivion | Daniel O et al. *Pain Ther* 2022 | Separate, smaller sham RCT, N=55, episodic and chronic migraine | 2-h pain-free 41.7% vs 20%. ≥50% pain reduction at 2 h 52% vs 17%. Small sample. Sponsor-authored (Sharon, Deutsch). |
| Quell | Jamison RN et al. *J Pain Res* 2021; also *Neuromodulation* 2022 (same trial, different analyses) | Sham RCT, fibromyalgia, 3 mo, N=119 (62 active / 57 sham) | **Primary endpoint (PGIC) not different in ITT** (p=.351). Secondary FIQR −7.5 points favoured active. Pre-specified high-pain-sensitivity subgroup (n=60) benefited. Blinding problems: 87.5% of sham participants guessed their arm. This trial supported the De Novo. |

Supporting / non-randomised:
- Alnajjar et al., *BMC Neurol* 2025: meta-analysis of 12 REN studies (N=9,912), mostly single-arm or real-world. Pooled single-arm 2-h pain relief was 64% and pain-free 22%. The double-arm 2-h pain-free odds ratio was 2.77 (95% CI 1.63–4.71). The authors are independent of Theranica, but the data come mostly from sponsor studies.
- Canales-Rodríguez et al., *Eur J Neurol* 2026: systematic review of 70 non-invasive neuromodulation trials (nVNS, e-TNS, sTMS/rTMS, REN). Acute migraine trials were mostly sham-controlled and blinded, but endpoints often departed from IHS guidelines and preventive trials were heterogeneous. The authors call for more robust sham-controlled studies. Independent and broader than this entry.
- Hershey 2021 (adolescents 12–17, open-label; 60 enrolled, 39 completed a test treatment; 2-h pain relief 71%, pain-free 35%).
- Buse et al. 2022 (*Pain Med*): REN plus GIER relaxation outperformed REN alone in a non-randomised real-world comparison of 85 vs 85 users matched by age and sex.
- Orr et al. 2025 (*Headache*): paediatric emergency-department double-dummy pilot RCT of REN vs typical care, N=19 treated. It tested feasibility only, not efficacy.
- Magis et al. 2013: Cefaly safety survey, N=2,313.
- Kong & Gozani 2018 (*J Pain Res*): observational real-world data from Quell users, company-authored.

**Gaps and critiques:**
- Nearly every trial is sponsor-funded with company employees as co-authors (e.g., Ironi, Harris, Rabany at Theranica; Sharon at Neurolief; Gozani at NeuroMetrix). Independent replication is scarce.
- Sham blinding is hard because the stimulation is perceptible, and sham response rates are high (37–55% 2-h relief).
- Effect sizes for pain freedom are modest: TEAM gives a 7% absolute gain.
- No head-to-head trials against triptans or gepants.
- Fibromyalgia evidence rests on one trial with a negative primary outcome.
- No evidence for other overlap conditions (ME/CFS, IBS, etc.).
- Mechanistic claims (CPM engagement for REN) are inferred from the design rather than demonstrated in patients with CPM paradigms.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory

- **2009–2014:** Cefaly sold in Europe (CE mark, c. 2009, unverified). The 2013 PREMICE trial and the 2014 FDA De Novo brought wide media coverage as "the first device to prevent migraine."
- **2015–2018:** Quell launched with heavy DTC advertising and a Kickstarter-era wearable buzz, about 2015–2016. Cefaly Acute/Dual clearances came in 2017.
- **2019–2023:** Nerivio's De Novo (2019), adolescent label (2021) and prevention label (2023) made it the dominant brand. There are many REN publications, VA adoption, and a growing r/migraine presence. Relivion was cleared in 2021. Quell-FM was authorised in 2022, but NeuroMetrix revenue stayed small. The OTC Quell appears to have been wound down in favour of the Rx fibromyalgia device.
- **2024–2026:** Nerivio Infinity, the paediatric (8+) label and international real-world studies (India, veterans, children). NeuroMetrix was absorbed by electroCore (agreement Dec 2024, closing about May 2025). The Cefaly Connected 510(k) (K234029) came in Jul 2024. Neurolief now leads with Proliv Rx for depression.

Quantitative indicators are limited: meta-analytic N (~10k REN users with data), the 2,313-person Cefaly survey, and multiple large real-world REN datasets. Company claims of total users were not verified. Cumulative reach across the four brands is very uncertain. It is put at roughly 10^5–10^6 users (log10 ≈ 5.5), anchored on about 10^4 users in published REN datasets and a 2,313-person Cefaly survey; sales figures were not verified. Search budget was again exhausted during fact-checking, so Reddit and Google Trends checks were not possible.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms

- **Commercial science.** Most trials were designed, funded and partly authored by manufacturers. High sham response rates and unblinding raise questions about specific vs placebo or expectancy effects. The Quell fibromyalgia trial showed a clear unblinding problem.
- **Regulatory bar.** De Novo/510(k) authorisation is a lower bar than drug approval. Quell-FM was authorised despite a negative primary endpoint, on the basis of secondary and subgroup results.
- **Cost and access.** Prescription and specialty-pharmacy channels (Nerivio, Relivion) and disposable units create recurring costs. Coverage is patchy.
- **Hype vs mechanism.** Marketing language ("shuts off migraine," "normalises the brain's electrical activity") outruns the mechanistic evidence. These devices are nonetheless more mainstream and evidence-anchored than most programs in this survey.

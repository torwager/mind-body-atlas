## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
- **Stress as a common pathway.** Benson took Walter Cannon's "fight-or-flight" response and noted it was described in the same Harvard room. He argued that repeated or chronic activation of sympathetic and stress-hormone systems (epinephrine, norepinephrine, cortisol) causes or worsens hypertension, headache, insomnia, pain, GI disorders, anxiety, depression and many other complaints. BHI repeats an unsourced claim that 60–90% of doctor visits are for stress-related conditions.
- **The relaxation response (RR)** is described as an innate, opposite physiological state:
  - lower oxygen consumption and metabolic rate, heart rate, respiratory rate and blood pressure
  - more parasympathetic activity
  - reduced end-organ responsiveness to norepinephrine
  - in later framing, altered brain activity (fMRI: Lazar, Bush, Gollub, Fricchione, Khalsa & Benson, *NeuroReport* 2000;11(7):1581–1585)
- **Molecular extension (2008–2015).** Gene-expression studies in peripheral blood (Dusek 2008; Bhasin 2013) claim that RR practice upregulates energy-metabolism, mitochondrial ATP-synthase and insulin pathways and downregulates NF-κB inflammatory and stress pathways. This "genomic counter-stress" is presented as a mechanism shared across diseases.
- **Belief / "remembered wellness".** In *Timeless Healing* (1996) Benson reframed placebo effects as a self-care resource: expectation and belief amplify the RR. Faith-based focus words were encouraged.
- **3RP resiliency model** (Park et al. 2013). Resilience depends on lowering physiological arousal (RR), building stress awareness and adaptive appraisal (cognitive restructuring), adding positive psychology and social connection, and keeping healthy lifestyle behaviours. Together these buffer allostatic load.

## 2. One mechanism or many?
**Mostly unified, with condition-specific emphasis.** One stress/RR mechanism is applied to all stress-sensitive conditions; nothing in the program posits different pathophysiology for different illnesses.

- Condition-specific groups add disease education and tailored content, for example a physician-led GI session in the IBS/IBD program and separate programs for fertility, menopause, cancer and cardiac rehab.
- Unlike ANS Rewire, DNRS or the Sarno method, it does **not** claim that ME/CFS, fibromyalgia, IBS and the rest are "one illness". It claims only that stress modulates many diseases, including organic ones (IBD, cancer, cardiac).
- Stress is presented as an aggravator, not the sole cause. It works alongside conventional care.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
**Core RR technique** (1975 book; later updated to a 9-step version):
- Two essential elements: (1) a repetitive mental focus (a word, sound, phrase, prayer, the breath or a repetitive movement) and (2) passively disregarding intruding thoughts, returning to the focus.
- Originally also a quiet environment and comfortable position.
- Practise 10–20 min once or twice daily.

**Multiple ways to elicit the RR** (taught in 3RP/SMART):
- diaphragmatic breathing and "mini-relaxations" (brief in-the-moment breathing)
- body scan and progressive muscle relaxation
- mindful awareness and guided imagery
- self-hypnosis/autogenic-style suggestion
- repetitive prayer
- yoga, tai chi and qigong

**SMART-3RP structure.** BHI clinical format: 3 individual appointments plus 8 weekly 2-h groups. The research versions ran 8–9 weekly 1.5-h sessions with 15–20 min of daily home practice. The four pillars:
1. **RR elicitation:** daily practice with audio recordings.
2. **Stress awareness:** monitoring stress warning signs, triggers and physiological reactivity.
3. **Adaptive strategies:** CBT-style cognitive restructuring and coping, acceptance, and positive psychology (gratitude, optimism, humour, empathy, meaning).
4. **Healthy lifestyle:** sleep, nutrition, physical activity and social support.

**IBS/IBD version** (Kuo 2015): 9 weekly 1.5-h groups led by a nurse practitioner or psychologist, plus one GI-physician session. Sessions 1–4 covered stress physiology and the RR; sessions 5–9 covered lifestyle and cognitive coping.

**Other offerings:** yoga and tai chi classes, Resilient Youth/Schools curricula, the Successful Aging program, and the Cardiac ICR program (mind-body plus exercise and nutrition, run with the MGH Cardiovascular Disease Prevention Center).

## 4. Roots and influences
- **Transcendental Meditation (Hindu mantra meditation).** TM practitioners asked Harvard to study their blood pressure. Benson's lab, with R. Keith Wallace (UCLA TM research), documented a "wakeful hypometabolic state" (1970–72). Benson then "secularised" it: any repetitive focus works, with no guru or fee. He described it as a scientific validation of age-old wisdom and catalogued parallels in Christian, Jewish, Buddhist, Daoist and Sufi contemplative practices.
- Walter Cannon's fight-or-flight physiology; Hans Selye's stress theory.
- Psychophysiology, biofeedback and autogenic training (Schultz), and Jacobson's progressive relaxation. These are its older relatives; the RR was framed as their common physiological denominator.
- Tibetan g-Tum-mo yoga: Benson studied it in monks (*Nature* 1982;295:234–236).
- In the 3RP: CBT (Beck), positive psychology (Seligman), mindfulness (Kabat-Zinn), and yoga and tai chi.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- **Institutional home.** BHI is part of Massachusetts General Hospital and Harvard Medical School. Benson was Mind Body Medicine Professor of Medicine at HMS.
- **Training reach.** BHI trains clinicians, medical students and fellows. It runs the "Herbert Benson, MD Course in Mind Body Medicine" (Harvard CME, 22–24 Oct 2026) and a SMART certification program; about 155 certified practitioners are listed, about 130 in the US (47 in MA) and the rest in 13 countries including Canada, Brazil, Germany, Iceland and Korea.
- **Ubiquity of the technique.** The RR is taught in nursing, primary-care and behavioural-medicine settings. Benson's name and technique appear in Harvard Health Publishing material. Mind-body/RR content has been used in programs for hospital employees and healthcare workers.
- **Insurance:**
  - Individual intakes are billed to insurance; SMART groups are self-pay.
  - The cardiac program is delivered as intensive cardiac rehab, a Medicare benefit category. CMS added the Benson-Henry Institute Cardiac Wellness Program to the ICR benefit effective 6 May 2014 (one of three approved ICR programs, alongside Ornish and Pritikin); verified on cms.gov.
  - No guideline body (NICE, ACP) endorses the branded SMART-3RP specifically. Relaxation training in general appears in pain, IBS and anxiety guidelines as a psychological option.

## 6. Evidence base
**Branded 3RP / SMART-3RP and BHI RR programs (mostly in-house investigators):**
- **Kuo B, Bhasin M, et al. 2015**, *PLoS One* 10(4):e0123861 (PMID 25927528; NCT02136745, registered retroactively):
  - Design: single-arm, open-label pilot of a 9-week RR mind-body group; n=19 IBS and n=29 IBD.
  - IBS results: IBS-SSI fell from 215 to 128 at week 10 (p<0.001) and was 147 at week 13. IBS-QOL improved.
  - Both groups: trait anxiety and pain catastrophizing fell. Brief Pain Inventory, CRP and ESR did not change.
  - Blood transcriptome changes involved NF-κB networks.
  - Limitations: no control group; 9 of 48 (4 IBS, 5 IBD) withdrew after 1–3 sessions; a correction was published in 2017 (PLoS One 12(2):e0172872).
- **Stahl JE, Dossett ML, et al. (incl. Benson) 2015**, *PLoS One* 10(10):e0140212:
  - Design: retrospective propensity-matched cohort; 4,452 3RP participants vs 13,149 controls.
  - Results: billable encounters fell 43% in the year after the program. Relative reductions vs high-utilising matched controls were about 18–25%.
  - Limitations: observational design, regression to the mean, and author conflict (BHI staff).
- **Park ER, Traeger L, Vranceanu AM, et al. 2013**, *Psychosomatics* 54(2):165–174: describes the development of the 3RP. It is a program description, not a trial.
- **Neurofibromatosis.** A 3RP-NF pilot (Vranceanu et al., *J Neurooncol* 2014) and an RCT: **Vranceanu AM, Riklin E, Merker VL, Macklin EA, Park ER, Plotkin SR. Mind-body therapy via videoconferencing in patients with neurofibromatosis: an RCT. *Neurology* 2016;87(8):806–814** (n=63; 3RP-NF vs attention control). The 3RP group improved more on physical-health, social and environmental QoL and anxiety, sustained at 6 months; depression improved equally in both arms and pain did not improve more than control. Non-target population, but it shows the branded program has RCT-level testing (by the developers).
- **Depression.** Several small community/Chinese-American pilots of RR-based groups (2014–2015).
- **Chronic pain, earlier BHI work.** Caudill et al. 1991, *Clin J Pain* 7(4):305–310 (n=109): clinic visits fell 36% in the year after an RR-based outpatient behavioural-medicine program, with reductions persisting at 2 years in the first 50 followed (uncontrolled; estimated savings).
- **Somatic symptoms.** Mind/body clinic papers from 2001 (*Behav Med*; *Psychother Psychosom*) reported symptom reduction in somatising patients (uncontrolled).

**Generic RR / relaxation-meditation trials:**
- **Keefer L & Blanchard EB 2001**, *Behav Res Ther* 39(7):801–811, with a one-year follow-up (*Behav Res Ther* 2002;40(5):541–546, PMID 12038646):
  - Design: 16 adults with IBS, matched in pairs and randomized to 6 weeks of Benson's RR meditation (15 min twice daily) vs symptom-monitoring waitlist; 13 completed. Independent (Albany) group.
  - Results: meditation beat the control on composite symptom score (p=0.04). Flatulence and belching improved post-treatment; flatulence, belching, bloating and diarrhoea improved at 3-month follow-up.
  - One-year follow-up: 10 of the 13 completers were re-assessed. Pain, diarrhoea, flatulence and bloating were lower than pre-treatment, with further reductions in pain and bloating after 3 months (uncontrolled at that point).
  - Very small sample.
- **Headache:** Benson et al. 1974, *Headache* 14(1), uncontrolled.
- **Mechanistic studies:** Wallace, Benson & Wilson 1971 (*Am J Physiol*); Hoffman et al. 1982 (*Science* 215:190–192, PMID 7031901), reduced sympathetic (NE) responsivity; Dusek 2008 and Bhasin 2013 (*PLoS One*). Bhasin 2013 tested 26 healthy novices before and after 8 weeks of RR training (vs their own health-education baseline) and 26 long-term practitioners cross-sectionally; the authors call the findings preliminary.
- The wider literature on relaxation training and meditation in IBS, pain, anxiety and hypertension (many RCTs and meta-analyses) supports modest effects of the generic technique. It is not specific to the BHI brand.

**Gaps:**
- No adequately powered, independent RCT of SMART-3RP for IBS, fibromyalgia, chronic pain, ME/CFS or long COVID was found.
- Most branded evidence is in-house and uncontrolled.
- The genomic studies are small, use peripheral blood, and are hypothesis-generating.
- The STEP prayer trial (Benson et al. 2006, *Am Heart J*) was a null and partly adverse trial from the same group, on intercessory prayer.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory
- **1975:** *The Relaxation Response* (William Morrow) became a bestseller. Benson's books total more than 5 million copies in print in many languages (Wikipedia).
- **1986:** a US national survey reported by the NYT found it was the self-help book clinical psychologists most often recommended to patients.
- **1979–2010:** a steady stream of books, including *Beyond the RR* (1984), *The Wellness Book* (1992), *Timeless Healing* (1996), a 25th-anniversary edition (2000), *The Breakout Principle* (2003) and *Relaxation Revolution* (2010).
- **1988:** Mind/Body Medical Institute founded.
- **2006:** the institute moved to MGH and became BHI.
- **2008–2015:** genomics papers brought a media revival ("meditation changes gene expression").
- **2015:** utilisation study (43% fewer visits) widely reported.
- **2022:** Benson died (obituaries in major papers).
- **Current scale:**
  - BHI cites "thousands of patients" a year and "hundreds" of clinicians at the annual CME course.
  - About 155 certified practitioners.
  - Research output: PubMed records with "relaxation response" in title/abstract rose from 4 (1975) to a plateau of 45–57 a year in 2000–2015, then fell to 44 (2020) and 37 (2025).
  - Wikipedia (English) pageviews fell by about a third over ten years: *The Relaxation Response* from about 16,800 (2016) to 9,000–11,300 a year (2023–2025); *Herbert Benson* from about 16,000–18,000 (2016–2019) to about 10,000 (2025).
  - Reddit: no dedicated subreddit was found, but Reddit could not be checked directly (reddit.com blocks automated fetches, the Wayback Machine has no capture of r/RelaxationResponse, and web search turned up no Reddit hits). Google Trends was also not retrievable (rate-limited). The term appears in r/Anxiety and r/Meditation discussions and in nursing and psychology coursework.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **TM appropriation dispute.** The TM movement objected that Benson repackaged TM without its specific mantra. Benson's position was that any repetitive focus produces the same response, and later comparative literature broadly supports nonspecific relaxation effects.
- **Prayer research.** Benson led the Templeton-funded STEP trial of intercessory prayer in cardiac-bypass patients (Benson et al., *Am Heart J* 2006): null overall, with more complications in patients told they were being prayed for. The trial and the funding of prayer research drew public criticism (the specific commentary previously cited here could not be verified and has been removed).
- **Claims run ahead of the data:**
  - the "60–90% of doctor visits are stress-related" figure is widely repeated but weakly sourced
  - genomic "switching off" of inflammation genes is claimed from small, uncontrolled samples
  - the cost-savings claims rest on retrospective billing data
- **Conflict of interest.** Nearly all branded-program evaluations come from BHI-affiliated investigators.
- **Comparison with other programs in this review.** It is less contentious than ME/CFS "brain retraining" programs: it does not claim to cure organic or contested illnesses, and it is positioned as an adjunct.

## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
- **Original model, "hypothalamitis"** (background, unverified for exact wording; the term no longer appears on the site). ME/CFS and related syndromes were attributed to an over-active, "inflamed"/hyperactivated hypothalamus. Because the hypothalamus coordinates the nervous, endocrine and immune systems, its persistent activation was said to produce symptoms across all three. The proposed driver was psychological rather than infective: a habit of overriding the body's emotional signals.
- **Current model, the Prolonged Activation State (PAS):**
  - *Normal cycle.* Under normal conditions, the body senses a situation, an activation follows, the person responds, and the activation settles.
  - *How PAS arises.* When these signals (framed as *feelings* arising from how one interacts with people and situations) are repeatedly overridden, postponed or unanswered, every new situation adds fresh activation. The system then stays "on," like a car revving in park.
  - *Symptoms by system.* The hypothalamus is still described as the coordinating hub, with symptoms listed for each system:
    - nervous: fatigue, brain fog, dizziness, headache, light and noise sensitivity, poor sleep, anxiety
    - endocrine: low stress resilience, temperature and appetite dysregulation, energy crashes
    - immune: post-viral sensitivity, inflammation, allergy/MCAS-type reactions, recurrent infections
  - *Maintained in the present.* PAS is said to be maintained by how one responds to *present* life, not by past trauma ("the body isn't stuck in the past").
  - *Triggers.* An infection or other event (relevant to long COVID and post-viral ME/CFS) may start the activation, but ongoing unanswered signals keep it going.
- **Symptoms as messages.** Bodily symptoms and feelings are reframed as communications that call for action. They are not defects to suppress.
- **Positioning.** The site presents PAS as a framework for *why* activation persists, complementary to biomedical research on *what* is happening physiologically.

## 2. One mechanism or many?
**A single unified mechanism.** One PAS/hypothalamic-activation model covers ME/CFS, long COVID, fibromyalgia, chronic pain, anxiety, IBS and allergy/MCAS-type symptoms. The site gives no diagnostic criteria, subtypes or condition-specific mechanisms. Different illnesses are treated as different symptom expressions of the same unanswered-signal loop, with only the trigger (for example a virus) varying. Emphasis shifts by marketing page (long COVID page, fatigue focus) rather than by mechanism.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
- **Core method: noticing and responding to body signals.**
  1. Notice a feeling or symptom.
  2. Treat it as information about a current situation or relationship.
  3. Take a concrete response or action (for example setting a boundary, expressing a need, changing an activity) instead of overriding the feeling with thinking, "shoulds" or effort.
  - "Feeling vs Story" teaching separates the body's felt signal from the mind's narrative about it.
  - "3 Keys to Recovery" is a video framework whose content is not public.
- **Five principles** (site):
  1. Feelings arise from interaction with life.
  2. Feelings are signals to respond to.
  3. PAS develops when signals go unanswered.
  4. Symptoms are messages.
  5. Recovery means responding to signals.
- **Historical format** (background, unverified): a short course of sessions with a trained practitioner, typically a handful of sessions over weeks, plus symptom/feeling diaries and homework on acting on identified emotions and reducing "head"-driven overriding behaviour.
- **Current dose and structure:**
  - Core Course: 5 modules, 17 lessons, self-paced, lifetime access.
  - Guided track: 3 one-to-one coach sessions within 9 months.
  - Premium/personal track: 3 sessions with Mickel plus 6 months of voice-note/written support.
  - Optional monthly workshops (replays included).
- **Not included:** no diet, supplement, pacing protocol, graded exercise or medication components are described. The method is explicitly not about "fixing symptoms through effort or control."

## 4. Roots and influences
- **Stress physiology / HPA-axis models** (Selye; hypothalamic coordination of the stress response).
- *Note:* the program cites none of the influences below; they are the reviewer's inferences.
- **Emotional-awareness and expression traditions.** Symptoms are treated as signals of unexpressed feelings, similar in spirit to the Sarno/TMS idea and to later EAET, though without a psychodynamic "repressed rage" framing or a focus on the past.
- **Life coaching / solution-focused, present-oriented coaching.** Delivery is through "coaches," not therapists.
- **Somatic or body-as-messenger ideas** from humanistic and somatic psychology (Gendlin-style felt sense is a plausible influence; not cited by the program).
- **Lineage.** Mickel Therapy is widely described as the ancestor of **Reverse Therapy** (Dr John Eaton, UK, about 2002; "bodymind" approach, also built on hypothalamitis). Fact-check could not confirm the lineage or the date: reverse-therapy.com names Eaton as developer but does not mention Mickel or the hypothalamus. It sits in the same 2000s UK wave as the Lightning Process (Phil Parker, 1998), and anticipates the limbic/brain-retraining programs (DNRS, Gupta, ANS Rewire) that later framed ME/CFS as a stuck threat or stress state.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- No NHS commissioning, hospital program or university-affiliated delivery was identified.
- Not recommended by NICE (CG53 2007; NG206 2021) or other guidelines. NG206 explicitly warns against the Lightning Process "or therapies based on it." Mickel Therapy is not named, but its comparable unproven brain/stress-model coaching falls under the same sceptical framing.
- The ME Association site has only a passing mention: a 2011 *Observer* letter from a reader stating that Mickel Therapy-type treatments, which locate ME in "primary emotions and their effect on the hypothalamus," had helped some patients, and a critical reader comment beneath it.
- Not insurance reimbursable. Practitioners are coaches certified by the company (two training weekends, £1,495); there is no external accreditation or regulated professional status.

## 6. Evidence base
- **No peer-reviewed trials of Mickel Therapy were found.** Searches run 2026-10-12:
  - PubMed: "Mickel therapy"[tiab] returns 0 hits; Mickel[tiab] AND fatigue returns 0. Re-checked 2026-10-12: '"mickel therapy"[tiab] OR hypothalamitis AND fatigue' returned one unrelated hit, a case report of fatal anti-Ma autoimmune hypothalamitis (Bustos et al., *J Endocr Soc* 2018). The unquoted search "mickel therapy" returns about 120 hits, all of them for authors named Mickel.
  - ClinicalTrials.gov: no relevant registration.
  - ISRCTN: 0 hits.
  - The site cites no studies on any page reviewed.
- **Evidence consists of testimonials**, such as a video recovery story from a long-COVID patient. The site makes no claims about success rates.
- **Related evidence (generic, not the branded program):** emotional awareness/expression approaches (EAET) and stress-reduction interventions have RCT support in fibromyalgia and chronic pain. CBT has mixed support in ME/CFS. None of this tests the PAS/hypothalamitis model or the Mickel protocol.
- **Mechanistic gap:** no published data link "unanswered feelings" to hypothalamic activation in ME/CFS. HPA-axis findings in ME/CFS, mostly mild hypocortisolism, do not obviously fit an "overactive hypothalamus" model.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory
- **About 1999–2004, emergence.** Mickel develops the hypothalamitis model in Scotland and trains practitioners. Reverse Therapy splits off around 2002 (background, unverified).
- **About 2004–2010, peak.** UK press and patient-forum discussion runs alongside the Lightning Process. Practitioner networks form in the UK, Ireland, the Netherlands, Australia and New Zealand (background, unverified).
- **2011–2019, decline.** Patient-community scepticism hardens after PACE, and attention moves to newer brain-retraining brands (DNRS, Gupta, later ANS Rewire).
- **2020–2026, modest revival.** The program rebrands around long COVID and the PAS model, moves online (course, app, workshops, YouTube), and adds "Wired for Wonder."
- **Indicators found:** a "thousands of people" worldwide claim (site, 2026); a 2026 site with online courses, a coach directory and a certification pathway. No user counts, follower counts or Trends data could be retrieved in this session. The official YouTube channel URL returned 404.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **"Hypothalamitis" is not a recognised diagnosis.** In medicine, hypothalamitis refers to rare autoimmune or inflammatory hypothalamic lesions; using the term for ME/CFS lacks supporting evidence. For example, PubMed's hypothalamitis literature consists of case reports of rare lesions such as anti-Ma-associated hypothalamitis. The term has since disappeared from the program's materials.
- **Patient-advocacy concern.** ME/CFS communities have criticised Mickel Therapy, like the Lightning Process and Reverse Therapy, for implying that patients sustain their illness through their emotional habits. This raises the risk of blame and of overexertion, and testimonials stand in for outcome data.
- **Commercial certification.** Practitioners are credentialed only by the company (short training), and the method is marketed for serious post-viral illness with no published outcomes.

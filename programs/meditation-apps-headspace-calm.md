# Mass-market meditation apps (Headspace, Calm, Insight Timer, Healthy Minds Program)

*Compiled 2026-10-12 from headspace.com (About, pain-meditation page), calm.com/about, insighttimer.com, humin.org, Wikipedia company pages, PubMed (E-utilities) and ClinicalTrials.gov. Content paraphrased. Note: the session's web-search budget ran out early, so prices and some corporate events are taken from widely reported figures and are flagged as such. Every trial listed below was checked on PubMed or ClinicalTrials.gov.*

This is an **umbrella entry** for general-purpose consumer meditation apps. None of them is built around a theory of chronic illness. They are included for two reasons: their reach is very large, and they serve as a **contrast case** to the condition-specific "brain-retraining" programs. All four offer pain, sleep and anxiety content inside a general mindfulness or wellbeing framework.

## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
- **No disease model.** The apps do not offer a theory of how chronic pain, fatigue or functional syndromes arise. Their implicit model is the generic mindfulness/stress model:
  - stress and reactivity, mind-wandering and rumination amplify suffering;
  - meditation trains attention, non-judgemental awareness and "decentering";
  - this lowers stress reactivity, arousal and (for sleep) pre-sleep cognitive and somatic arousal.
- **Pain framing (Headspace).** Meditation does not remove pain but changes one's *relationship* to it. Resisting pain makes it worse; meeting sensations with curiosity and "stepping back" reduces secondary suffering. This is the classic MBSR distinction between primary sensation and secondary reaction. In support, Headspace's pain page cites Kabat-Zinn's 1980s MBSR studies, Cherkin 2016 (MBSR for low back pain) and meditator cortical-thickness work, none of which tested the app itself.
- **HMP framing.** Wellbeing is a set of trainable skills across four pillars: **awareness, connection, insight, purpose**. This draws on Davidson's affective-neuroscience research on neuroplasticity, emotion regulation and default-mode/self-referential processing. "Insight" practice targets fused, self-referential thinking (cognitive defusion).
- **Calm** emphasises relaxation, sleep (Sleep Stories), breathwork, and stress/anxiety reduction through parasympathetic down-regulation. These ideas are implicit and are not set out as a pathophysiological theory.

## 2. One mechanism or many?
**Transdiagnostic and not disorder-focused.** The same practices (breath focus, body scan, noting, loving-kindness, visualisation, sleep content) are packaged into themed courses for anxiety, sleep, focus, pain, grief and so on. The courses differ in emphasis only. No mechanism specific to any named illness is proposed, and the apps make no claim that conditions such as fibromyalgia, IBS or ME/CFS share a common central cause. This makes them the conceptual opposite of programs like DNRS or ANS Rewire.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
**Headspace**
- Basics course (3 parts, then longer), plus single sessions of 3–20 min.
- Themed courses, including a **30-day "Pain Management" course** for subscribers that uses noting, curious investigation of sensation and "stepping back" from pain. Also courses on anxiety, sleep, stress and depression.
- Sleepcasts, focus music, movement, short "SOS" sessions.
- Ebb, an AI companion (launched about 2024).
- Headspace Care: text-based coaching, video therapy and psychiatry (from Ginger).
- Netflix series *Headspace Guide to Meditation/Sleep* (2021).

**Calm**
- Daily Calm (about 10 min), 7/21-day programs (e.g., "7 Days of Calm", "How to Meditate").
- Breathing exercises, body scans, 300+ Sleep Stories (since Dec 2016), music and soundscapes, Calm Body (gentle movement).
- Pain-themed sessions within the library.
- Calm Health: condition-oriented programs (initially anxiety/depression), delivered through employers and health plans.

**Insight Timer**
- A timer with bells, plus 250–360k free tracks from about 20k independent teachers.
- Content ranges from vipassana and yoga nidra to EFT tapping, Joe Dispenza-style and "mind-body"/TMS-oriented pain tracks (e.g., uploaded by PRT/Sarno-oriented coaches).
- MemberPlus adds offline listening and multi-day courses.
- Content is not curated to any single model, so quality and claims vary widely.

**Healthy Minds Program**
- Structured "journey" across the four pillars, with podcast-style lessons and formal sitting practices (5–30 min) or "active" practices (guided while walking or doing chores).
- In trials, the typical dose is about 10 min/day for 4–8 weeks.

## 4. Roots and influences
- Buddhist vipassana/insight and Tibetan practice: Puddicombe trained as a monk; Insight Timer's name and many of its teachers come from this tradition.
- Zen.
- MBSR/MBCT (Kabat-Zinn; Segal, Williams, Teasdale).
- Hindu yoga/pranayama/yoga nidra: breathwork and yoga content.
- Relaxation Response/autogenic-style relaxation: Calm's breathing and sleep content.
- Third-wave CBT: HMP's insight/defusion module draws on ACT-type concepts.
- Positive psychology and the wellbeing movement: HMP's pillars, Calm's gratitude content.
- Contemplative neuroscience (Davidson, Lutz): HMP.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- **Employer and health-plan channel.** This is where the money is.
  - Headspace for Work/EAP has thousands of enterprise clients.
  - Calm Business and Calm Health (launched Oct 2022 after the Ripple Health Group acquisition) are sold to health plans and employers.
  - Kaiser Permanente lists Calm and Headspace among member self-care apps; its page states these apps are *not* covered health-plan benefits, and Headspace access includes 90 days/year of text coaching (KP site, fetched Oct 2026; start date not verified).
- **NHS.** During COVID-19 (2020), Headspace was offered free to NHS staff; this cohort became the 2,182-person RCT by Taylor et al. 2022.
- **Clinical integration.** Headspace merged with Ginger (announced Aug 2021, closed Oct 2021; valuation about USD 3B), which added insurance-billable therapy and psychiatry. Headspace created a Headspace Health subsidiary in 2018 to pursue prescription digital therapeutics; no FDA-cleared Headspace product was confirmed this session. Headspace now describes itself as "part of Sword" (Sword Health, a digital musculoskeletal and pelvic-pain company); the timing and terms were not verified.
- **Guidelines.** No pain or fatigue guideline names these apps. Guidelines endorse *mindfulness-based interventions* generically (e.g., MBSR for low back pain), not app delivery.
- **Insurance.** No consumer reimbursement as a medical device or therapy for the subscriptions; coverage comes through employer and health-plan benefits.

## 6. Evidence base
**Branded app trials (stress, distress, mood, sleep; mostly non-clinical samples)**
- **Taylor et al. 2022**, *JMIR mHealth uHealth* (PMID 36006668). Definitive superiority RCT in 2,182 NHS health-care workers: Headspace vs NHS Moodzone (active control).
  - Small reductions in stress (b = −0.31), depression, anxiety and worry.
  - No effect on burnout.
  - The first author's studentship was funded by Headspace.
- **Radin et al. 2025**, *JAMA Netw Open* (PMID 39808431). RCT in 1,458 academic-medical-centre employees: digital meditation (Headspace; senior author has Headspace grants and fees) vs waitlist.
  - Perceived stress d = 0.85 at 8 weeks, 0.71 at 4 months.
  - Headspace, Inc. co-funded the study and supplied subscriptions; senior author Prather reported Headspace grants and fees.
  - Waitlist control and self-report outcomes inflate these effects.
- **Huberty et al. 2019**, *JMIR mHealth uHealth* (PMID 31237569). RCT in 88 stressed college students: Calm (10 min/day for 8 weeks) vs waitlist.
  - Improvements in stress, mindfulness and self-compassion (d about 0.6–1.2).
  - Huberty became Calm's Director of Science about one year after the study was designed and analysed (per the COI statement).
- **Huberty et al. 2021**, *PLoS One* (PMID 33411779). RCT in 263 adults with sleep disturbance: Calm vs waitlist.
  - Reduced fatigue, daytime sleepiness and pre-sleep arousal.
  - The authors include Calm's Director of Science and advisory-board members (Vranceanu, Larkey, Irwin); the COI statement says Calm did not fund the research.
- **Goldberg et al. 2020**, *JMIR Ment Health* (PMID 33245288). Three-arm RCT in 343 participants: HMP (two versions) vs waitlist.
  - Small to moderate reductions in distress (d about 0.28–0.41).
  - 54% retention.
  - Davidson founded and is president of the nonprofit that owns HMP.
- **Hirshberg et al. 2025**, *JAMA Intern Med* (PMID 40824577). RCT in 2,315 Mexican health-care professionals: HMP app for 13 weeks plus nine 2-hour Zoom sessions vs wait-list control.
  - Distress SMD −0.24 at 13 weeks, −0.36 at 37 weeks.
  - This is a blended intervention, not the app alone. Davidson (founder/president of the HMP owner) is a co-author.
- **Others (all on PubMed):**
  - Economides 2018 (PMID 30294390)
  - Bostock 2019 (PMID 29723001; workplace stress)
  - Flett 2020 (PMID 32046499; students, weak effects on distress, mean about 8 sessions)
  - Zawadzki 2025 (PMID 40257119; stress, EMA)
  - Sarlon 2025 (PMID 39230745; 83 inpatients/outpatients with MDD, Headspace adjunct for 30 days; improvement on some depression measures)
  - Abbott 2023 (PMID 36787997; anxiety reduced, worry null by NHST)
  - Torok 2026 (PMID 41637757; Headspace improved subjective and objective sleep)

**Chronic pain and functional conditions (the relevant gap)**
- **MEMPHIS:** Forbes et al. 2020, *BMJ Open* (PMID 32165549). Three-arm feasibility RCT in 90 women with chronic pelvic pain: a modified pre-existing mindfulness app vs a relaxation app vs usual care.
  - Mean mindfulness-app use was **1.8 days**.
  - Confidence intervals were consistent with no benefit.
  - The authors concluded that a definitive trial was not warranted.
  - Note that this was **not** Headspace or Calm.
- **NCT03495726, "Meditation for Pain"** (Emory). Headspace (10 min/day, 6 weeks) vs treatment as usual with delayed Headspace access, chronic pain, N = 115. Completed March 2020; no results posted, and no publication found.
- **NCT03936088.** Headspace vs a water-tracking app (My Water Balance), both with OA education, in knee osteoarthritis, N = 75. Completed Sept 2022; no results posted.
- No branded RCTs were found in fibromyalgia, IBS, ME/CFS, long COVID, migraine, FND or CRPS.

**Syntheses**
- **O'Daffer et al. 2022**, *JMIR Ment Health* (PMID 36125880). Systematic review of Headspace and Calm RCTs (to May 2021): 14 Headspace RCTs and 1 Calm RCT.
  - 93% were in non-clinical populations, and most were underpowered.
  - Headspace improved depression in 75% of the trials that measured it; results for stress and anxiety were mixed.
  - 50% (7/14) of Headspace RCTs had a company-related conflict of interest (most commonly free premium access), and 2 of 14 involved Headspace employees in design and analysis. The single Calm RCT was coded as having no COI.
  - Only 36% (5/14) of Headspace RCTs were preregistered; the Calm RCT was not.
- **Gál, Ștefan & Cristea 2021**, *J Affect Disord* (PMID 33049431). Meta-analysis of 34 RCTs (N = 7,566) of mindfulness apps in general.
  - Stress g = 0.46, anxiety g = 0.28, depression g = 0.33, psychological wellbeing g = 0.29; no significant effect on distress (g = 0.10).
  - High risk of bias, and mostly waitlist controls.

**Related generic evidence.** Trials of in-person MBSR for chronic low back pain (Cherkin 2016) and laboratory work on pain modulation (e.g., Wielgosz et al. 2022, *Am J Psychiatry*, PMID 35899379: MBSR reduced NPS response to thermal pain) support *mindfulness* for pain. They do **not** test these apps, and they should not be counted as evidence for the branded products.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory
- **2009–2011:** Insight Timer begins as a meditation timer app. Headspace runs events and CDs in London from 2010. Tew's "Do Nothing for 2 Minutes" goes viral in 2011.
- **2012:** The Headspace app and Calm launch.
- **2014–2017:** Rapid growth driven by app-store featuring and celebrity and media attention. Calm launches Sleep Stories (Dec 2016), is Apple App of the Year (2017) and reports USD 22M revenue (2017).
- **2019:** Calm valued at USD 1B (Feb 2019; USD 116M raised in total). The HMP app launches. App research expands.
- **2020:** COVID-19 surge. Free offers go to health workers, the unemployed and public-sector bodies, and downloads peak. This is the peak year for Google-Trends-type interest in "meditation app" (description only; not quantified this session).
- **2021:** Headspace's Netflix series. The Headspace–Ginger merger (about USD 3B; closed Oct 2021). The founders step back from day-to-day leadership (Jan 2021) and leave the board in May 2022.
- **2022–2024:**
  - Calm has about 4M paid subscribers (Nov 2022) and launches Calm Health.
  - Layoffs at both companies (Calm about 20% in 2022; Headspace in 2023).
  - The Headspace Health name is dropped (Oct 2023), and Tom Pickett becomes CEO (Aug 2024).
- **2025–2026:** Cumulative downloads reach Headspace 105M+ and Calm 180M+. Insight Timer reports about 30M users. Headspace is absorbed into Sword. The consumer market is mature and flat, and the strategy is shifting to B2B and clinical channels.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **Conflicts of interest.**
  - Company-funded or company-affiliated investigators are common (O'Daffer 2022).
  - Calm's Director of Science (Huberty) led most Calm RCTs, although O'Daffer et al. coded the one Calm RCT in their review as COI-free because her role began after that study.
  - HMP trials are led by the program's founder's center (a nonprofit, but still a COI).
- **Weak designs.** Waitlist controls, self-report outcomes, high attrition and low engagement (e.g., about 8 sessions over 3 months in Flett 2020; 1.8 days in MEMPHIS). Real-world retention for meditation apps is low.
- **Pain claims.** Pain marketing leans on MBSR and neuroimaging studies of in-person or long-term practice rather than on app trials. Pain-specific app trials are either null, unpublished or unreported.
- **Corporate.**
  - Headspace therapists' complaints about abrupt cancellations during the 2023 layoffs.
  - Calm's AI-cloned voice of James Stewart (d. 1997) for a Christmas sleep story (Dec 2023).
  - Data-privacy concerns common to mental-health apps.
- **Uncurated content (Insight Timer).** It hosts a range of unvetted health claims, including limbic-retraining, "neuroplastic pain" and energy-healing tracks.
- **Adverse effects.** Generally rare. Beloborodova 2026 (PMID 42284591) characterises self-reported adverse experiences in digital meditation (HMP trials).

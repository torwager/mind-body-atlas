## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
- **Nervous-system / brain-generated pain.** Lin's model is "brain-first." Most chronic pain is framed as *chronic primary pain* (the ICD-11 category MG30.0): pain out of proportion to tissue findings, generated or amplified by an over-sensitised pain-signalling system. The site's analogy is an alarm that sounds for a candle, or for no fire at all, and it says that "all pain is ultimately mediated by the brain." The ICD-11 framing is reinforced by advisor Shaheen Lakhan (MD, PhD), described as part of the WHO chronic-pain classification effort.
- **Danger appraisal and fear.** Following PRT, pain persists because the brain misreads safe signals as dangerous. Fear of movement and pain-related worry keep the loop going. The 2023 white paper names TSK-11 kinesiophobia reduction as a "critical mediator," citing the PRT RCT.
- **Stress and emotion.** In-house data are used to argue that stressful life events co-occur with pain onset: 69% recall a stressor at onset and 22% report family conflict. Suppressed or unprocessed emotion is treated as an amplifier, which is the EAET influence.
- **Cross-system disorder.** The white paper reports that 85% of patients have pain in more than one site. It treats this as evidence that chronic pain is a "multi-system, multi-symptom, bio-psycho-social" disorder, not a set of separate local problems.
- **Clinician triage.** Medical staff sort each patient into one of three groups. Reported shares: 70% exclusively primary pain, 26% mixed primary plus structural, and 4% pain proportionate to a structural cause.

## 2. One mechanism or many?
**Mostly unified, with a medical screen.** The same nervous-system-sensitisation and danger-appraisal model is applied to every listed condition, from back pain and migraine to IBS, POTS, CFS, chronic hives, tinnitus and "pseudoseizures."
- **Where it differentiates:** Lin separates *primary* from *secondary/structural* pain at the physician review stage. The site positions the program as complementary to medical care, not a replacement; it does not prescribe medications.
- **Where it does not:** There are no condition-specific mechanistic accounts (for example, no autonomic model for POTS and no immune model for hives). Condition-specific landing pages (e.g., a Mayo fibromyalgia page) apply the same framework with different marketing.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
**Pathway:**
1. Online intake questionnaire and pain-history review.
2. Medical review by a pain-specialised physician (anesthesiology, family medicine, psychiatry and neurology are represented in the medical group).
3. Matching with a **Pain Recovery Coach**. Coaches are often health/wellness coaches (NBC-HWC), not licensed therapists, and work under clinical supervision.
4. A personalised recovery plan.
5. **Weekly live coaching sessions** (Intensive tier: 4/mo; Core: 2/mo) plus asynchronous in-app messaging.

**Engagement and duration:** The company reports patients engaging 2–3 times per week, a typical stay of about 6 months, and 80% returning for a second month. A "3-month boot camp" carries a pain-reduction guarantee. Plans run month-to-month up to 1 year.

**Techniques named:**
- **Pain Reprocessing Therapy (PRT)**
  - pain neuroscience education
  - "evidence lists" gathering reasons to see the pain as non-structural
  - somatic tracking: observing sensations mindfully with safety reappraisal
  - graded re-engagement in feared movement and activity
- **Emotional Awareness and Expression Therapy (EAET):** linking stress and emotions to pain, and emotional expression
- **CBT and ACT** elements
- **Mind-body practices:** breathwork, meditation and gentle yoga, mentioned in patient stories
- **Fear-of-movement reduction**, tracked with TSK-11
- **Supporting input:** physical therapy (Charlie Merrill, MSPT, is a clinical advisor) and psychiatry when indicated

**App:** short lessons, "care kits," symptom and outcome tracking. AI is used for back-end operations and care coordination; care itself is delivered by people.

**Free material (rethinkpain.lin.health):**
- a three-part mini-course of 6–7 min lessons ("Why nothing has worked yet…," "The new science…," "4 tips for breaking the chronic pain cycle")
- a PRT "crash course" webinar taught by Yoni Ashar
- practice exercises, webinars and recovery stories

## 4. Roots and influences
- **PRT (Alan Gordon / Ashar et al.)** and the broader **Sarno → Schubiner "mind-body syndrome"/neuroplastic pain** lineage.
- **EAET (Lumley and Schubiner),** which draws on psychodynamic and emotional-processing ideas.
- **CBT for pain, ACT** (third-wave CBT), and the fear-avoidance model.
- **Pain neuroscience education:** the gate control and neuromatrix tradition, Moseley/Butler "Explain Pain," central sensitisation.
- **ICD-11 chronic primary pain:** the IASP/WHO biopsychosocial classification.
- **Mindfulness practices:** somatic tracking, meditation.
- **Health and life coaching** delivery model, with Collaborative Care Model integration.
- **Telehealth and digital-therapeutics** industry practices.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- **Payers:**
  - **Anthem BCBS Maine** statewide network, from Mar 2023.
  - **BCBS Minnesota** behavioral-health provider network (date not confirmed).
  - By Oct 2024 the company reported access via Aetna, Anthem, BCBS TX, Cigna, Harvard Pilgrim, Humana, Medicare and UHC, covering 27M people. By Dec 2025 it claimed 60M covered Americans.
  - The pricing FAQ is older and lists coverage only in TX, CO and ME. Coverage likely varies by state and plan.
- **Health systems:**
  - **Mayo Clinic** is both investor and collaborator (press release 22 Oct 2024). Lin provides post-discharge transition care for Mayo Rochester's Fibromyalgia Treatment Program, with planned expansion to Mayo Arizona primary care. Mayo has a financial interest.
  - Other referral partners: WellSpan, and Lowell Pain Center/Tufts Medicine (a pain-physician case study).
- **Leadership:** In 2025 Lin appointed a neurologist CMO, Eric Anderson (MD, PhD, ex-Synapticure). Its clinical advisors (2022) included Yoni Ashar, Antje Barreveld, Pavel Goldstein and Shaheen Lakhan, and Sherry Dubester (ex-Beacon, Anthem) joined the board.
- **Guidelines and agencies:** No CMS national coverage determination, NICE or guideline endorsement of Lin specifically. Lin bills as a provider practice, not as an FDA-cleared digital therapeutic. It markets itself as aligned with national non-opioid pain guidelines.

## 6. Evidence base
**Trials of the branded Lin program:** None found in PubMed or Europe PMC as of Oct 2026. A "New study: virtual pain care helps reduce healthcare utilization & improve work productivity" item on Lin's site has no journal, authors or link, and could not be verified as peer-reviewed.

**In-house, uncontrolled data (not peer-reviewed):**

*2023 white paper*
- 1,045 patients, Jun 2022–Jan 2023; voluntary self-report, with sample size varying by metric.
- 76% "doing better" at 3–6 months; 45% reduction in pain intensity (benchmarked to IMMPACT thresholds).
- Fear of movement (TSK-11) elevated in 57% at intake vs 13% at last report.
- NPS 60.

*Later website and press claims*
- 92% report improved pain and function.
- "Only 1 in 10" still have high pain.
- 59% of medium-to-high pain patients reach low or no pain by 6 months.
- 47% show significant pain improvement within 3 months (2024).

*Caveats:* there is no control group, a large share of data is missing, completers are self-selected, and different documents give inconsistent figures.

**Related-technique trials Lin cites (not trials of Lin):**
- **Ashar YK et al. 2022**, *JAMA Psychiatry* 79:13–23 (doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2021.2669; PMID 34586357).
  - Design: RCT of PRT vs open-label placebo injection vs usual care for chronic back pain.
  - N = 151.
  - Result: 66% of PRT participants pain-free or nearly pain-free post-treatment, vs 20% placebo and 10% usual care; largely maintained at 1 year.
  - Lin's "98% reduced their pain" figure is its own derivation from this trial.
  - 5-year follow-up: Ashar et al. 2025, *JAMA Psychiatry* 82:1049–51 (PMID 40736996).
  - Conflict of interest: of the authors, only Ashar disclosed personal fees from Lin Health (2022 paper; consultant per the 2025 follow-up). Ashar has also been a Lin clinical advisor. Other authors disclosed ties to UnitedHealth Group, the PRT Center, Curable and others, not to Lin.
- **EAET trials** (e.g., Lumley et al. 2017, fibromyalgia RCT, *Pain*) are cited as a "series of studies." They support EAET as a technique, not Lin's coach-delivered version.
- **Sturgeon J et al. 2025**, *Reg Anesth Pain Med* (PMID 40973446).
  - Design: single-arm pilot of 3-session telehealth PRT for fibromyalgia.
  - N = 35 initiated, 33 completed.
  - Result: pain intensity, interference and pain-related fear reduced among completers (3-month d ≈ 0.9–1.06); 42.3% were 'much' or 'very much improved' at 3 months. There was no control group.
  - The trial does not test Lin. Co-author Ashar is a Lin consultant (disclosed). It shows the technique extending to fibromyalgia.

**Gaps:**
- The core trials used licensed psychotherapists. Lin delivers PRT-style care mainly through coaches, a dose and fidelity difference that has not been tested.
- There is no randomized or independent evaluation of the Lin platform.
- The program's evidence for non-pain conditions it lists (POTS, CFS, hives, tinnitus, FND) is absent.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **Outcome marketing:** Headline statistics (92%, 98%, 76%, 45%, 47%) come from uncontrolled self-report or are borrowed from the PRT RCT. Some site pages show placeholder "0%" figures, and the symptom-duration criteria are inconsistent (3 vs 6 months).
- **Breadth of indications:** The listed conditions include POTS, CFS/ME, chronic hives, tinnitus, GERD, chronic cough and "pseudoseizures." This extends a back-pain evidence base to conditions with distinct or poorly understood pathophysiology.
- **Delivery and fidelity:** Care is mainly delivered by coaches rather than licensed therapists.
- **Conflicts of interest:** The first author of the pivotal PRT trial (Ashar) is a paid Lin consultant and advisor, and Mayo Clinic is both an investor and a collaborator.
- **Patient criticism:** Isolated reviews say the program overpromises, or amounts to outsourced emotional support. The wider "it's all in your brain" critique from ME/CFS and POTS patient communities applies to the model.

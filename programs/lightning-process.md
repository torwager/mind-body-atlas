# Lightning Process (LP)

*Compiled 2026-10-12 from lightningprocess.com (home, "What is the LP", Phil Parker bio, FAQ, suitability, research pages), PubMed records (25 hits for "lightning process", 22 relevant), NICE NG206, and English and Norwegian Wikipedia (LP, Recovery Norge, Live Landmark). Content is paraphrased. ASA rulings, the NTNU trial (registry, NTNU page, NEM decision) and the Norwegian draft guideline were checked against primary documents on 2026-10-13.*

## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
- **The "Physical Emergency Response" (PER).** The model centres on the PER, LP's term for the fight-or-flight / stress system. If the PER stays switched on for months or years, the resulting "allostatic load" is said to impair sleep, immunity, healing and energy.
- **Self-sustaining loop.** The illness is seen as a loop held in place by habitual neural pathways. These cover physiological arousal, attention to symptoms, thought patterns and language.
- **Neuroplasticity as the remedy.** The core premise is neuroplasticity: repeated practice of the LP tools is said to interrupt the PER loop and lay down new pathways that "support healing and energy."
- **Physical illness, neuro-physiological fix.** The FAQ calls ME/CFS a *physical* illness and calls LP a "neuro-physiological," not psychological, approach. It also says positive thinking or "pushing through" will not work.
- **How critics read it.** Critics and qualitative participants describe the model as attributing persistence to how the patient habitually "does" the illness. That framing produces feelings of blame when the process fails (Reme 2013).
- **Older framing.** Early versions also drew on osteopathic ideas of the body's self-regulating capacity (A.T. Still, J.M. Littlejohn).

## 2. One mechanism or many?
- **One mechanism.** The same PER / brain-body pathway model, and the same 3-day course, are applied to every condition: ME/CFS, long COVID, pain, fibromyalgia, anxiety, depression, MS fatigue, post-cancer fatigue.
- **No per-diagnosis protocols.** The site's suitability page lists no diagnoses at all, only generic complaints (exhaustion, recurring pain, feeling low). Practitioners tailor the examples, not the mechanism.
- **Exclusions:** active psychosis; comprehension below that of a 6-year-old; some cognitive impairment; and people who do not believe health can be influenced through a training programme.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
- **Part 1 audio (~4 h).** Presents the PER and neuroplasticity rationale and asks the person to commit to the approach. It acts as a screening and readiness step.
- **Seminar (3 consecutive days, ~12 h).** Delivered in a small group or 1:1. Methods: stories, slides, discussion and practical exercises. Components named on the site:
  - Recognising when the PER is activated.
  - A physical-plus-verbal interrupt routine combining movement, posture, gestures and spoken phrases. It is repeated many times a day, every time old symptom or thought patterns arise. The steps are proprietary; participants have described being asked to keep them confidential (Reme 2013).
  - Mental rehearsal and visualising the desired state; "meditation-like" techniques.
  - Language work: noticing and changing how one describes symptoms and identity, for example action-oriented "doing" language in place of "having" an illness. Crawley's group lists the "language focus" as a distinctive LP element.
  - Self-coaching tools and goal-setting, and a gradual return to activities.
- **Follow-up.** About 3 h of practitioner support, then self-directed practice "embedded in daily life." There is a practitioner register and an optional newsletter / ongoing-support offering.
- **Dose.** A brief, intensive front-loaded intervention (≈12 h of contact plus ≈3 h of follow-up), followed by continuous self-application of the technique.

## 4. Roots and influences
- **Neuro-linguistic programming (NLP):** language patterns, "anchoring", state change, modelling.
- **Osteopathy:** Still and Littlejohn's ideas of self-regulation.
- **Life coaching and positive psychology:** Parker's papers on positive psychology.
- **Ericksonian hypnotherapy and solution-focused therapy.** Recovery Norge's Wikipedia entry notes LP's overlap with these.
- **Selye / allostatic-load stress physiology:** the PER concept.
- **CBT-like elements.** An independent comparison (Crawley group, Taylor & Francis) found substantial overlap with CBT for fatigue. Its distinctive features were the language focus, the neurophysiological rationale, the affective/physiological "change" technique, and the delivery style.
- **Parker's spin-offs:** the Rediscovery Process for addiction (studied with London Metropolitan University), and practitioner training through the Phil Parker Training Institute.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- **UK.**
  - Not available on the NHS.
  - The SMILE trial was run inside an NHS paediatric CFS/ME service in Bath, with University of Bristol involvement. The NHS has not adopted LP.
  - **NICE NG206 (Oct 2021) explicitly says not to offer LP or therapies based on it** for ME/CFS.
- **Advertising.** Two ASA rulings against Phil Parker Group Ltd followed Hampshire Trading Standards complaints:
  - **22 Aug 2012 (A11-158035), lightningprocess.com.** 2 of 4 issues upheld: the CFS/ME survey claim ("81.3%" of clients free of their issues by day 3) and the condition-specific pages, both because the self-assessed, uncontrolled studies were not robust evidence. Not upheld: "completely unique" and the NHS references.
  - **27 Mar 2013 (A12-210374), Parker's personal site lightningprocess.co.uk.** Upheld: it implied LP could treat ME/CFS, MS, depression, anxiety, chronic pain and eating disorders. The claims had been removed in Feb 2013.
- **Patient bodies.** The World ME Alliance (2022) declined to endorse it, and the UK and Norwegian ME associations advise against it.
- **Norway.** This is where LP has had the most institutional traction:
  - NAV (the labour and welfare agency) has at times recommended and partly paid for courses.
  - **Recovery Norge** was started by physician-researcher Henrik Vogt (network Jan 2017; organisation Jan 2018). It gathers people who say they recovered through mental and behavioural techniques. Most of its recovery stories involve LP, and many LP instructors are members.
  - **Live Landmark** is an LP instructor, holds a PhD in psychology from NTNU (thesis defended May 2026, per no.wikipedia) and is the author of a 2009 recovery memoir. She was among Recovery Norge's founders and a PhD candidate on the NTNU trial of a 3-day CFS/ME intervention (Kennair group, funded by the Research Council of Norway in 2020). The intervention is the branded LP: NTNU's study page says the study "will use the Lightning Process course," and the registry says it is built on Parker's 2013 LP manual.
  - **Ethics history.** REK midt approved the first version on 16 Nov 2020. NEM overturned that approval in spring 2021 over her financial conflict of interest. REK midt approved a revised application on 31 Jan 2022. NEM rejected complaints from ME organisations on 13 Jul 2022 and added conditions, which settles the "February vs July 2022" discrepancy in no.wikipedia. Recovery Norge provides user involvement.
  - **Trial design (NCT05236465).** Adults with CFS/ME (Canadian criteria) are randomised to the course or a 10-week wait-list. Target n=100; primary outcome is physical function at 10 weeks.
  - **Status.** The registry gives an actual start date of 21 Mar 2022 and was still "recruiting" at its Nov 2025 update; primary completion is estimated for Jan 2027.
  - **Recruitment.** The course is free to participants, and residents of participating Viken municipalities get priority.
  - Helsenorge.no linked to Recovery Norge. In December 2019 a lawyer objected publicly that the government site was promoting quackery.
  - **National guideline.** Helsedirektoratet's draft national guideline on long-lasting fatigue incl. ME/CFS (draft published 3–4 Feb 2026; consultation closed 4 May 2026; final expected 15 Dec 2026) had a 21-member working group.
    - Recovery Norge held 2 of the 4 user-representative seats (Ann Kristin Grøntjernet, Hannah Sagstad). The other two were Norges ME-forening and Norsk Covidforening; ME-foreldrene had a parent seat.
    - Four professional members filed dissents.
    - The draft's background page and Helsedirektoratet's announcement do not mention LP. Consultation responses criticised Recovery Norge's share of the user seats.
- **Insurance.** No insurer or CMS coverage. Purely self-pay.

## 6. Evidence base
**RCT of the branded programme (only one):**
- **Crawley EM et al., *Arch Dis Child* 2018;103:155–164 (SMILE)**, PMID 28931531, doi:10.1136/archdischild-2017-313375.
  - Design: pragmatic, open-label RCT in 12–18-year-olds with mild/moderate CFS/ME, comparing specialist medical care (SMC) with SMC+LP. N=100 randomised (51 to SMC+LP), 81 analysed at 6 months.
  - Primary outcome: SF-36 Physical Function was higher with SMC+LP, by +12.5 (95% CI 4.5–20.5) at 6 months and +15.1 (5.8–24.4) at 12 months.
  - Secondary outcomes: fatigue and anxiety improved at 6 months; depression and school attendance at 12 months. The authors call it "probably cost-effective."
  - **Correction (2019; doi:10.1136/archdischild-2017-313375corr1).** Issued after critics (notably David Tuller and others) showed that the trial had been registered retrospectively, that participants from the feasibility phase had been folded into the full trial, and that the primary outcome had been switched from school attendance to SF-36 PF. The journal published a correction and clarification, plus an editor's note (e3), rather than retracting. Critics also point to the open-label design, the subjective outcomes in an intervention that teaches positive self-report, and the absence of an attention-matched control.
- **Supporting SMILE papers:** feasibility study and protocol, Crawley et al., *Trials* 2013 (PMIDs 24304689, 24370208).

**Other peer-reviewed LP studies (uncontrolled or qualitative):**
| Citation | Design | N | Key result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reme SE, Archer N, Chalder T. *Br J Health Psychol* 2013 (PMID 22989369) | Qualitative interviews, young people with CFS/ME | 9 (+3 parents) | 7 much improved, 2 not. Unhelpful aspects: intensity, secrecy, little follow-up, feeling blamed when it failed. Independent of LP. |
| Fauske L et al. *Cancers* 2021 (PMID 34439229) | Uncontrolled mixed-methods pilot, AYA cancer survivors with chronic fatigue | 13 | Fatigue fell at 3 and 6 months (p<0.001). Oslo group (Reme). |
| Parker P, Aston J, de Rijk L. *Explore* 2021 (PMID 32888829) | Systematic review | 14 studies | "Emerging" evidence; no meta-analysis; early studies lacked controls. **Authored by the founder and an ex-practitioner.** |
| Finch F et al. *Explore* 2024 (PMID 38176975) | Case reports, long COVID | 2 | Improved fatigue at 3 months. |
| Arroll B et al. *J Family Med Prim Care* 2025 (PMID 40115575; erratum PMID 41280592; critical letter PMID 41624666) | Retrospective telephone audit, long COVID | 12 | 11/12 reported being ≥85% back to normal; no harms reported. |
| Landmark L, Lindgren RM, Sivertsen B, Magnus P, Conradi S, Thorvaldsen SN, Stanghelle JK. *Tidsskr Nor Laegeforen* 2016;136:396 (PMID 26983138) | Multi-author debate article on LP experience (3 critical comments, Apr 2016) | n/a | Not outcome data. |

- **Not peer-reviewed.** These are hosted on the LP site and are uncontrolled:
  - "Alpine" outcome study (n=96)
  - SF-36 outcome study (n=205)
  - Snapshot survey 2007–10 (n=1,297; 81% "improved")
  - MS proof-of-concept
  - Norwegian pain evaluation (n=12)
- **Patient surveys pointing the other way:**
  - UK ME Association 2010: of the 101 LP users, the largest share "greatly improved," but deterioration was also common.
  - Norwegian ME Association member survey (n=1,096 respondents, ~2012–13): 49% of LP course-takers reported being worse or much worse vs. 21% better. no.wikipedia does not say how many respondents had taken LP.
  - Kielland A, Liu J, Jason LA. *J Health Psychol* 2023;28:1189–1203 (PMID 37114822). Independent survey of 660 Norwegian fatigue patients, recruited by respondent-driven sampling. LP was among the interventions rated. On average, patients rated most interventions as having low-to-negative effects, and higher PEM scores went with worse experiences.
  - NAFKAM (Norway) 2015 report: 39% had a negative course.
- **Gaps:**
  - Only one RCT, and it is open-label.
  - No adult RCT; no RCT in long COVID or pain.
  - **Planned:** FALCON (NCT07697261), McMaster University (Busse et al.). A virtual RCT in long COVID of LP vs. activity pacing, n≈100, with self-reported fatigue at 1 year as the primary outcome. In Sept 2026 it was still seeking ethics approval, and a petition with >1,700 signatures called for a pause (*The Sick Times*, 22 Sep 2026).
  - No objective outcomes (actigraphy, employment).
  - Most studies involve the founder or LP practitioners.
  - The site's claims go well beyond the data: it cites 17 studies in one place and "18 peer-reviewed studies" in another, calls LP "safe", and says it has "0 side effects".

## 7. Popularity and trajectory
- **~1998–2004:** Parker develops LP in his osteopathy and coaching practice. Spread is mainly word of mouth in the UK.
- **2007–2012: UK peak of media attention.**
  - Celebrity testimonials and national-press features.
  - 2010 book.
  - ME Association survey (2010).
  - Rapid Norwegian uptake; Landmark's memoir appeared in 2009.
  - ASA rulings (Aug 2012, Mar 2013).
  - Norwegian controversy, including a November 2011 NRK report of a 13-year-old's breakdown and suicide attempt after a course.
- **2013–2016:** SMILE feasibility and protocol papers (2013). Norwegian debate in *Tidsskriftet* (2016).
- **2017–2019:** SMILE results (Sept 2017) bring a surge of UK coverage and criticism, followed by the 2019 correction. Recovery Norge is founded (2017–18).
- **2020–2022:** Founder's systematic review (2020/21). NICE NG206 recommends against LP (2021). Long COVID becomes a new marketing target.
- **2023–2026:**
  - Long COVID case reports and audits (2024–25).
  - BBC *File on 4* investigation (2024).
  - The NTNU trial (NCT05236465) has been recruiting since 2022.
  - Recovery Norge holds 2 user seats on Norway's draft fatigue guideline (2026).
  - 2026: the McMaster FALCON long COVID trial (planned) draws a petition and media criticism.
  - The site claims >75,000 people trained over "27 years."
- **Overall pattern:** a persistent niche business with high visibility relative to its size, driven largely by controversy.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **Trial integrity.** SMILE was retrospectively registered, had outcome switching and included feasibility-phase participants, and was corrected rather than retracted in 2019.
- **Regulation.** ASA upheld complaints about misleading efficacy claims (Aug 2012; Mar 2013). NICE recommends against LP (2021).
- **Harms.**
  - Patient-organisation surveys in the UK and Norway report substantial rates of deterioration.
  - Participants report feeling blamed when the method fails, and report secrecy rules.
  - In a Norwegian case reported by NRK in November 2011, a 13-year-old had a breakdown and attempted suicide after the course.
  - The site's "no side effects" claim is disputed.
- **Framing.**
  - It implies ME/CFS is maintained by the patient's own thoughts and language, despite the site's "physical illness" wording.
  - In a 2024 BBC *File on 4* report, a coach told the reporter there was nothing physically wrong with her body.
  - Neuroscientist Camilla Nord has criticised its use of neuroscience language; in the *BMJ* (21 Sept 2017), Nigel Hawkes described it as secretive and as having a "cultish quality."
- **Commercial model.**
  - Trademark licensing and fee-sharing; some critics liken it to multi-level marketing.
  - Conflicts of interest in research: founder-authored reviews, and LP instructors as investigators (the Norwegian ethics reversal in 2021).
- **Norway.** Polarised debate between Recovery Norge / LP-affiliated researchers and the Norwegian ME Association over research ethics and national guidance.

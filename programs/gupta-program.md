## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
- **Original model (Gupta 2002, *Med Hypotheses* 59:727).**
  - CFS is a neurophysiological disorder centred on the **amygdala**.
  - During a "traumatic" neurological event (acute psychological stress plus a virus, chemical or physiological stressor), a conditioned "cell assembly" forms in the amygdala.
  - Visceral and bodily symptom signals, and chemical or dietary stressors, become **conditioned stimuli**. The conditioned response is chronic **sympathetic outflow** via the hypothalamus, with immune reactivation, exhaustion and secondary complications.
  - The amygdala monitors exactly these symptoms, which closes a self-perpetuating loop that runs outside awareness.
  - Recovery requires medial prefrontal "top-down" inhibition of the amygdala.
- **Current model (website and trial papers).**
  - "Insula" was added for interoceptive monitoring.
  - After a significant stressor (virus, bacteria, pain, chemical exposure, prolonged stress), the brain's threat system (amygdala/insula) stays "on high alert" and the nervous system gets "stuck" in fight/flight/freeze.
  - The brain then sends ongoing danger signals that keep the **immune system, nervous system and pain networks** activated.
  - The result is fatigue, pain, sensitivities, inflammation, "faster energy use" and hormonal imbalance.
- **Framing.** The program does not call symptoms psychological. It calls them real, physical, brain-driven and reversible through **neuroplasticity** (repeatedly interrupting the old pattern and building "safety" pathways).
- **Core mechanism terms:** limbic/amygdala threat conditioning, interoceptive hypervigilance (insula), autonomic sympathetic overdrive, and stress-immune activation.

## 2. One mechanism or many?
**One unified mechanism.** Every condition page (ME/CFS, long COVID, MCS, MCAS, mould, Lyme, POTS, IBS and others) attributes the illness to the same overprotective brain threat response after a trigger. Differences are only in the trigger and the dominant symptom:
- chemicals and odours for MCS
- food for food sensitivities
- post-exertional malaise for ME/CFS

No condition-specific techniques are described publicly. Practice is tailored by applying the same interrupt-and-retrain technique to whatever symptom or trigger each person has. Bratty (2023) explicitly cites the "functional somatic disorder" / shared brain–body danger-signal hypothesis as justification for applying AIR across 16 conditions. Medical care, medication and pacing are framed as complementary and not to be stopped. (In the Iranian fibromyalgia RCT, however, AIR participants were asked to stop their FM medication.)

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
- **Primary neuroplasticity techniques.**
  - The retraining protocol is a multi-step practice; the current site calls it a "7-step process." It uses a floor or printed **retraining chart**: noticing a symptom, worry or trigger thought, then interrupting it (e.g., a firm internal "STOP"), physically stepping across the chart, and replacing the pattern with rehearsed calm/safety statements and vivid **visualisation of a healthy past or future self** and positive emotion.
  - Done as full sessions (about 10–20 min) plus **30–60-second mini-retrains** many times a day whenever symptoms, symptom-checking or anxious thoughts arise.
- **Supporting techniques.**
  - Mindfulness meditation, about 20 min/day. A Spanish academic adaptation formally merged this into "MAIR."
  - **Alternate-nostril breathing** and other breathwork, somatic and vagus-nerve "regulation" exercises, and daily live "Guptacise" sessions.
  - Hundreds of guided meditations; a calm morning ritual.
- **Lifestyle components.** Sleep, nutrition, pacing-style energy management, general health-supplement suggestions, and work on "retraining parts" (self-talk, perfectionism, people-pleasing).
- **Dose.** About **40–60 min/day**, per the 2023 trial: core techniques, a few minutes of breathing and a 20-min meditation. The site advertises "as little as 20 minutes a day." Minimum recommended duration is **3 months, preferably 6**.
- **Structure.**
  - Current version: 70+ modules in four parts (nervous-system regulation, brain retraining, lifestyle, "retraining parts"), with a 90-day pathway or self-paced use.
  - Research versions: 15 modules plus weekly webinars.
  - Hospital adaptations: 8–10 weekly group sessions, sometimes followed by monthly boosters.
- **Support.** Weekly live Q&A webinars with Gupta, certified coaches (former patients), a forum and buddy system, an AI assistant, and a free 15-min discovery call.

## 4. Roots and influences
- **Conditioning and fear-learning neuroscience** (LeDoux-style amygdala fear conditioning; Pavlovian "conditioned stimulus" language), plus **prefrontal top-down regulation** of extinction.
- **NLP / cognitive "pattern interrupt"**, affirmations and visualisation (life-coaching / positive-psychology idiom). This is structurally similar to the Lightning Process (Parker, ~1998–2000) and to DNRS (Hopper, 2008), which belong to the same "limbic retraining" family.
- **CBT-style thought monitoring and replacement**, and **mindfulness** (MBSR-derived meditation) and **yogic pranayama** (alternate-nostril breathing).
- **Polyvagal / nervous-system-regulation** vocabulary in the newer somatic and vagal modules.
- Integration with **functional/integrative medicine** networks: its advisors and endorsers include Hanaway (Institute for Functional Medicine), Neil Nathan and Michael Ruscio.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- **Guidelines.** Not recommended in any guideline.
  - The UK NICE ME/CFS guideline (NG206, 2021) says not to offer the Lightning Process "or therapies based on it." Gupta is not named, but patient groups often place it in this class.
  - The BMJ living systematic review of long-COVID interventions (Zeraatkar et al. 2024) indexes the AIR RCT among long-COVID trials. This does not amount to an endorsement.
- **Institutional research use.**
  - Mayo Clinic Fibromyalgia & Chronic Fatigue Clinic (pilot RCT, 2009–2011).
  - Hospital Universitario Miguel Servet / University of Zaragoza (García-Campayo group: fibromyalgia 2015–16; long COVID 2023, n=120).
  - Helsinki University Hospital Clinic for Functional Disorders (3-arm RCT, n=360 planned, registered 2022).
  - Miami VA / Nova Southeastern (Klimas, Tamariz: long-COVID RCT registered 2023).
- **Clinical use.** No routine NHS, VA or US health-system clinical use found. Uptake runs through referral by integrative/functional practitioners, and the company offers clinics a co-branded "concierge service."
- **Insurance:** none found. The program is self-pay.

**Uncontrolled / other publications:**
- Gupta A. *J Holistic Healthcare* 2010;7(2):12–15. Clinical audit of CFS patients; the site reports 93% improved. Not peer-indexed and uncontrolled.
- Bratty AJ. *Integr Med (Encinitas)* 2023;22(6) (Dec). PMID 38404605. - Gasión V, …, Montero-Marin J. *BMC Complement Med Ther* 2023;23:405. doi:10.1186/s12906-023-04240-0. **Protocol** for AIR + mindfulness vs relaxation in Spanish-NHS-diagnosed long COVID (protocol planned n=100; NCT05956405 lists 120 actually enrolled, completed March 2024). - Vangelova-Korpinen V, …, Liira H. *PLoS One* 2025. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0316169. **Protocol** for a Helsinki University Hospital 3-arm RCT (online AIR vs HUS internet CBT vs TAU, n=360) in persistent somatic symptoms, fibromyalgia, ME/CFS and post-COVID condition (NCT05212467; registry status "unknown," estimated completion Dec 2024). Results not found.
- **CCRF-sponsored RCTs registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (results unpublished as of Oct 2026):** long COVID at Miami VA (NCT05851846, n=130 planned, status "unknown"), multiple sclerosis (NCT07427433, n=132, completed March 2025), mould illness (NCT07425431, n=134, completed August 2025), perimenopause (NCT07427459, n=160 planned, recruiting). The MS and mould trials were first registered on 16 Feb 2026, after they had finished, so they are **retrospectively registered**.

**Assessment.**
- The evidence is a series of small trials with large effect sizes, heavy and often differential attrition, self-report outcomes only, and active controls that are not credibility-matched.
- Most recent trials are company-consultant co-authored and donor-funded.
- No objective outcomes have been reported (actigraphy, exercise tests, PEM), and there is no long-term follow-up.
- The amygdala/insula mechanism has never been tested: no neuroimaging or conditioning measures appear in any trial.
- The most independent work is the Mayo pilot and the Zaragoza pilot.
- Two larger independent-hospital RCTs (Zaragoza long COVID, Helsinki) would be the most informative, but they are unpublished.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory
- **2002:** Hypothesis paper published.
- **c. 2007–2010:** DVD/home-study "Amygdala Retraining" course sold from London (about £99). An early following in UK ME/CFS circles, with early debate on Phoenix Rising (from 2008) and other ME forums.
- **2009–2012:** Mayo pilot conducted and published, which gave a credibility boost and is still cited.
- **2013–2019:** Steady niche presence. Online membership launched. The ASA upheld a complaint in 2018, reported by the ME Association on 11 April 2018, and a Science for ME discussion thread opened the same day.
- **2020–2023:** **Large growth with long COVID**: interviews in *Integrative Medicine* (2021, 2022), long-COVID RCT press releases (Newswise/HealthDay, July 2023), expansion into mould/CIRS, MCAS and Lyme audiences via functional-medicine podcasts, and the app launch (around 2023). Spanish, German, Italian and Finnish versions followed.
- **2024–2026:** Daily live sessions, AI support, multiple registered CCRF trials and an affiliate scheme.
- **Current metrics:**
  - The company claims 40,000+ users.
  - YouTube @GuptaProgram: about 11.7K subscribers, about 1.35M channel views (scraped Oct 2026).
  - Trustpilot: 465 reviews, 4.5/5 (60 in the last 12 months).
  - Google Play: 10K+ installs.
- **Reach.** Smaller than DNRS (whose own claim is larger) and comparable to ANS Rewire / Primal Trust in social-media footprint.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **UK ASA (2018).** On 11 Apr 2018 the ASA upheld all three challenged claims against Harley Street Solutions Ltd t/a Gupta Amygdala Retraining (ruling A17-400324): that the program could treat fibromyalgia, CFS/ME and "electrical sensitivities." The company had submitted two published studies and a draft manuscript. The ASA found them insufficient: the Mayo trial tested the program as an add-on to standard care and lost over half its participants, the draft study did not match the advertised method, and the audit was uncontrolled and written by the company's director. It found breaches of CAP Code rules 3.1, 3.7 and 12.1 and told the company not to imply the program can treat these conditions. The current site still lists ME/CFS, fibromyalgia and electrical hypersensitivity under "Conditions We Treat/Support" and calls the program "clinically proven" (it hedges by saying it "supports" recovery).
- **Patient-community objections.** ME/CFS patient organisations and forums (S4ME, Phoenix Rising, ME Association) object to:
  - the premise that ME/CFS is perpetuated by conditioned brain threat responses
  - the risk that "overriding" symptoms leads to post-exertional crashes
  - the cost
  - the guarantee's restrictive terms
  - the implied blame when retraining fails
- **Research integrity.** Recent trials are run by a paid company consultant, with donor funding channelled through an affiliated fund. Two of the CCRF trials were registered only after they had finished. The site claims 92% of users report "symptom reversal" but gives no method. The headline website percentages derive from an uncontrolled user survey but are presented as trial results. The site describes the program as "clinically proven."
- **Scope creep.** Claims extend to Lyme disease, SIBO, "certain autoimmune conditions," MS and perimenopause, with no mechanistic differentiation.
- **Commercial ecosystem.** The program is classed with other "illness entrepreneurship" recovery-coach models: former patients selling recovery (cf. Geiger & Loza, *J Bus Ethics* 2026, which discusses the recovery-coach genre).

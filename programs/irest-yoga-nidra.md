## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
- **Officially non-mechanistic and contemplative.** iRest's own pages describe it as an adaptation of yoga nidra combining yogic/nondual teaching with Western psychology and neuroscience. The stated aim is equanimity: meeting sensations, emotions and thoughts without judgment, clinging or aversion. The site's About, Yoga Nidra and Chronic Pain pages give **no physiological mechanism** for disease.
- **Implicit model (from the protocol, Miller's books and trial rationales):**
  - Suffering is driven by **reactivity to and identification with** body sensations, emotions and thoughts. Steps 6–7 treat these as "messengers" to be welcomed rather than resisted.
  - Distress lessens through **dis-identification** ("I am not pain, I have pain," in the Barber 2025 participants' words) and by resting in "awareness" (nondual self-inquiry: "Who am I?").
  - **Felt safety via an "Inner Resource"** acts as an anchor for trauma-exposed people, a trauma-informed titration element.
  - **Deep relaxation / parasympathetic shift and sleep-like states.** Research rationales cite reduced arousal, HRV and respiratory-rate changes, and "conscious non-REM sleep" (Parker 2019; Datta 2022 EEG).
- For PTSD, the implied mechanism is reduced hyperarousal and emotional reactivity plus self-compassion; for pain, reduced pain-related distress/interference and greater self-efficacy. Pain science is discussed in a paid webinar only.

## 2. One mechanism or many?
**Transdiagnostic, not disorder-focused.** The same 10-step protocol is used for every population. Differentiation is only in emphasis and packaging:
- "iRest for Pain" group programs (6 weeks, Australia), with pain-science framing.
- Trauma-informed delivery (the Inner Resource first, choice and safety) for PTSD/MST.
- Sleep-oriented audios.
- *The iRest Program for Healing PTSD* (book, 2015).

The protocol makes no claims about distinct pathophysiology for named illnesses. It does not address ME/CFS, POTS, MCS, Lyme etc. as such.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
**The 10-step protocol** (guided, usually lying down; steps 1–3 every session; steps 1–5 suffice for beginners):
1. **Heartfelt desire (sankalpa):** a deep life wish, felt as already true.
2. **Intention** for the session (e.g., rest, or exploring a sensation or emotion).
3. **Inner Resource:** a felt sense of security and calm in the body, optionally via an imagined place or person. It can be returned to at any time, especially when distressed.
4. **Body sensing:** a rotation of attention through body regions.
5. **Breath awareness:** sensing breath at the nostrils, throat, ribs and belly, and as energy through the body; counting breaths.
6. **Welcoming emotions**, including opposites held together (e.g., anxious/at ease), without trying to change them.
7. **Witnessing thoughts, beliefs and images**, and their bodily correlates; working with opposites of beliefs.
8. **Joy/wellbeing:** cultivating warmth from the heart or belly and extending it outward.
9. **Awareness / "lasting peace":** resting in nondual, awake awareness.
10. **Reflection and integration** into daily life.

**Dose/structure in practice:**
- VA classes typically run 8–10 weeks, 1–2 × 60–90-min sessions per week (Pence 2014: 19 sessions over 10 weeks), plus daily home audio of 20–30 min.
- University and health-worker programs: 8 weeks.
- Older adults (Wahbeh 2019): a 2-day retreat + 20 min/day for 6 weeks.
- Short "iRest at Ease" practices of 5–10 min for daily stress.

**Other components:**
- co-meditation "dyads"
- movement/breath classes ("Move Breathe Rest")
- prāṇāyāma course
- silent retreats

## 4. Roots and influences
- **Hindu yoga / Vedanta:** yoga nidra as described in the Mandukya and Taittiriya Upanishads, the Yogataravali, and Patanjali (per the site). It was modernised by Swami Sivananda's lineage, Swami Satyananda Saraswati (Bihar School, 1960s–70s), Swami Veda Bharati and Rammurti Mishra.
- **Nondual traditions:** Advaita Vedanta, Kashmiri Tantra, Taoism and Buddhism (listed in Miller's bio). Secondary accounts link Miller to the Advaita teacher Jean Klein; this was not verified on the current site.
- **Western psychology:** humanistic/transpersonal psychology, mindfulness (iRest is often described as "a form of mindfulness meditation"), and trauma-informed resourcing similar to Somatic Experiencing (Inner Resource).
- **Relaxation tradition:** body-scan and relaxation-response methods (autogenic training, progressive relaxation).

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- **DoD:** The origin study was a Walter Reed Army Medical Center / Samueli Institute PTSD feasibility study (NCT00269490; start Dec 2005, 7 enrolled; 18 classes over 10 weeks + daily CD practice). No peer-reviewed results paper for it was found. The site states, without cited documentation, that the US Army Surgeon General and the Defense Centers of Excellence recognized iRest as a "complementary program" for chronic pain and PTSD.
- **VA:**
  - The DC VA Integrative Health & Wellness Program offered iRest alongside acupuncture and qigong; it was the third most-used service in its first year (Hull 2015).
  - Classes run at many VAMCs and Vet Centers, including Tacoma and the Washington DC WRIISC (War Related Illness and Injury Study Center).
  - Meditation is among the CIH approaches VHA must make available under Directive 1137 (2017).
- **Other:** Spaulding/Harvard's virtual chronic-pain program (FINER) and the Barwon Health pain unit in Australia have run iRest-style or iRest-for-Pain groups. Integrative mental health inpatient programs (EHN Guardians Gateway, Canada) also use it.
- **Guidelines:** iRest is not named in NICE, APA or VA/DoD PTSD clinical practice guidelines as a recommended treatment. Meditation in general is listed only as a complementary option.
- **Insurance:** No dedicated billing code. It is reimbursed only indirectly: free in VA/DoD, or embedded in clinician sessions. Teacher training is self-paid (some CE credit).

## 6. Evidence base
**Trials and studies of the branded iRest protocol** (verified via PubMed or DOI):

| Study | Design | N | Key result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stankovic 2011, *Int J Yoga Therap* 21:23–37 (PMID 22398342) | Qualitative feasibility, 8 wk, combat veterans with PTSD (Bay Area) | 16 (11 completers) | Self-reported less rage, anxiety and reactivity; more relaxation and self-efficacy. |
| Eastman-Mueller 2013, *Int J Yoga Therap* 23:15–24 (PMID 24165520) | Uncontrolled pre–post, 8 wk, college students | 66 | Reduced stress, worry and depression; increased mindfulness. |
| Pence 2014, *Int J Yoga Therap* 24:53–62 (PMID 25858651) | Uncontrolled pilot, 10 wk (19 × 90 min), women with sexual trauma/MST at a VA | 15 (10 completers) | PCL d=0.66, depression d=0.64, self-blame cognitions d=0.52. |
| Nassif 2015/16, *Military Behavioral Health* 4(1):82–89, doi:10.1080/21635781.2015.1119772 | Pilot **RCT**, iRest vs TAU, 8 wk, veterans with moderate TBI and chronic pain | 9 (4 vs 5) | In the iRest arm, pain intensity fell 23–42% (g 0.37–0.61) and interference fell 34–41% (g 0.92–1.13). These are reported as changes on "most outcome measures", not as significant between-group tests. Very underpowered. |
| Gutman 2017, *OTJR* 37:5–13 (PMID 27760887) | **RCT**, 3 arms (iRest + sleep hygiene vs Dreampad pillow vs sleep hygiene), 2 wk | 29 (9 iRest) | iRest group had greater total sleep time (d≈1.8). The Dreampad group had fewer night awakenings than iRest. No difference in sleep quality, latency or fatigue. Miller is a co-author (COI). |
| Livingston 2018, *Holist Nurs Pract* 32:160–166 (PMID 29642130) | Pre–post, 8 wk, health-care workers | 15 | Mindfulness (FFMQ) improved; sleepiness and pain did not change significantly. |
| Wahbeh & Nelson 2019, *Int J Yoga Therap* 29:9–17 (PMID 30354905) | **Pilot RCT**, 2-day iRest retreat + 6 wk home practice vs "vacation" control, older adults with depressive symptoms | 30 | Feasible; sleep impairment improved vs control at 6 wk; only trends for depression and pain. |
| Wahbeh & Fry 2019, *Int J Yoga Therap* 29:51–56 (PMID 30664388) | 6- and 12-month follow-up of the above | 25 | Both groups improved from baseline. **No between-group difference** in depression; stress and negative mood favoured the *control* group. Only 5 iRest participants were still practising at 12 months. |
| Breneman 2023, *Glob Adv Integr Med Health* 12 (PMID 37151571; NCT02180243) | **RCT** (run 2016–2021), iRest + auricular acupuncture vs Gulf War health education (active control), 8 wk, Gulf War Illness. The abstract says "mindfulness meditation"; the registry specifies iRest Yoga Nidra | 149 (75 vs 74) | Better mental-health functioning, fatigue, depression and overall GWI severity vs control. iRest is **confounded** with acupuncture. |
| Barber 2025, *Complement Ther Clin Pract* 59:101955 (PMID 39862599) | Qualitative, 6-wk "iRest for Pain" group in a hospital pain unit | small (qualitative) | Themes: relaxation, dis-identification from pain, self-efficacy, intent to reduce opioids. Co-authors include Miller and Lopez of the iRest Institute, yet the paper declares no competing interests. |
| Martin 2026, *Psychiatr Q* (PMID 41663818) | Observational component analysis, inpatient PTSD/SUD program | 345 | Of optional components, only iRest participation was associated with additional improvement in depression. Non-randomised. |

**Generic yoga nidra** (not iRest-specific; mostly Satyananda/Bihar-style scripts, many Indian trials):
- Ghai 2026 meta-analysis, *Ann NY Acad Sci* (PMID 41327816): 73 studies, N=5,201. Large pooled effects on stress, anxiety and depression (g −0.7 to −1.7), but low quality and likely inflated.
- Ghai & Ghai 2025, pain meta-analysis (PMID 41187098): 12 studies. Benefit vs passive controls (g −2.05) but **not vs active comparators** (g −0.31, ns).
- Sleep meta-analyses: Singh 2026 (PMID 42043659) and Dutta 2026 (PMID 41144325).
- Sharpe 2023 (PMID 36731199) sleep-lab RCT, N=22: no EEG alpha or HRV change; respiratory rate fell.
- Registered trials: UCL (University College London) remote **iRest-audio** yoga nidra RCT for insomnia/PTSD (NCT06888336, N=60, completed Mar 2026, results not yet published) and several UCF chronic low back pain trials (NCT06979713, NCT07543835).

**Gaps:**
- No adequately powered RCT of iRest alone for PTSD, chronic pain or insomnia.
- No trials in ME/CFS, fibromyalgia, IBS or other central sensitivity syndromes.
- Many studies have small samples, no control group, high attrition, or involvement of the developers (Miller/Lopez co-authors).
- The site's claim of "35+ research studies" includes theses, conference reports and unpublished projects.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory
- **2005–2006:** Walter Reed study; nonprofit established (IRS ruling Dec 2006).
- **2009–2010:** irest.us website live (Wayback 2009). Miller's *Yoga Nidra: A Meditative Practice for Deep Relaxation and Healing* (Sounds True, 2010). Army Pain Management Task Force era (c. 2010) brings military media coverage.
- **2011–2015:** First peer-reviewed iRest papers. Expansion across VA facilities. Revenue grew from $0.49M (2011) to $0.86M (2015) (IRS 990 via ProPublica).
- **2015:** *The iRest Program for Healing PTSD* (New Harbinger).
- **2016–2020:** Revenue $1.08M → $1.54M (2020 peak). VHA Directive 1137 (2017) institutionalises meditation in the VA.
- **2021–2024:** Revenue $1.24M → $1.40M → $0.99M (2023) → $0.78M (2024), with expenses exceeding revenue in 2021 and 2023, suggesting a plateau or slight decline after the pandemic online boom. Meanwhile "yoga nidra" as a generic term rose sharply with Huberman's "Non-Sleep Deep Rest" (NSDR) popularisation (2021+) and Insight Timer/YouTube yoga nidra tracks. Much of that traffic is generic rather than iRest-branded (inferred; Google Trends not quantified here).
- **2024–2026:** 500+ certified teachers; ongoing online Level 1/2 trainings (several cohorts a year); Miller's role shifted to consultant. Academic yoga nidra research has expanded rapidly (multiple 2025–2026 meta-analyses).

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- Evidence claims on the site (e.g., "shown to be effective in scientific trials," a pain study cited without reference) go beyond the small, mostly uncontrolled literature. The one long-term RCT follow-up (Wahbeh 2019) found no advantage over a vacation control.
- Developer involvement: the founder or institute staff co-authored several studies.
- The nondual/spiritual "awakening" framing sits alongside clinical claims. Some clinicians note that body-focused practices can trigger trauma memories; iRest answers this with its Inner Resource and trauma-informed delivery.
- No major public controversy (contrast with Lightning Process/DNRS); criticism concerns evidence quality rather than harms.

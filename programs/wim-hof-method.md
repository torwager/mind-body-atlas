## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
WHM is **not framed as a disorder model**. It is a "hormetic" self-regulation practice: deliberate, brief, voluntary stressors are claimed to train the autonomic, immune and stress systems.

- **Voluntary sympathetic activation → immune modulation (core science claim).** Cyclic hyperventilation followed by breath-holding produces respiratory alkalosis and intermittent hypoxia. This sharply raises plasma **epinephrine**, which raises IL-10 and suppresses TNF-α, IL-6 and IL-8. This rests on Kox et al. 2014 (PNAS): the autonomic and innate immune systems, once thought involuntary, can be influenced at will. Note that this is acute *sympathetic arousal*, the opposite of the "calm the nervous system" logic of most programs in this survey.
- **Vagal / parasympathetic claims (marketing level).** Site and app copy say diaphragmatic breathing "stimulates the vagus nerve", lowers cortisol, "alkalizes the body" and activates brown fat. These claims are uncited.
- **Cold exposure as resilience / vascular training.** Cold is said to train vascular smooth muscle, activate brown adipose tissue, reduce inflammation, boost immunity and improve stress tolerance. A twin case study found no brown-fat difference in Hof.
- **"Brain over body" / mindset.** Commitment and focus are claimed to give top-down control of physiology. The site cites a single-subject fMRI/PET study of Hof (Muzik 2018) that reported periaqueductal-grey and insula activation during cold. It also cites later small PET/fMRI work on interoceptive networks and endocannabinoid (CB1) binding.
- **Metabolic.** Practitioners show higher plasma lactate and pyruvate, which correlate with an anti-inflammatory cytokine profile (Zwaag 2020, metabolomics).
- **Pain.** Hyperventilation raises electrical pain-detection thresholds acutely and for about 4 hours (Zwaag 2023).

## 2. One mechanism or many?
**Single unified mechanism.** There is no disease taxonomy. All benefits are attributed to the same three pillars acting on autonomic, immune and stress physiology plus "mindset". The method does not distinguish between ME/CFS, fibromyalgia, pain or mood conditions, and does not discuss central sensitization or neuroplastic pain. The only condition-specific content is **contraindications**:
- cardiovascular disease, heart failure or stroke
- epilepsy
- pregnancy
- Raynaud's type II
- cold urticaria
- kidney failure
- medicated hypertension
- recent surgery
- caution with migraine for ice baths
- when ill: breathing only, no cold

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
**Three pillars: Breathing, Cold, Mindset/Commitment.** About 20 min/day.

1. **Breathing (the signature technique).** Sit or lie down, never in water or while driving.
   - Take ~30 deep belly breaths: full inhale, relaxed passive exhale.
   - After the last exhale, hold the breath (retention) until the urge to breathe.
   - Take one deep recovery breath and hold it for ~15 s.
   - Do 3–4 rounds, typically on waking or on an empty stomach.
   - Expected effects: tingling, light-headedness, sometimes fainting. The site calls these harmless if practised safely.
   - Variants: slow-paced or "power" breathing, and push-ups during retention (Classic course).
2. **Cold exposure.** Graded cold showers, starting with 15–30 s at the end of a warm shower and building to minutes. Then ice baths (~1–2 min, building up), outdoor cold and barefoot snow walks. Breathe normally during water immersion.
3. **Mindset / commitment / focus.** Concentration, visualization and "third-eye" meditation (the Kox 2014 protocol), gratitude and intention-setting. Also yoga-style stretching and strength exercises (Fundamentals).

**Packaging:**
- Free Mini Class: 3 × ~10 min lessons.
- Fundamentals: weekly themes (stress, sleep, creativity), yoga lessons, 1:1 sessions, 1 yr of app access.
- Classic 10-Week: weekly video unlocks.
- Power of the Mind course.
- App: customizable "breathing bubble", guided cold showers and ice baths, 30-day challenges, streaks and stats.
- Instructor-led workshops and multi-day retreats with group ice baths.

Research protocols ran from 4 days (Radboud) to 21 days (UCSF), 29 days (Queensland), 7 weeks (spinal cord injury) and 8–12 weeks (axSpA, MS).

## 4. Roots and influences
- **Tibetan Tummo** ("inner fire") meditation, a Vajrayana inner-heat practice. Hof's company name, Innerfire, nods to it. The Kox 2014 protocol included "third eye meditation".
- **Hindu yoga / pranayama.** The breathing resembles kapalabhati/bhastrika-style rapid breathing followed by kumbhaka (breath retention). Kopplin & Rosenthal describe it as "pranayama-inspired".
- **Cold-water immersion traditions** (Nordic/Russian winter swimming, Kneipp hydrotherapy) and Hof's own self-experimentation. Also draws on **Selye-type stress/hormesis** ideas and positive-psychology "mindset" framing.
- **Contrasts:** Buteyko and Oxygen Advantage (Patrick McKeown) teach *reduced*, nasal, CO₂-tolerant breathing, the opposite of WHM's hyperventilation. Holotropic Breathwork (Grof) is closer in physiology but framed as a psychedelic or psychotherapeutic state.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- **No guideline endorsement**; not reimbursed by any insurer.
- Used as a research intervention in university and rehabilitation settings: Radboud UMC, Amsterdam UMC, a Dutch spinal-cord-injury rehabilitation centre, a Slovak MS clinic, UCSF Aging, Metabolism & Emotions Center, University of Queensland, Wayne State.
- Widespread in sports, military and corporate wellness, and in the commercial cold-plunge industry.
- **Professional cautions:** the American Heart Association and British Heart Foundation advise medical clearance before cold immersion. An expert quoted by the press put cold-shock arrhythmias at 1–3% in healthy young people, higher with breath-holding before immersion.

## 6. Evidence base
Trials of WHM-branded protocols, verified by DOI/PMID. All are small, unblinded and short-term, and most were in healthy volunteers.

| Study | Design / N | Key result |
|---|---|---|
| Kox et al. 2014, *PNAS* 111:7379 (doi 10.1073/pnas.1322174111) | RCT, healthy males, 12 trained (10 d, taught by Hof) vs 12 untrained; IV endotoxin 2 ng/kg | Higher epinephrine, faster and higher IL-10, lower TNF-α/IL-6/IL-8, fewer flu-like symptoms. The seminal study. |
| Zwaag et al. 2022, *Psychosom Med* (doi 10.1097/PSY.0000000000001065) | Two RCTs: N=40 (trainer and duration manipulated); N=48 (breathing vs cold vs both vs none) + endotoxin | Breathing alone drove the anti-inflammatory effect; cold alone did nothing but augmented breathing. Effects were reproducible with an independent trainer. |
| Zwaag et al. 2023, *J Pain Res* (doi 10.2147/JPR.S400408) | N=20 acute + N=48 four-arm RCT, quantitative sensory testing | Electrical pain-detection thresholds rose during breathing and 4 h after. Small pain modulation from cold training. |
| Buijze et al. 2019, *PLoS ONE* 14:e0225749 | Open-label randomized one-way crossover, axial spondyloarthritis, N=24 (19 completers), 8 wk | ESR and ASDAS-CRP/BASDAI improved within the intervention group; no serious adverse events. Proof-of-concept; no between-group test, no blinding. |
| Kopplin & Rosenthal 2022, *Curr Psychol* (doi 10.1007/s12144-022-03739-y) | 4-arm randomized trial, N=86, 2 wk | Only combined breathing + cold reduced perceived stress (medium–large effect) vs control. |
| Ketelhut et al. 2023, *Sci Rep* (doi 10.1038/s41598-023-44902-0) | RCT, N=42, 15 d | **Null.** No effects on HRV, BP, arterial stiffness, stress, affect or vitality. |
| Blades et al. 2024, *Compr Psychoneuroendocrinol* 20:100272 (UCSF/Epel lab) | RCT, N=84 women with high depressive symptoms, 21 d; WHM vs slow breathing + warm showers | Depression, anxiety and stress fell equally in both arms. WHM had a small advantage on daily rumination after stress (quadratic time × condition p=.048), which faded by the end; the authors note it would not survive multiple-comparison correction. All 6 dropouts were in WHM. |
| Epel et al. 2025, *Ann Behav Med* 59:kaaf081 (doi 10.1093/abm/kaaf081) | Pilot RCT, N=141 stressed women, 3 wk, 4 arms (WHM, HIIT, mindfulness, slow breathing + warm showers) | No group differences post-intervention. Post hoc "as treated" analysis: WHM maintained depression reductions better at 3 mo and increased daily positive affect more than control. |
| Fox, Biddell & King 2025, *Sci Rep* (doi 10.1038/s41598-025-29187-9) | Semi-randomised, N=404 healthy adults, 29 d; WHM in-person/remote vs mindfulness | Greater momentary energy, clarity and stress-handling with WHM; minimal trait differences. |
| de Groot et al. 2026, *Clin Rehabil* (doi 10.1177/02692155261428705) | RCT, chronic spinal cord injury, N=62; WHM ± cold vs usual care, 7 wk | Null on primary mental-health outcome. The cold arm improved pain interference, inspiratory flow and fasting glucose. 44% dropout in the no-cold arm. |
| Slezáková et al. 2026, *Neurol Sci* (doi 10.1007/s10072-026-08895-8) | Randomized pilot, MS, N=60 (43 analysed); WHM vs lifestyle vs control, 12 wk | WHM and lifestyle both lowered IL-17A/IL-18. WHM alone lowered IFN-γ. No change in NfL/GFAP. |

**Mechanistic and case studies** (not efficacy evidence):
- Kox 2012 (Hof, n=1, endotoxin)
- Muzik, Reilly & Diwadkar 2018, *NeuroImage* (Hof, n=1, fMRI/PET)
- Vosselman 2014 (twin case)
- Zwaag 2020 *Metabolites*
- Citherlet 2021 (sprint pilot: no benefit; ~1/3 reported side effects)
- Marko 2022 (breathing economy)
- Petraskova Touskova 2022 (Antarctic cohort)

**Systematic review:** Almahayni & Hammond 2024, *PLoS ONE* (doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0286933). It covered 9 papers / 8 trials, N=15–48 each, 86% male. Possible anti-inflammatory effect via epinephrine; mixed results on performance. Most RCTs were rated "high concerns" for risk of bias, and overall quality "very low".

**Gaps:**
- No trials in ME/CFS, long COVID, fibromyalgia, IBS, POTS, FND or chronic pain populations.
- Lab immune findings involve an acute, artificial endotoxin challenge.
- Clinical trials with active controls (slow breathing, mindfulness, HIIT) mostly show **no superiority**.
- Early Radboud trials used Hof as trainer; later work showed effects do not depend on him.
- The site's science page mixes peer-reviewed RCTs with blogs, n=1 studies and an expedition report, and overstates some results.

- **2014:** PNAS paper gets global coverage; iOS app launched in Sept.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **Drowning and deaths.** Shallow-water blackout risk from hyperventilation followed by breath-hold underwater. Carney's 2023 documentary linked 13 deaths to the method, and his public spreadsheet of deaths and incident reports listed 32 alleged deaths as of March 2024 (alleged; causation mostly unestablished). Four drownings in 2015–16 prompted family warnings. A US$67M wrongful-death suit (Metzger, 2022, over a 17-year-old's pool drowning) was dismissed in July 2024 on causation. The company later strengthened its "never in water" warnings.
- **Overclaiming.** Hof's statements on RA, MS and cancer were disowned by his own lead researchers. He told media the study showed his body "neutralized bacteria", which it did not. The site's "backed by science" framing leans on n=1 studies.
- **Personal conduct.** In 2012 Hof was convicted of assaulting the 18-year-old son of his then-partner (40 h community service and a €350 fine). A *de Volkskrant* investigation (28 Sept 2024) reported allegations of longer-term abuse by his ex-partner and children, which Hof denies; a planned biopic was put on hold.
- **Physiology critiques.** Hyperventilation causes hypocapnia and cerebral vasoconstriction. Cold-shock and autonomic-conflict arrhythmias occur with cold immersion plus breath-holding. AHA and BHF cautions apply. Adverse effects (tinnitus, light-headedness, "heaviness") are reported in trials.

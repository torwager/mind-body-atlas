*Compiled 2026-10-12. ## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
- **Sensorimotor learning and self-image.** Feldenkrais held that action is organised by a person's "self-image," made up of movement, sensation, feeling and thought. Habitual, inefficient or protective patterns are learned, so they can be unlearned by improving awareness and differentiation of movement. Pain and dysfunction are read largely as products of poorly organised habitual action, such as excess effort, parasitic co-contraction or protective guarding, more than as fixed structural damage.
- **Neuroplasticity framing.** Modern proponents describe it as harnessing brain plasticity: refining the body schema and somatosensory maps, strengthening motor cortex–body connections, and building interoceptive awareness. Doidge (2015) popularised this view. Wikipedia summarises proponents' claim that it repairs impaired motor cortex–body connections.
- **Threat reduction and the nervous system.** Lessons use slow, small, effortless, non-painful movements and pay attention to differences. The proposed result is less protective muscle guarding and a lower sense of threat. Feldenkrais tied anxiety to flexor (protective) postural patterns and a fear of falling (*Body and Mature Behavior*, 1949). This links to present-day fear-avoidance and central-sensitisation accounts, but the method does not itself make central sensitisation its framework.
- **Learning over correction.** Hillier & Worley (2015) found the effects generic across conditions. They read this as consistent with a learning-based mechanism rather than a disease-specific one.

## 2. One mechanism or many?
**Transdiagnostic, single framework.** The method has no disease taxonomy. All presentations are approached as problems of how the person organises action, attention and self-image. Practitioners adapt lesson content to the presenting complaint (e.g. lessons for the spine, jaw, breathing or eyes), but the mechanism is the same. It makes **no claims specific to ME/CFS, POTS, MCS, EHS, Lyme or mould**. It is closer to a general somatic-education practice than a disorder-targeted program.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
- **ATM lessons.** Feldenkrais created a large corpus of ATM lessons. The Alexander Yanai collection (recorded weekly Tel Aviv classes, early 1950s to late 1970s) is published in numbered volumes that run past lesson #200. Total counts (often given as "over 1,000") have no published source; the IFF catalogue and lesson index pages were checked but are not machine-readable. Each lesson is a verbally guided sequence of slow, small movements, often lying down, varied systematically. It asks the learner to:
  - attend to how the movement is done and to sensation, rather than stretch or strengthen;
  - reduce effort ("do less") and rest often;
  - stay out of pain;
  - differentiate body parts, e.g. move head and eyes separately;
  - compare sides, e.g. do one side, then sense how the two sides differ;
  - imagine movements as well as perform them.

  Typical dose: 45–60 min, weekly in class or several times a week at home. Trial doses ranged from 1 × 2 h per week for 12 weeks (Lundqvist 2014) to 2× per week for 5 weeks (Ahmadi 2020).
- **FI sessions.** The practitioner uses gentle touch and passive or assisted movement to "communicate" new movement options. Sessions last 30–60 min. A course is often 5–10 sessions, or open-ended.
- **Principles practitioners teach.** Learning as a process; posture as dynamic equilibrium ("acture"); exploratory rather than performance movement; working on the whole before the parts; repetition with variation; curiosity and comfort over achievement.
- **Self-care.** Recorded ATM lessons, books (*Awareness Through Movement*, 1972), and transfer of awareness to daily activities (sitting, walking, computer work, instruments, sport).
- **What it does not include.** No diet, supplements, pacing protocol or explicit psychological or emotional processing. Its work with anxiety and emotion goes through posture and breath.

## 4. Roots and influences
- **Judo and martial arts.** Feldenkrais studied with Jigoro Kano's school, co-founded the Jiu-Jitsu Club de France, and wrote early judo books. He took from judo its ideas of balance, effortless efficiency and the centre of gravity.
- **Émile Coué / autosuggestion.** In 1929 Feldenkrais published a Hebrew translation of Coué's *The Practice of Autosuggestion*, adding two chapters of his own (later republished as *Thinking and Doing*).
- **Physics, engineering and mechanics**, along with cybernetics and early neurophysiology (Sherrington, Pavlov-era reflex physiology), and Freudian psychoanalysis. He discussed anxiety and sexuality in *Body and Mature Behavior*.
- **Somatic education contemporaries.** He was aware of F.M. Alexander's technique, He also studied the work of Elsa Gindler and travelled to Switzerland to study with Heinrich Jacoby (Wikipedia). Feldenkrais is a core strand of the 1970s "somatics" movement (Thomas Hanna, Esalen).
- **Downstream influence.** Hanna Somatics, Anat Baniel Method (NeuroMovement), Jeremy Krauss's Feldenkrais-based approaches, Basic Body Awareness Therapy cross-talk in Scandinavia, and elements of modern pain-science movement therapy and dance/movement pedagogy.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- **Clinical use.** Used inside some physiotherapy, occupational therapy and rehabilitation settings. Buchanan 2014 found that 36.9% of US Guild teachers also held conventional healthcare licences and about a fifth of clients were referred by conventional providers. Group Feldenkrais has been run within Swedish multidisciplinary chronic-pain programs (Öhman 2011, qualitative). It is common in performing-arts schools.
- **Guidelines and HTA.** No major pain or rehabilitation guideline recommends it specifically.
  - **Australia:** the 2015 Department of Health review of natural therapies found no clear evidence of effectiveness. The method was then removed from private health insurance rebate eligibility (announced 2017, effective April 2019).
  - **Germany (IQWiG HTA HT20-05, 2023):** 6 RCTs across 5 indications, all at high risk of bias. Hints of benefit in 2 indications: Parkinson's disease (vs lecture-based education) and chronic low back pain (vs trunk-stabilisation education at end of treatment; vs Back School, a hint of more pain reduction but less QoL benefit at 3 months). Overall CLBP evidence inconsistent; no hint of long-term benefit; no harms data. The report also flags an ethical concern that trademark protection may lead users to expect benefits the evidence does not support.
- **Insurance (US).** Not a recognised reimbursable service in its own right. Indirect reimbursement is possible when a licensed PT delivers Feldenkrais-informed exercise under standard CPT codes. Some HSA/FSA use is possible with a letter of medical necessity.

## 6. Evidence base
Only studies verified in PubMed are listed. All are trials of the **Feldenkrais Method itself**, which has no branded sub-program.

**Systematic reviews and HTA**
- **Hillier S, Worley A. 2015**, *Evid Based Complement Alternat Med* (PMID 25949266). Systematic review of 20 RCTs. In 7 studies of older adults, balance improved: TUG MD −1.14 s, Functional Reach +6.08 cm. Single studies reported less perceived effort, better body image and comfort. Risk of bias was high and effects were generic.
- **Berland R et al. 2022**, *IJERPH* (PMID 36360614). Systematic review and meta-analysis of 16 RCTs. TUG d = −1.14 (95% CI −1.78 to −0.49; same point estimate and CI as Hillier & Worley's TUG mean difference, so the pooled data likely overlap). No effect on MS self-efficacy. Authors conclude effects in spine pain are comparable to other physiotherapy techniques.
- **IQWiG HTA HT20-05, 2023** (PMID 36780406; NBK589183). Six RCTs (5 indications), all at high risk of bias; nearly all evidence is from group ATM, not FI. Parkinson's: hint of benefit for mobility and QoL versus lecture education. Chronic low back pain: inconsistent (hints in both directions depending on comparator and time point). No long-term benefit shown. Cost €10–20 per group and €60–90 per individual session.
- **Martin S et al. 2024**, *Complement Ther Med* (PMID 39151592). Systematic review of the method in psychiatric care (14 articles). Possible benefits for body awareness and emotional regulation. Little direct psychiatric evidence.

**Pain-relevant trials**
- **Ahmadi H et al. 2020**, *Clin Rehabil* (PMID 32723088). Single-blind RCT, N = 60, chronic non-specific low back pain. Comparator: home core-stability exercise plus education; Feldenkrais given 2×/week for 5 weeks. Feldenkrais was better for QoL, interoceptive awareness (MAIA) and disability. **Pain improved equally in both arms** (no between-group difference).
- **Paolucci T et al. 2017**, *Disabil Rehabil* (PMID 27215948). Single-blind RCT, N = 53, chronic low back pain, versus Back School. No between-group difference in pain; both arms improved in pain and disability.
- **Lundqvist LO et al. 2014**, *Arch Phys Med Rehabil* (PMID 24907640). RCT, N = 61 visually impaired adults with chronic neck/scapular pain, versus untreated control. Feldenkrais was 2 h/week for 12 weeks. Pain was lower on VAS at post-treatment and at 1 year. No difference on SF-36 bodily pain.
- **Malmgren-Olsson EB, Bränholm IB 2002**, *Disabil Rehabil* (PMID 12017464). Three-arm comparison (Body Awareness Therapy vs Feldenkrais vs conventional physiotherapy), N = 78, non-specific musculoskeletal disorders. Patients were recruited consecutively to the three groups, so this was **not a randomised trial**. Effect sizes were somewhat larger for BAT and Feldenkrais, with pain self-efficacy maintained at 1 year. Few between-group differences.
- **Giorgi V et al. 2023**, *J Bodyw Mov Ther* (PMID 37949579). Uncontrolled proof-of-concept study, N = 128 with fibromyalgia, group ATM. Polysymptomatic distress and pain catastrophising improved; the FIQR change was not significant. **This is the only fibromyalgia-specific study found, and it is not an RCT.**
- **Saki F et al. 2025**, *BMC Geriatr* (PMID 40745630), and **Ziya M et al. 2026**, *Geriatr Nurs* (PMID 41633229). RCT and comparative studies of Feldenkrais combined with dynamic neuromuscular stabilisation in older women with low back pain (Iranian group).

**Other indications (not this review's target conditions)**
- **Balance and falls in older adults:** Vrantsidis 2009 (PMID 19299839); Ullmann 2010 (PMID 20070145).
- **Parkinson's disease:** Teixeira-Machado 2015 (PMID 25599428).
- **Multiple sclerosis:** Johnson 1999 (PMID 10381247).
- **Diabetic neuropathy balance:** Jimenez-Mazuelas 2024 (PMID 39593566); Elshinnawy 2026 (PMID 42177654).
- **Intellectual disability balance:** Torres-Unda 2017 (PMID 28923375).
- **Eating disorders:** Laumer 1997 (PMID 9265198, German).

**Gaps and critiques**
- Trials are small, usually single-site, and often run by Feldenkrais practitioners.
- Blinding is not possible, and active comparators are rare or weak. Mehling 2005 (PMID 15865501) discusses bias control in bodywork trials.
- Long-term follow-up is rare.
- There are no trials in ME/CFS, long COVID, POTS, IBS, MCS, FND or CRPS.
- Where it is compared with active exercise, pain outcomes are generally equivalent.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory
- **1949–1960s.** Books and Tel Aviv classes. International attention through Ben-Gurion and Peter Brook/theatre circles.
- **1970s–1980s growth.**
  - *Awareness Through Movement* (Harper & Row, 1972).
  - Esalen and the US human-potential movement.
  - San Francisco training (1975–78, 65 teachers trained) and Amherst training (235 students began in 1980; ended 1983 per FGNA). FGNA says about 300 practitioners were trained in total across Feldenkrais's three trainings.
  - Feldenkrais Guild established by Feldenkrais in 1977 (renamed FGNA in 1997).
  - Feldenkrais died in 1984.
- **1990s–2000s.** Steady expansion through accredited training programs in North America, Europe, Israel, Australia and Japan. IFF founded in Paris in 1992. Peak academic-review interest came around 2004–2006 (several *Phys Med Rehabil Clin N Am* overviews).
- **2014.** About 1,287 US Guild-certified teachers (Buchanan 2014). Most worked part-time and saw fewer than 10 individual and fewer than 10 group students per week. The main reason students came was pain.
- **2015.** Norman Doidge's *The Brain's Way of Healing* (NYT bestseller) gave the method a long chapter in a neuroplasticity frame, which likely boosted interest. The Australian negative review came the same year.
- **2020–2021.** COVID moved ATM classes online, through Zoom classes and recorded-lesson subscription platforms, increasing reach.
- **2020s.** Steady stream of RCTs, including from Iran and Spain. IQWiG HTA in 2023.
- **Online presence.** Many ATM lessons on YouTube. An r/Feldenkrais subreddit has existed since at least 2014 (Wayback capture, July 2014). Its member count is unknown: Reddit's about.json, api.reddit.com and third-party stats sites blocked or returned nothing, and the Wayback CDX service was offline on 2026-10-13. Google Trends was not checked (no machine-readable access).
- **Overall.** Interest is long and steady and has stayed niche relative to yoga or mindfulness.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **Sceptics.** Quackwatch lists it among "unnaturalistic methods." David Gorski has called it "glorified yoga" bordering on quackery and compared FI to faith healing.
- **Overreach.** Proponents' claims for autism, cerebral palsy and MS outrun the evidence.
- **Overall verdict.** The Australian (2015) and German (IQWiG 2023, published Feb 2023) government reviews found no clear evidence, or only weak hints from high-risk-of-bias trials.
- **Theory.** The neuroplasticity explanations are post-hoc and untested. No mechanistic study shows that cortical map change mediates clinical benefit.
- **For a fair reading.** The method makes fairly modest, non-disease-specific claims compared with limbic-retraining programs. It does not promise cures for ME/CFS-type illness.

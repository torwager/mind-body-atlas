## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
- **Three pain types (PainGuide "About Pain"):**
  - **Nociceptive:** tissue damage or inflammation; a protective warning signal.
  - **Neuropathic:** damage to peripheral or central nerves.
  - **Nociplastic / "centrally augmented":** the CNS amplifies minimal or non-nociceptive input. The site stresses that this pain is as real as pain from injury. Fibromyalgia, much low back pain and some headaches are its examples.
- **Central amplification model (Clauw/Williams).** Their academic work frames fibromyalgia as the prototype of a centralized-pain phenotype. That phenotype is described by:
  - augmented sensory processing, i.e. a "volume control" or gain set too high;
  - reduced descending inhibition;
  - altered neurotransmitter and connectivity profiles, such as insular glutamate and default-mode/insula connectivity.

  Key sources: Williams & Clauw, J Pain 2009; Clauw, JAMA 2014; Fitzcharles, Cohen & Clauw, Lancet 2021.
- **Contributors and triggers.** PainGuide lists genetics, infections, hormonal abnormalities, physical or psychological trauma, repetitive injury and sustained stress as factors that can produce central augmentation.
- **Behavioural maintenance (the CBT layer).** Deconditioning, boom-bust activity patterns, poor sleep, negative automatic thoughts, emotional distress and poor communication are presented as factors that worsen and maintain pain. These are the targets of the self-management modules.
- **Not a psychogenic model.** Unlike Sarno-, PRT- or DNRS-style programs, it does not claim the pain is a learned danger signal that can be "unlearned" for cure. The aim is management and functional improvement, used alongside medication and other professional care. It makes no claims about the autonomic nervous system or the limbic system.

## 2. One mechanism or many?
**Mostly unified, with condition-specific emphasis.**
- **Shared mechanism, stated cautiously.** The COPC pages say these conditions co-occur and "may share" a common mechanism or underlying causes. Clauw's group is a main source of the COPC/nociplastic framework that other programs on this list borrow (Maixner, Fillingim, Williams et al., J Pain 2016).
- **Explicit differentiation, unlike most commercial programs.** Each condition, and each patient, can have nociceptive, neuropathic, nociplastic or mixed mechanisms. Osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, MS, sickle-cell and cancer pain get separate pages with disease-specific professional care, and there is an explicit diagnostic step before self-care.
- **Same skill set for everyone.** The self-management toolkit is essentially identical across conditions, i.e. transdiagnostic CBT.
- **Out of scope:** ME/CFS beyond its COPC listing, POTS, MCS/EHS, "chronic Lyme" and mould illness are not addressed as treatment targets.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
**Original "Living Well with Fibromyalgia" (2010 trial).** 13 modules in three segments:
- **Segments:** (a) FM education (what FM is, causes, multimodal treatment); (b) symptom-management skills; (c) lifestyle-change skills.
- **Each module contained:** a clinician video lecture, a written summary, homework and self-monitoring forms, and extras such as audio relaxation.
- **Skills:** aerobic exercise and pacing, relaxation, pleasant-activity scheduling, sleep hygiene, problem solving, reframing (cognitive restructuring), goal setting, communication, and maintenance.
- **Dose:** self-paced over 6 months, used alongside standard medical care, with no therapist contact.

**Current PainGuide self-care modules:**
- Exercise.
- Pacing.
- Nutrition and supplements (the evidence is labelled as emerging).
- Relaxation (regular practice is stressed).
- Reframing of automatic negative thoughts.
- Resilience: identifying personal strengths. This reflects Hassett's positive-psychology work.
- Managing emotions: stress management and pleasant activities.
- Communication skills with family, clinicians and employers.
- Sleep.
- Self-acupressure.
- Spirituality.
- Ergonomics and posture.

**Other components:**
- "Professional Care" pages (medications, therapies, devices, procedures) give balanced information on drug and non-drug treatments.
- The symptom & activity tracker at app.painguide.com supports self-monitoring and reinforcement of helpful behaviours.

**Structured research versions:**
- **PRISM-CBT (Hassett 2026):**
  - Modules: activity and pacing, sleep, relaxation, reframing, maintenance, goal setting and communication.
  - Format: one per week for 8 weeks.
  - Coaching: medical-assistant coaching, one face-to-face meeting of under 30 minutes, then weekly calls of about 15 minutes.
  - Add-ons in the experimental arm: resilience exercises from positive psychology.
- **Tailored PainGuide (NCT05843890):**
  - One module per week for 4 weeks.
  - Messaging tailored to PROMIS-29+2 scores.
  - A Fitbit for step counts.
- **BEST "enhanced self-care" arm:** 12 weeks of self-paced PainGuide-derived modules with no clinician.

## 4. Roots and influences
- **CBT for chronic pain:** Beck/Ellis cognitive restructuring; Fordyce operant/behavioural pain management; Turk and Holroyd-style coping-skills training. The website is a direct translation of Williams's face-to-face 6-session FM CBT protocol (J Rheumatol 2002).
- **Exercise and activity pacing:** rheumatology and physiotherapy tradition.
- **Relaxation:** progressive muscle relaxation and Relaxation Response–style methods.
- **Positive psychology and resilience:** Hassett.
- **Acupressure:** draws on traditional Chinese medicine. U-M has run separate acupressure RCTs (Harris, Murphy).
- **Pain science:** gate-control/neuromatrix pain science and the fibromyalgia "central sensitization" literature (Yunus, Woolf). Clauw's own centralized/nociplastic pain construct was later formalized under the IASP's 2016–17 "nociplastic" term.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- **Academic home.** Fully academic, created and hosted by a major U.S. medical school and used in U-M's own clinical services.
- **NIH HEAL Back Pain Consortium (BACPAC):**
  - It is the self-care arm of the multisite BEST SMART trial (NCT05396014): 1,019 enrolled per the 2025 papers (1,014 on the registry) and 805 randomized (205 to self-care); the BEST design paper (Mauck et al. 2025) confirms the ESC modules are labelled "PainGuide", with enhanced self-care compared against ACT, exercise/manual therapy and duloxetine.
  - It is the 4-week run-in for the U-M interventional-response phenotyping SMART. The protocol (Hassett et al., Pain Med 2023) expected 10–20% to respond to PainGuide alone.
- **Derivatives:**
  - PROSPECT (chemotherapy-induced neuropathy), adapted from FibroGuide.
  - The U-M breast-cancer symptom self-management site (Henry et al. 2018).
  - Pain EASE for veterans (VA/U-M collaboration, with Williams as co-author). This is a related but separate program.
- **Guidelines and listings:**
  - Professional societies recommend it to patients; for example, the International Pelvic Pain Society lists it as a resource.
  - It is not itself named in NICE, ACR, EULAR or CDC guidelines, although those guidelines recommend the CBT and exercise content it delivers.
- **Insurance:**
  - There is no need for coverage because it is free.
  - The PRISM-CBT model, with medical assistants coaching CBT, was designed to fit within routine clinic workflows.
  - No payer policy specific to PainGuide was found.

## 6. Evidence base
Branded program, or its direct predecessor/successor website:
1. **Williams DA, Kuper D, Segar M, Mohan N, Sheth M, Clauw DJ. Internet-enhanced management of fibromyalgia: a randomized controlled trial. *Pain* 2010;151(3):694–702** (PMID 20855168).
   - Design: standard care alone vs standard care plus the "Living Well with Fibromyalgia" website (called WEB-SM in the paper; precursor of FibroGuide). N = 118, mostly rural patients recruited via the Avera telemedicine network (Sioux Falls, South Dakota), 6-month follow-up, ITT analysis, 10.2% attrition.
   - Pain and physical function: significantly better in the web arm. 30% pain responders were 29% vs 8% (NNT ≈ 5).
   - Global improvement (PGIC; available for only 68 of 118): 20/35 (57%) vs 7/33 (21%) improved. Physical-function responders 31% vs 6% (NNT 5).
   - Other secondary outcomes: no significant differences.
   - Limitations: no attention control; developers ran the trial.
2. **Sparks T, Kawi J, Menzel NN, Hartley K. Implementation of health information technology in routine care for fibromyalgia: pilot study. *Pain Manag Nurs* 2016;17(1):54–62** (PMID 26777125).
   - Design: single-group pre/post pilot of FibroGuide (10 modules) in an APRN clinic, N = 35, 12 weeks.
   - Results: FIQ impact improved (p = .017); most found it helpful, though many preferred talking to a provider.
   - Independence: an independent (UNLV) team, but uncontrolled.
3. **Hassett AL, Voepel-Lewis T, Zhu G, … Brummett CM, Clauw DJ, Williams DA. Randomized controlled trial of medical assistant-coached behavioral intervention for chronic pain. *Reg Anesth Pain Med* 2026 (online Mar 6; PMID 41791834).**
   - Design: adults with spinal pain plus fibromyalgia-type symptoms, randomized to PRISM-CBT (n = 119), standard CBT (n = 120) or usual care (n = 60). Both CBT arms used FibroGuide modules.
   - Primary outcome (FIQR at 8 weeks): no difference between PRISM-CBT and usual care.
   - 12 months: PRISM-CBT was 7.4 points better than usual care (p = .045).
   - Pain interference (secondary): PRISM-CBT was 0.88 points lower than usual care on the BPI at 8 weeks and 6 months, and 1.42 points lower at 12 months; it also beat standard CBT on interference.
   - The abstract reports no significant advantage of the standard FibroGuide-CBT arm over usual care on the primary outcome.
   - Interpretation: modest, delayed effects of the resilience-enhanced version; the primary endpoint was null. Registered as NCT03304613.

Adapted derivative websites (same platform and content, different populations):
4. **Knoerl R, Smith EML, Barton DL, Williams DA, et al. Self-guided online cognitive behavioral strategies for chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy: a multicenter, pilot, randomized, wait-list controlled trial. *J Pain* 2018;19(4):382–394** (PMID 29229430).
   - Design: PROSPECT (adapted from FibroGuide) vs usual care, N = 60 (analysed n = 38), 8 weeks.
   - Results: "worst" pain improved (p = .046, d = 0.58); no significant secondary effects. A mediation follow-up found no significant mediators.
5. **Henry NL, Kidwell KM, … Clauw DJ, Smith EML, Williams DA. Pilot study of an internet-based self-management program for symptom control in patients with early-stage breast cancer. *JCO Clin Cancer Inform* 2018;2:1–12** (PMID 30652562).
   - Design: single arm, N = 50, 8 weeks.
   - Results: significant PROMIS improvements in anxiety, sleep, fatigue, activity and pain; 63% reported improvement in their primary symptom.

Antecedent (face-to-face, not the website):
6. **Williams DA, Cary MA, Groner KH, et al. Improving physical functional status in patients with fibromyalgia: a brief cognitive behavioral intervention. *J Rheumatol* 2002;29:1280–6** (PMID 12064847).
   - Design: N = 145, 6-session CBT plus standard care vs standard care.
   - Results: 25% vs 12% reached clinically meaningful 1-year functional improvement; no lasting pain difference.

Ongoing or unpublished:
- **BEST trial (NCT05396014):** completed 2024; results posted to the registry in July 2025. Design and baseline papers appeared in Pain Med 2025; no peer-reviewed primary-outcome paper was located. **Registry-posted primary outcome (PEG change score, LS mean at 24 weeks, higher = worse):** ESC/PainGuide 4.72 (95% CI 4.30–5.14; n = 205) vs ACT 4.16, duloxetine 3.86, exercise/manual therapy 3.57 (3.15–3.99). So the PainGuide-based self-care arm did numerically worst and its CI does not overlap that of exercise/manual therapy. These are unpublished, un-peer-reviewed registry data; interpret cautiously.
- **Tailored PainGuide RCT (NCT05843890):** target n = 550, PI Clauw, recruiting since Aug 2023; primary completion expected Feb 2028. It compares structured/tailored PainGuide with standard PainGuide, so it has no non-PainGuide control.
- **U-M interventional-response phenotyping SMART:** protocol published in Pain Med 2023.

**Gaps and critiques:**
- Only one adequately controlled efficacy RCT of the website, and it is 16 years old.
- No attention or placebo-education control.
- All controlled efficacy trials of the branded content come from the developers' group.
- In the largest head-to-head comparison so far (BEST, registry data only), PainGuide-based self-care looked the weakest of four first-line options for chronic low back pain.
- Effects are modest, typical of internet CBT for FM (cf. the e-health systematic reviews).
- Low engagement is a general problem with unguided web programs; for example, Pain EASE users accessed fewer than half of the modules recommended to them.
- No trial of the current public PainGuide as used without any study structure.
- Note that the PROSPER-FM digital-ACT trial (Lancet 2024), on which Williams is co-author, tested a separate commercial app (Swing Therapeutics' Stanza) and is **not** evidence for PainGuide.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory
- **2002–2010:** research phase. Face-to-face protocol (2002), NIAMS/DoD-funded web trial, *Pain* 2010 publication.
- **c. 2010–2016:** FibroGuide offered free to the public. Clauw promoted it in lectures as matching approved FM drugs in effect size for function. It was used in at least one independent nurse-practitioner implementation (2015) and listed by patient-resource directories. One UNLV thesis describes an interactive DVD-ROM version, which suggests several formats existed.
- **c. 2019–2022:** PainGuide relaunch, broadened to all chronic pain and COPCs, with a BACPAC-specific mirror (© 2022). Adopted as the self-care backbone of NIH HEAL back-pain trials.
- **2023–2026:**
  - Tailored PainGuide RCT recruiting (2023–).
  - PRISM-CBT RCT published (2026).
  - FibroGuide URL redirected to the PainGuide brand.
  - Site maintained (© 2025 footer).
- **Public footprint is small:**
  - No Reddit threads were found discussing PainGuide or FibroGuide.
  - No consumer app-store listing with substantial downloads was found.
  - No published usage statistics.
  - Its reach is via clinicians, trial enrolment (several thousand participants across trials) and Clauw's lectures and grand rounds.
- **Indirect influence is large.** The scientific framework behind it (nociplastic pain and COPCs; Clauw JAMA 2014, Lancet 2021) is widely cited and borrowed by commercial programs.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **Little controversy about the program itself.** It is non-commercial, avoids curative claims and offers conventional CBT and exercise content.
- **Criticisms apply to the underlying construct and to unguided e-health in general:**
  - The "centralized/nociplastic" label is criticized as a diagnosis by exclusion that lacks a validated biomarker. Some patient advocates feel it risks psychologizing illness or dismissing peripheral pathology, e.g. small-fibre neuropathy in a subset of FM patients.
  - Effect sizes are modest.
  - Engagement and adherence are low without coaching.
  - The developers have conducted nearly all the trials, and some disclose royalties from a commercial digital pain self-management tool (Tonix) and consultancies with digital-therapeutics firms.
  - Registry-posted BEST data suggest the unguided PainGuide self-care arm did worst among four active first-line treatments for chronic low back pain.

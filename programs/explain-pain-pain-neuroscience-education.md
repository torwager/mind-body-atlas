## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
- **Pain is an output of the brain, not an input from tissue.** Pain is a protective perception, constructed when the brain evaluates "credible evidence of danger" to body tissue. It is not a readout of tissue damage. The NOI book frames this as "pain is a protector" and "pain is not an accurate measure of tissue health."
- **Bioplasticity and sensitisation.** In persistent pain the nervous system becomes overprotective. Peripheral and central sensitisation, upregulated danger receptors, spinal amplification and altered cortical body maps (the neuromatrix) all increase protective output. Immune and endocrine systems are also part of the protective response.
- **Danger in Me (DIMs) vs Safety in Me (SIMs).** The *Protectometer* metaphor. Any input that the brain reads as danger (a thought, belief, context, sight, word, place, movement or tissue signal) pushes toward pain. Safety cues push away from it. Pain occurs when DIMs outweigh SIMs.
- **Learning and conditioning.** The longer pain persists, the better the system becomes at producing it. Pain can be cued by contexts and movements and can spread, as stated on Tame the Beast.
- **Cognitive and behavioural maintainers.** Threatening beliefs about tissue damage, catastrophizing and fear of movement sustain the system's sensitivity. Reconceptualising pain, i.e. changing what pain is believed to mean, is the proposed active ingredient. Moseley ties this to conceptual-change learning theory.
- **Tissue is not dismissed.** Nociception and tissue pathology are acknowledged as inputs. The emphasis is that, in chronic pain, they are poor predictors of how much it hurts.

## 2. One mechanism or many?
**Mostly unified, with some condition-specific emphasis.** The same model (a protective, sensitised, plastic nervous system evaluating threat) is applied to all persistent pain. Clinicians are taught to tailor examples and "target concepts" to the condition and the patient. *Explain Pain Supercharged* includes four targeted curricula. Specific emphases:
- **CRPS and phantom limb:** distorted cortical body maps and motor imagery, handled by the separate GMI program.
- **Fibromyalgia and chronic fatigue:** central sensitisation and impaired endogenous inhibition (the Nijs/Meeus Belgian line).
- **Pelvic pain:** threat and context.

Unlike ANS Rewire or DNRS, PNE does not claim that ME/CFS, MCS, EHS or POTS are a single brain disorder. Its scope is pain, with fatigue a modest extension. Louw's TNE adds nerve-specific content (e.g. "sensitive nerves," ion-channel turnover) for radiculopathy and surgery.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
- **Education sessions.** Classic trial dose: 1–2 sessions of about 1–3 h, or 4–6 shorter sessions. Delivered with metaphors (alarm system, "the beast," Protectometer), drawings, neuroscience "nuggets" and stories. Content covers:
  - nociception vs pain
  - sensitisation and nerve "alarm" sensitivity
  - the brain's evaluation of threat
  - the role of beliefs and context
  - bioplasticity
  - the fact that hurt does not equal harm
- **Protectometer exercise (self-care).** The patient lists personal DIMs and SIMs, then reduces DIMs and increases SIMs (understanding, safe movement, social support, sleep, valued activities).
- **Graded exposure and pacing.** "Twin peaks" model: the protective pain threshold sits below the actual tissue-damage threshold. Patients make small, regular increases in activity ("baselines, then nudge up") and use flare-up plans.
- **Combined with active therapy.** Most evidence pairs PNE with exercise or motor-control training: Malfliet's "cognition-targeted motor control training" and Louw's education plus movement. The RESOLVE trial (Bagg 2022) packaged it with graded sensorimotor retraining.
- **Body-map work.** Left/right discrimination, imagined movements and mirror therapy, in the GMI entry.
- **Pre-operative TNE.** One session before spinal or knee surgery (Louw).
- **Public campaigns.** Short videos (Tame the Beast, ~5 min). Flippin' Pain's "six key messages," podcasts and worksheets. Pain Revolution's bike-tour community talks and trained Local Pain Educators.

## 4. Roots and influences
- Melzack & Wall's **gate control theory** (1965) and Melzack's **neuromatrix theory** (1990s), which is the conceptual core.
- Wall's and Woolf's work on **central sensitisation** (1980s).
- Butler and Shacklock's **neurodynamics** (nervous-system mobilisation, 1990s).
- **Fear-avoidance models** (Vlaeyen & Linton) and **CBT** for pain.
- **Educational psychology**: conceptual change theory.
- **Cortical-map neuroscience** (Ramachandran's mirror therapy; Flor's work on cortical reorganisation).
- Later, **predictive processing / Bayesian brain** accounts.
- Not drawn from contemplative traditions.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- Widely taught in entry-level physiotherapy programs and continuing education. EIM's Therapeutic Pain Specialist certification in the US is built on Louw's TNE. Used in multidisciplinary pain programs, in the UK NHS (Flippin' Pain partners include NHS bodies and the Scottish Government, with endorsement from the British Pain Society and EFIC), and in Australian public health.
- Pain Revolution is an initiative of Adelaide University (formerly UniSA), funded by donations and sponsorship.
- Guidelines (NICE NG59 for low back pain; APTA/JOSPT neck and back guidelines; Lancet LBP series 2018) recommend patient education and reassurance, often citing pain-science education. NICE NG193 (chronic primary pain, 2021) does not name PNE specifically.
- **Insurance.** No separate reimbursement code for PNE. It is reimbursed as part of covered physiotherapy or pain-management visits.

## 6. Evidence base
*Trials of PNE generally. Many are by the originators (Moseley, Louw, Nijs/Meeus), so independence is limited. Trials of branded NOI materials and of "generic PNE" overlap heavily.*

| Study | Design / N | Key result |
|---|---|---|
| Moseley 2002, *Aust J Physiother* 48(4):297–302 | RCT, chronic LBP, N=57 (49 analysed) | A 4-week combined program (manual therapy, exercise and neurophysiology education) beat GP-directed care: pain −1.5/10, RMDQ −3.9. Effect maintained at 1 yr. Education is not isolated here. |
| Moseley, Nicholas & Hodges 2004, *Clin J Pain* 20:324 | RCT, chronic LBP, N=58 | A single intensive neurophysiology education session vs back anatomy/physiology education improved pain attitudes, catastrophizing, straight-leg raise and forward bending. The effect on disability (RMDQ) was statistically significant but small. Pain intensity was not a primary outcome. |
| Meeus et al. 2010, *Arch Phys Med Rehabil* 91:1153 | Double-blind RCT, chronic fatigue syndrome, N=48 | One session of pain physiology education vs pacing/self-management education, assessed immediately after the session. Improved neurophysiology-of-pain knowledge and reduced rumination (PCS subscale). Effects on pain thresholds were not supported; there was no longer-term follow-up. |
| Van Oosterwijck et al. 2013, *Clin J Pain* 29(10):873–882 | Double-blind RCT, fibromyalgia, N=30 | Pain physiology education vs pacing self-management education improved SF-36 domains, endogenous pain inhibition and pain scores at 3 mo. Small trial by the developers' group. |
| Louw et al. 2014, *Spine* 39:1449 | RCT, pre-op lumbar radiculopathy, N=67 | One PNE session plus booklet added to usual care. No difference in back pain, leg pain or function at 1 yr. Better surgical experience and 45% lower post-op healthcare expenditure (reported in this 2014 paper). The 3-yr follow-up (Louw 2016, *J Spine Surg*) again found no difference in patient-reported outcomes. |
| Malfliet et al. 2018, *JAMA Neurology* 75:808 | RCT, chronic spinal pain, N=120 | PNE plus cognition-targeted motor control training vs best-evidence physio (back/neck education plus general exercise). Small-to-medium benefits on Central Sensitization Inventory, disability (3–12 mo) and SF-36. The pressure-pain-threshold effect was borderline (95% CI crossed 0). No gray-matter changes. |
| Traeger et al. 2019, *JAMA Neurology* 76:161 (PREVENT) | RCT, acute LBP at high risk of chronicity, N=202 | Two 1-h sessions of pain-biology and self-management education vs placebo education (active listening), on top of first-line care: **no effect** on 3-mo pain (MD −0.3/10). Small disability benefit at 1 wk and 3 mo, not at 6 or 12 mo. |
| Bagg et al. 2022, *JAMA* 328:430 (RESOLVE) | RCT, chronic LBP, N=276 | 12 weekly sessions of graded sensorimotor retraining incorporating pain education vs sham laser/diathermy/brain stimulation plus attention: pain −1.0/10 at 18 wk (95% CI −1.5 to −0.4), small. Tests a bundled package, not PNE alone. Single centre. |
| Lane et al. 2022, *Pain* 163(5):852–860 | Pragmatic cluster RCT, 45 PT clinics, 319 patients with chronic spine pain | Training PTs in PNE vs usual care: **no difference** in PROMIS physical function at 12 wk. Small gain in pain self-efficacy. Delivery fidelity was poor. |
| Dams et al. 2023, *Pain* 164(7):1489–1501 (EduCan) | Double-blind RCT, post-breast-cancer surgery, N=184 | PNE vs biomedical pain education: **no difference** in pain-related disability at 12 mo or on any secondary outcome. |
| Huysmans et al. 2023, *Br J Anaesth* 131(3):572–585 | Multicentre RCT, lumbar radiculopathy surgery, N=120 | Perioperative PNE vs biomedical education: no difference in pain at 12 mo. Better SF-36 physical score, kinesiophobia and catastrophizing; cost-effective. |
| Louw et al. 2011, *Arch Phys Med Rehabil* 92:2041; Louw et al. 2016, *Physiother Theory Pract* 32:332 | Systematic reviews | Positive effects on pain, disability, catastrophizing, kinesiophobia and healthcare use. Authored by PNE proponents. |
| Wood & Hendrick 2019, *Eur J Pain* 23:234 | Meta-analysis, chronic LBP | 8 RCTs (n=615). PNE vs no PNE: short-term pain difference 0.73/10, not significant (low-certainty GRADE). PNE added to physiotherapy: pain 1.32/10 and RMDQ 3.94 (moderate certainty). |
| Watson et al. 2019, *J Pain* 20:1140 | Mixed-methods SR/MA, chronic MSK pain | 12 RCTs (n=755). Pain effects vs control were of low clinical relevance and not significant (short term −5.9/100, 95% CI −13.8 to 1.9). Disability effects small. Kinesiophobia effect was clinically relevant in the short term. Qualitative synthesis showed patients value reconceptualisation. |
| Siddall et al. 2022, *Pain* 163:e20 | Meta-analysis, chronic MSK pain | 5 RCTs (n=460). PNE plus exercise beat exercise alone at 0–12 wk: pain −2.09/10 (low certainty), and also disability, kinesiophobia and catastrophizing. Too few long-term data to pool. |

**Gaps.**
- Effects on pain intensity are generally small.
- Long-term data are thin.
- Heterogeneous definitions and doses of "PNE."
- Control conditions are often weak.
- Many trials and reviews are by developers.
- No trials in MCS, EHS, POTS or long COVID.
- Evidence is strongest for changing beliefs (catastrophizing, kinesiophobia) rather than pain itself.
- Pragmatic real-world delivery (Lane 2022) and post-surgical trials (EduCan, Huysmans, Louw) mostly show no effect on pain.
- Later meta-analyses (2022–2024, e.g. Bonatesta 2022 *J Pain*; Núñez-Cortés 2024 *Pain*, dose–response; fibromyalgia reviews 2022) broadly echo the pattern of benefit mainly when PNE is combined with exercise.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory
- **1990s:** NOI runs neurodynamics courses internationally.
- **2002–2004:** Moseley's first RCTs.
- **2003:** *Explain Pain* published. It became a staple physio text and was later translated into Dutch, French, Portuguese, German, Hebrew and other languages.
- **2011:** Moseley's TEDxAdelaide talk "Why things hurt" brings PNE to a large lay audience.
- **2013:** *Explain Pain* 2nd ed. and Louw's *Why You Hurt* / TNE books. Rapid growth of US uptake via EIM and ISPI (Louw's International Spine and Pain Institute).
- **2015:** Moseley & Butler, "Fifteen years of explaining pain" (*J Pain*). Protectometer handbook.
- **~2017:** *Explain Pain Supercharged*. Pain Revolution's Rural Outreach Tour begins (annual through 2026, Tweed Heads–Port Macquarie on 15–23 Oct 2026). Tame the Beast video released (Moseley, Dave Moen and a communicator; site refreshed 2024).
- **2018–2019:** Major trials and meta-analyses (Malfliet, Traeger, Watson, Wood).
- **2022–2024:** RESOLVE (Bagg 2022) and further null or mixed pragmatic/surgical trials (Lane 2022, EduCan 2023, Huysmans 2023).
- **~2021 onward:** Flippin' Pain UK campaign (180,000+ reached). PNE becomes the education layer of apps (Curable, Pathways) and of Pain Reprocessing Therapy. The PRT trial (Ashar 2022) boosted public interest in "pain is in the brain" messaging that descends from PNE.
- **2020s:** Pain Revolution reports 20,000+ lives changed, 150+ communities, 80+ communities with a trained Local Pain Educator, and AUD 700k+ raised.
- **Reddit/social:** PNE concepts are pervasive in r/ChronicPain, r/TMSdiscovery and physio Twitter/X. Usually referenced as "pain science" rather than by brand.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **Small effects on pain.** Critics, including meta-researchers in the physio and pain community, note modest effects and possible over-promotion relative to the evidence. Commonly cited are the null PREVENT trial (acute LBP), whose co-authors include Moseley, the null EduCan and Lane 2022 trials, and Watson 2019's non-significant pooled pain effect.
- **Risk of "it's all in your head" messaging.** Simplified slogans such as "pain is in the brain" can be read as invalidating. Patients sometimes feel blamed. Moseley has refined the messaging toward "pain is real, protective and modifiable."
- **Scope creep.** The concept has been extrapolated by commercial "brain retraining" programs (Curable, PRT-style apps, ME/CFS programs) to conditions where PNE itself has little or no trial evidence.
- **Conflicts of interest.** Originators sell the books and courses and conduct many of the trials and reviews.
- **Fatigue extension.** Its use in chronic fatigue syndrome (Meeus/Nijs) is contested by ME/CFS patient groups, though it is far less controversial than the Lightning Process or GET.

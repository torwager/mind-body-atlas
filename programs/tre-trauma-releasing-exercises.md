## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
- **Innate discharge reflex.** Tremoring is said to be an inborn, mammalian way of discharging stress energy once a threat has passed. Berceli cites children shaking in a Lebanese bomb shelter and wild animals (the much-repeated "bear shaking off" story) shaking after danger. Adults are said to suppress this reflex through social conditioning, which leaves the stress response "incomplete."
- **Stored tension and "trauma in the body."** Chronic stress and trauma are said to be held as chronic muscular contraction, especially in the **psoas**, which proponents frame as the fight/flight/fetal-curl muscle. Hall (2020) summarises the proponents' view as trauma leaving a "frozen residue of energy" that has not been discharged.
- **Nervous-system self-regulation.** Activating "neurogenic tremors" is said to calm a dysregulated nervous system, shifting it from sympathetic arousal back toward parasympathetic balance. It does this "without verbal processing" and "doesn't require belief to work." Secondary framing borrows from polyvagal theory (Porges endorses TRE on the site, and Berceli contributed a chapter to Grassmann, Stupiggia & Porges, eds., 2025). The polyvagal link has not been tested clinically.
- **Body intelligence.** "The body releases what it needs to release" (Berceli). The tremor is treated as self-organising, so the practitioner's job is to allow and titrate it rather than to direct it.
- **What is not claimed.** No specific brain circuit, central-sensitisation or predictive-coding model is offered; the framing is somatic and autonomic. The site gives no neurobiological detail beyond "neurobiological effects of trauma."
- **Critics' alternative.** The tremor is ordinary fatigue- or stretch-induced physiological tremor. Any benefit comes from relaxation, expectancy and group or social effects.

## 2. One mechanism or many?
**One mechanism.** A single, non-disorder-specific model covers everything: suppressed natural tremor plus stored tension leads to a dysregulated stress response. TRE is presented as a general stress and trauma self-regulation tool, not a disease treatment. It makes no differential claims for pain versus fatigue versus anxiety. Condition-specific use (MS, Parkinson's, fibromyalgia, restless legs) comes from individual practitioners and researchers, not from a differentiated theory. The only differentiation is in **dosing and safety**: people with complex trauma, psychiatric or medical conditions are told to work with a Provider and titrate more slowly.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
- **Exercise sequence (6–7 steps, about 10–15 min).** Ankle and calf stretches, calf raises, quad/thigh work, a hamstring forward-fold, a hip-opening stretch, and a sustained wall-sit or "slide" to fatigue the legs and psoas. These prime the musculature to tremor.
- **Tremor phase (about 15 min to start).** Lie supine with the soles of the feet together and knees open (butterfly), then gradually close the knees and raise the pelvis briefly. Let tremors start in the legs and spread to the pelvis, trunk, arms and head. Stop the tremor at will by straightening the legs.
- **Self-regulation and titration.** Stop or adjust on pain; pause or slow down if emotionally overwhelmed. Rest supine for a few minutes afterwards.
- **Dose (FAQ).** Beginners do about 15-minute sessions, no more than 3–4 times a week. After a few weeks, practise at least twice weekly up to daily, with longer sessions allowed. It is framed as a lifelong self-care tool.
- **Learning formats.** Intro workshops, often a 1-day or multi-session course; 1:1 sessions (e.g., four-session online intro courses); online group "shakes" and refreshers; retreats; 3-month burnout programmes; agency and large-group trainings for humanitarian, military and first-responder staff.
- **Certification (Global Certification Training Program).** Choose a Trainer and pay the Enrollee fee. Complete two 3-day face-to-face Modules (I and II), personal practice, observation and supervised client sessions, then get Trainer sign-off to become a Provider. There are add-on pathways (Neurogenic Yoga dual certification, Agency-Based Instructor, Specialized Certifying Instructor), and Trainers are by invitation. No prior clinical licence is required.
- **Adjuncts.** It is positioned as complementary to psychotherapy or medical care, not a substitute.

## 4. Roots and influences
- **Reichian body psychotherapy** (muscular "armour") and **Lowen's Bioenergetic Analysis.** Bioenergetics already used stress positions that provoke vibration and tremor ("grounding," the bow); TRE is in effect a simplified, non-cathartic, self-help descendant.
- **Ethological and somatic trauma models.** Peter Levine's Somatic Experiencing (animal shaking and discharge after threat) and Robert Scaer's *The Body Bears the Burden* (freeze and discharge). Both Levine and Scaer endorse TRE on the site, and Scaer co-wrote *Shake It Off Naturally* (2015).
- **Polyvagal theory** (Porges): adopted later as a framing.
- **Spontaneous-movement and shaking traditions**: qigong "zifagong," Kundalini kriyas, and Quaker/Shaker and ecstatic religious shaking. These are acknowledged in the broader literature on spontaneous movements (Begin et al. 2022) but are not a formal lineage.
- **Humanitarian trauma work and mindfulness**: Berceli & Napoli 2007 framed it as a mindfulness-based trauma-prevention tool for social workers.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- **Guidelines.** It is absent from NICE, APA, VA/DoD and WHO PTSD, anxiety and chronic-pain guidelines.
- **Government and military.** The US Defense Centers of Excellence included TRE in a 2011 grey-literature review of mind-body skills for autonomic regulation, noting ease of use and humanitarian deployment. It was not endorsed as evidence-based. There are Australian parliamentary submissions and a 2019 veteran case report.
- **Institutional uptake.** NGOs (SOS Children's Villages, Chrysalis Academy in South Africa), Danish MS and arthritis societies, UK services (TRE UK lists HM Prison Service, Mind, a fire service and police clients), and a Beijing Haidian mental-health hospital report (2025). A Chinese hospital-based RCT protocol uses TRE as an add-on to drug treatment for adolescent emotional disorders.
- **Insurance.** TRE is not a recognised billable modality. It may be delivered within licensed psychotherapy or physiotherapy sessions billed under generic codes. The site says it has not been evaluated by the FDA or AMA.

## 6. Evidence base
**Peer-reviewed trials of TRE specifically (PubMed-verified):**

| Study | Design | N | Key result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Harrison, Keating & Morgan 2018, *J Am Board Fam Med* 31(5):783–794 (PMID 30201675) | RCT, TRE (once weekly × 6 wk) vs discussion-group control, restless legs syndrome | 18 (9/9) | **No between-group differences** on any outcome (IRLS, global severity, stress, insomnia, depression); both groups improved over time. Independent (Monash) investigators. |
| Skovgaard, Trénel, Hanehøj & Lynning 2025, *Adv Mind Body Med* 39(1):4–12 (PMID 40064004) | RCT, 8 TRE sessions + daily home practice vs usual care, MS (Danish MS Society) | 28 | Primary outcome (MFIS fatigue) **null in the unadjusted analysis**; significant (p=.03) only after adjusting for baseline covariate imbalance. Pain and spasticity diaries improved. Unblinded, no active control. |
| Lynning et al. 2021, *J Tradit Complement Med* 11(5):383–389 (PMID 34522632) | Uncontrolled pilot, 9-wk TRE, MS | 9 | Self-reported symptoms, stress and MFIS fatigue (43.7→22.0) improved; objective spasticity (PSAD) unchanged. |
| Berceli, Salmon, Bonifas & Ndefo 2014, *Glob Adv Health Med* 3(5):45–48 (PMID 25568824) | Uncontrolled pre-post pilot, 10 wk, caregivers at SOS Children's Village, Cape Town | 21 | 91% adherence; QoL score rose from 3.81 to 4.35. Founder-led. |

**Reviews and protocols:**
- Roos, van Niekerk & Tönsing 2026, *Occup Ther Health Care* (PMID 42713778): JBI scoping review of 22 studies (24 records). Studies were mostly in stress- and trauma-exposed groups; possible benefits on mood, anxiety-related sleep and cognition; designs "not consistently rigorous." This grew out of Roos's 2023 University of Pretoria thesis.
- Zhao et al. 2024, *BMC Complement Med Ther* 24:403 (PMID 39574118): protocol for a 140-dyad mother-adolescent RCT of TRE plus medication versus medication alone (ChiCTR2100044553). No results paper found in PubMed or OpenAlex (checked Oct 2026).
- ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07242651 (Cairo University, Faculty of Physical Therapy, Egypt): TRE 3×/week for 8 weeks plus usual antihypertensive care versus usual care alone, outcomes quality of life and sleep. Women aged 50–65, estimated N=40. Listed as not yet recruiting (estimated start 2025-11-18); no results.
- Wikipedia also cites a 2023 scoping review of 18 studies ("preliminary and tentative"). Its citation confirms this is Roos's 2023 University of Pretoria master's thesis, the precursor of the 2026 paper; it is not PubMed-indexed.

**Other (non-PubMed or grey):** Heath & Beattie 2019 veteran PTSD case report (*J Mil Veterans Health*); Herold 2015 small Ukrainian samples; Oh & Shin 2021 anxiety pilot (Korean journal); Parker et al. 2024 East African refugees (*Psychology*, an open-access SCIRP journal); Fiol et al. 2025 MS (*J Yoga Physiother*); plus many master's and PhD theses (Berceli 2009: non-randomised controlled pilot in 61 students, STAI anxiety reduced after 6 sessions over 2 weeks, HRV changes "in the desired direction"; Atterbury 2019 on Parkinson's; Plajh 2023 on fibromyalgia) and ISTSS posters (Davis 2018).

**Gaps:** There are no adequately powered, blinded or active-controlled trials and no trials in the target chronic-overlapping-pain or fatigue syndromes. The two RCTs were in neurological conditions and were essentially null on primary outcomes. Much of the literature is produced by Providers or the founder. No mechanistic studies (EMG, HRV, cortisol) have been peer-reviewed. Tremor has never been tested against the same exercises without tremor, or against generic relaxation.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory
- **2002–2008: founding phase.** traumaprevention.com was live by January 2004. Berceli's books appeared in 2005 (*Trauma Releasing Exercises*) and 2008 (*The Revolutionary Trauma Release Process*, Namaste). Training spread through humanitarian networks in Africa and the Middle East.
- **2009–2015: institutionalisation.** The PhD came in 2009, followed by the DCoE mention (2011) and early spread to Europe (Denmark, Germany, Netherlands, Slovenia, Finland), Brazil, South Africa and Australia. *Shake It Off Naturally* (with Scaer) appeared in 2015, and the 2013 Spanish edition was one of many translations.
- **2016–2020: European clinical interest.** MS pilots (presented 2019, published 2021), the restless legs RCT (2018), and a Science-Based Medicine critique (Hall, April 2020), which signals mainstream visibility.
- **2020–2026: social-media growth.** TRE rode the pandemic-era "somatic" and "nervous-system regulation" wave, including "shaking" content on Instagram, TikTok and YouTube. That content is often not branded TRE but cites Berceli. The nonprofit was rebranded as TRE For All / treglobal.org around 2024. Wikipedia reports more than 1,000 certified Providers. A Wikipedia article was created around 2025–26, and a peer-reviewed scoping review appeared in 2026. The site listed 65 scheduled events for Oct 2026–May 2027.
- **Social metrics.** David Berceli's YouTube channel (@davidberceli) has about 48.2K subscribers and 375 videos (Oct 2026); the official TRE For All channel has 14 subscribers. OpenAlex indexes about 150 works mentioning "trauma releasing exercises"; PubMed has 3 with the phrase in title/abstract. No Reddit subscriber count or Google Trends series could be retrieved: Reddit's JSON endpoints (www and old.reddit) returned empty/blocked responses, and Google Trends is not accessible without a browser session.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **Implausible mechanism.** Hall (Science-Based Medicine, 2020) argues that "stored trauma energy" and the psoas-as-trauma-store are Reichian, untested and biologically implausible. On this view the tremors are normal fatigue tremor, and the animal-shaking story (e.g., the bear) is anecdote at best. Hall judges it probably safe and possibly relaxing but no better than other relaxation methods, and says "I can't recommend TRE." (Her statement that no scientific studies had evaluated its clinical benefits overlooked the 2018 restless legs RCT, which was itself null.)
- **Evidence presentation.** The site says TRE is "backed by research" but gives no specifics on the main pages. Its research page mixes theses, posters, parliamentary submissions and peer-reviewed papers without grading them. TRE UK claims ">60% reduction in anxiety and depression" with no published source.
- **Safety.** Reported adverse effects include transient nausea, headaches, muscle aches, and emotional flooding or dissociation in trauma survivors. Systematic safety data are lacking.
- **Lay certification.** Providers need no clinical licence, yet TRE is marketed for PTSD and trauma populations. Branding has also fragmented (TRE®, Total Release Experience®, Neurogenic Yoga™).

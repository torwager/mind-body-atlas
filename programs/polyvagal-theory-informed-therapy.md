## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)

- **Three-tier autonomic hierarchy.** The theory holds that the mammalian autonomic nervous system responds to challenge in a phylogenetically ordered sequence:
  1. A **"ventral vagal"** circuit (myelinated vagal fibres from nucleus ambiguus) linked to the face–heart "social engagement system": calm, connection and safety.
  2. If that fails, **sympathetic mobilisation** (fight/flight).
  3. If that fails, **"dorsal vagal" immobilisation** (unmyelinated fibres from the dorsal motor nucleus): shutdown, collapse, dissociation and conservation of energy.

  Porges presents this as the reverse order of evolution ("dissolution", following Hughlings Jackson). Respiratory sinus arrhythmia (RSA) is his index of ventral vagal "tone."
- **Neuroception.** The nervous system is said to judge safety, danger or life-threat automatically and below awareness, using cues from the body, the environment and other people. Faulty neuroception (detecting threat where there is none) is proposed to keep people in defensive states. The process is state-dependent: a defensive state biases perception towards threat.
- **Co-regulation.** Autonomic state is regulated socially, through prosody, facial expression and eye contact. Safe relationships are treated as a biological need ("safety is the treatment").
- **Applied to symptoms.** Chronic symptoms are framed as the body being "stuck" in sympathetic or dorsal-vagal defensive states after trauma or chronic threat. Kolacz and Porges (2018, *Front Med*) proposed that chronic diffuse pain and functional GI disorders after traumatic stress arise from a vagal-brake withdrawal, which promotes sympathetic and inflammatory states and visceral hypersensitivity. Dorsal vagal "shutdown" maps onto gut dysmotility, fatigue and dissociation (Kolacz, Kovacic and Porges 2019, *Dev Psychobiol*). PVI materials also describe the vagus as central to stress and inflammation-related disease.
- **Dana's clinical translation** ("Rhythm of Regulation"). The model is taught as a three-step "autonomic ladder": ventral at the top, sympathetic in the middle, dorsal at the bottom. Clients map their own states, triggers and "glimmers" and learn to move back up the ladder.

## 2. One mechanism or many?

**One mechanism (transdiagnostic).** The theory does not separate illnesses mechanistically. Anxiety, PTSD, depression, dissociation, chronic pain, IBS/functional GI disorders, fibromyalgia, autism-related auditory hypersensitivity and addiction are all read as expressions of defensive autonomic states and impaired neuroception or co-regulation. Differences across illnesses are differences of **state**, not disease:
- sympathetic-dominant presentations (anxiety, panic, hypervigilance, pain flares);
- dorsal-dominant presentations (depression, fatigue, numbness, collapse, GI hypomotility, fainting);
- blended states.

There is no condition-specific model for ME/CFS, POTS, MCS and similar illnesses within PVI or Dana's materials. Popular "nervous system regulation" coaches frequently extend polyvagal language to those conditions themselves.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies

Polyvagal theory is a lens rather than a manualised therapy. The common components are:

**Deb Dana's Rhythm of Regulation (clinical and self-help)**
- **Psychoeducation.** The autonomic ladder or hierarchy, and "befriending" the nervous system.
- **Mapping.** Personal maps of each state (how it feels, what you think and do in it), the triggers that move you down the ladder, and the "glimmers" or resources that move you up. Includes "profile" and "patterns" worksheets.
- **Tracking.** Moment-to-moment noticing of one's own state, which becomes the main self-monitoring skill.
- **Glimmers practice.** Five steps: see, stop, appreciate (e.g., hand on heart for a few seconds), remember (a "glimmer library"), share. The aim is to accumulate micro-moments of ventral state.
- **Shaping and anchoring.** Practices to stretch the "window" of ventral regulation:
  - slow breathing with long exhalations;
  - "voo" sounding, humming and singing;
  - attention to prosody and eye contact;
  - self-touch;
  - orienting to the environment;
  - "befriending" dorsal and sympathetic states rather than fighting them.
- **Co-regulation in therapy.** The therapist's own regulated state, voice and face are used as the active ingredient. Attunement is privileged over technique.
- **Dose.** No fixed dose. Practices are brief and done many times a day. Trainings for clinicians run 20–40+ hours (e.g., Foundations series, 40+ h; book-study course, 20+ h).

**Polyvagal Institute**
- **Certificate course.** 9 two-hour live classes plus 2 live discussions plus monthly Practice Pods, with 365 days of access. Topics: ANS, the state hierarchy, neuroception, interoception, co-regulation, movement and embodied trauma recovery, community as medicine, and relationships. No CE credits; open to anyone with a high-school diploma.
- **Other offerings.** Topic courses (addiction, yoga, education, parenting, SE, performance), monthly webinars, an annual International Gathering and a podcast. Device-based "Safe and Sound Protocol" and "Rest and Restore Protocol" filtered-music listening is promoted alongside (separate entry).

**Stanley Rosenberg's self-help** (*Accessing the Healing Power of the Vagus Nerve*, North Atlantic Books, 2017)
- **"Basic Exercise."** Lie on your back with fingers interlaced behind the head. Keep the head still and move the eyes to one side for about 30–60 s until a spontaneous swallow, yawn or sigh, then repeat on the other side. It is claimed to restore ventral vagal function.
- **Other exercises** include the "Salamander" exercise and neck and face massage techniques. The book claims benefit for anxiety, depression, migraine, neck/shoulder pain, COPD, autism and more.

**Generic "vagal toning" practices** often bundled under the polyvagal label: cold-water face immersion, gargling, humming, paced breathing, and HRV biofeedback.

## 4. Roots and influences

- **Psychophysiology and vagal research.** Porges's 1970s–80s work on heart-rate variability, RSA and attention; Hughlings Jackson's dissolution principle; comparative and evolutionary neuroanatomy.
- **Ethology and defence-cascade research.** Tonic immobility or feigned death; attachment theory (Bowlby). Peter Levine's Somatic Experiencing developed in parallel and adopted PVT early.
- **Trauma therapy movement.** Bessel van der Kolk's *The Body Keeps the Score* (2014) popularised PVT. Sensorimotor Psychotherapy (Ogden): Siegel's "window of tolerance" is often merged with Dana's ladder. Internal Family Systems is also an influence.
- **Older traditions.** Yoga, pranayama and chanting are re-explained in vagal terms (e.g., Sullivan et al. 2018, *Front Hum Neurosci*). Rosenberg's exercises come from craniosacral therapy and osteopathy. Mindfulness and contemplative practice are re-read through PVT (Poli et al. 2021 systematic review).
- **Selye/HPA stress physiology**, and **biofeedback/HRV training**.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance

- **Where it is used.** PVT is among the most widely taught frameworks in trauma-informed care, used across psychotherapy, social work, school "regulation" programmes, occupational therapy, yoga therapy, coaching and addiction treatment. PESI, W.W. Norton, Sounds True and Psychotherapy Networker all sell polyvagal content. Dana has international partner trainings, e.g., in Italy and France.
- **Medical endorsement.** No clinical practice guideline recommends it as a treatment. A 2024 review of PTSD psychotherapies noted that polyvagal-based methods lack an evidence base (Kowalski 2024, *Psychiatr Pol*, PMID 37074864). It is not listed by NICE, the VA/DoD or the APA. It is largely absent from hospital pain, rheumatology and gastroenterology protocols, apart from individual clinicians' use.
- **Insurance.** There is no specific billing code. Licensed therapists who use a polyvagal lens bill standard psychotherapy codes (e.g., US CPT 90834/90837). Courses, books and coaching are self-pay.

## 6. Evidence base

**Basic theory (not treatment trials)**
- Porges SW. Orienting in a defensive world: mammalian modifications of our evolutionary heritage. A Polyvagal Theory. *Psychophysiology* 1995;32(4):301–318. PMID 7652107. This is the founding statement.
- Kolacz J, Porges SW. Chronic diffuse pain and functional gastrointestinal disorders after traumatic stress: pathophysiology through a polyvagal perspective. *Front Med* 2018. PMID 29904631. Theoretical review that links PVT to the chronic overlapping pain conditions.
- Porges SW. Polyvagal Theory: current status, clinical applications, and future directions. *Clin Neuropsychiatry* 2025;22(3). PMID 40735382.

**Gaps**
- No RCT of Deb Dana's Rhythm of Regulation, the PVI curriculum or Rosenberg's Basic Exercise.
- No trials in ME/CFS, long COVID, fibromyalgia, POTS, IBS or chronic back pain.
- Existing studies are small, unblinded and often use HRV as a surrogate outcome. The validity of HRV as a vagal index is itself disputed.
- Benefits of overlapping techniques (slow breathing and HRV biofeedback, yoga, mindfulness, trauma-focused therapy, vagus nerve stimulation) are often cited as support for PVT. Those are tests of the generic techniques, not of the theory or the branded approach.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory

- **1994–2010: academic phase.** Steady psychophysiology citations, with PubMed title/abstract mentions of about 0–1 per year.
- **2011: book.** *The Polyvagal Theory* (Norton) is published.
- **2014: popular breakout.** Van der Kolk's bestseller (*The Body Keeps the Score*) features PVT and brings it to clinicians and lay readers.
- **2017–2018: rapid expansion.**
  - Porges's *Pocket Guide* (2017) and Rosenberg's self-help book (2017).
  - Dana's *The Polyvagal Theory in Therapy* and Porges and Dana (eds.), *Clinical Applications of the Polyvagal Theory* (2018).
  - Wikipedia views: 23.6k (2017), then 77.8k (2018), then 144k (2019).
- **2020–2021: peak.** Pandemic-era online trainings; PVI launches (website live by December 2020). Dana publishes *Polyvagal Exercises for Safety and Connection* (2020) and *Anchored* (Sounds True, 2021); Porges publishes *Polyvagal Safety* (2021). Wikipedia views peak at 190k (2020) and 219k (2021).
- **2022–2024: plateau.** "Glimmers" goes viral on TikTok and Instagram (about 2022), and "nervous system regulation" becomes a wellness-influencer genre. Dana publishes *Polyvagal Practices* (2023), then card decks, flip chart and workbooks. PubMed mentions run at about 23–27 per year. Wikipedia views are about 185–203k per year.
- **2025–2026: still active but slipping.** PubMed mentions are at their highest (31 in 2025; 28 by Oct 2026), driven partly by the critique and debate literature. Wikipedia views fell to 133k (2025) and 75k for January–September 2026, which suggests attention is declining or plateauing.
- **Reddit is small.** Dedicated subreddits are tiny: r/polyvagal had about 664 members (June 2023 capture) and r/PolyvagalTheory 939 (March 2025 capture). Discussion happens mostly in general trauma and chronic-illness communities. Live Reddit counts were blocked, so these come from Wayback captures.
- **Google Trends not available.** The Trends API refused automated requests (HTTP 429) on 2026-10-13, so no search-interest series is reported; Wikipedia pageviews serve as the proxy.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms

- **The physiology is disputed.**
  - Grossman and Taylor (2007, *Biol Psychol*, PMID 17081672) argued that RSA is not a pure index of cardiac vagal tone, and that the dorsal motor nucleus is not phylogenetically "primitive."
  - Neuhuber and Berthoud (2022, *Biol Psychol*, PMID 36100134) found that the vagal anatomy does not fit PVT's ventral/dorsal functional split.
  - Grossman (2023, *Biol Psychol* 180:108589, PMID 37230290) argued that all five basic premises are untenable or highly implausible, and that equating RSA with vagal tone is a category mistake.
  - Grossman P, Ackland GL, … Taylor EW, … Zucker IH (2026, *Clin Neuropsychiatry* 23(1):100–112, PMID 41768017) is a consensus critique by **39 autonomic-physiology and evolution experts**. It concluded that the major tenets of PVT are not supported and that the theory is "untenable." The disputed tenets cover RSA, the functions of nucleus ambiguus and the dorsal motor nucleus, vagal evolution (e.g., myelinated vagal fibres and RSA-like coupling in non-mammals), and mammal-specific sociality.
  - Porges replied (2026, PMID 41768026) that the critics attack a misrepresented "proxy" of the theory.
- **Testability.** Porges has said the theory was not meant to be "proven" or "falsified" (2021). Critics see that as unfalsifiability; he later disputed the characterisation.
- **Clinical overreach.** Critics argue that the clinical "superstructure" (safety, co-regulation, defensive states) restates older psychological ideas, and gains nothing from the contested neurophysiology. Popular use simplifies the theory further, into claims such as "dorsal vagal shutdown causes fatigue" and "tone your vagus to heal chronic illness". It is then used to market courses and devices to people with ME/CFS, long COVID and similar conditions, without condition-specific evidence.
- **Conflicts of interest.** Porges and Dana have commercial links: Porges developed the Safe and Sound Protocol, and Dana advises Unyte-iLs, which sells it. Both receive book and training revenue.

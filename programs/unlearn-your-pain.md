## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)

- **"Mind Body Syndrome" (MBS), later renamed "neuroplastic," "nociplastic," "primary," "psychophysiologic" or "neural circuit" disorders.** Symptoms are real and are generated by the brain through **learned neural pathways**. There is no ongoing tissue damage. Schubiner says the brain "learns" pain much as it learns other habits, and this pain can be unlearned.
- **Danger/threat signal and predictive processing.** The brain predicts danger and produces pain or other symptoms as a protective alarm. Fear of the symptom, attention to it and hypervigilance keep the alarm running in a self-reinforcing **pain–fear cycle**. The 2026 materials use "predictive coding" language.
- **Stress, emotion and repressed or avoided feelings.** This element comes from Sarno's TMS model. Childhood adversity, trauma, unresolved emotional conflict, suppressed anger and guilt, and personality traits (perfectionism, people-pleasing, self-criticism) are seen as sensitising the danger system. Current stressors that echo earlier ones trigger symptoms. The emotional-processing arm became EAET.
- **Autonomic and limbic activation.** These are invoked as downstream effectors (the fight-or-flight / danger system). Schubiner also describes the same circuitry producing fatigue, dizziness, GI and bladder symptoms, anxiety and depression.
- **Diagnosis "ruling in."** The model is made explicit in Schubiner et al. 2024 (J Pain). Primary pain is ruled in by positive features, not by excluding other causes: onset during stress without injury, symptoms that spread or move, triggers that are unrelated to physical load (weather, time of day, stress), symmetric or bilateral pain, multiple functional syndromes, childhood adversity, and conditioned triggers. Imaging anomalies are treated as incidental.

## 2. One mechanism or many?

**Mostly unified, with some condition-specific emphasis.** One brain-generated danger and learning mechanism is applied to all MBS conditions: pain syndromes, IBS, bladder symptoms, fatigue, POTS-type symptoms, anxiety and depression. The workbook series is split only by presenting complaint (*Unlearn Your Pain* vs *Unlearn Your Anxiety & Depression*). Differentiation happens **within** a condition rather than between named diagnoses. The first step is to decide whether a given patient's symptoms are primary (neuroplastic) or secondary (structural, nociceptive, neuropathic, autoimmune, cancer), and the program applies only to the primary ones. The research arm adapts the protocol per condition (an IBS-specific EAET, a urogenital-pain interview, POTS Reprocessing Therapy 2026), but the underlying mechanism stays the same. The 2024 J Pain paper estimated 88% of chronic back/neck pain in a physiatry clinic to be primary.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies

**Book/workbook program.** The original was a structured **4-week (28-day) daily program**, with later editions expanded:
1. **Medical evaluation first** to rule out structural disease. This is followed by a self-assessment checklist for MBS features and a timeline linking symptom onset to life events.
2. **Education / reattribution.** Learning that symptoms are real but reversible, brain-generated and not dangerous. The aim is to build "100% belief" in the diagnosis.
3. **Expressive writing.** Daily exercises: free writing about stressful events, unsent letters (anger, forgiveness, grief), dialogues with oneself or others, and writing about self-compassion. These derive from Pennebaker-style disclosure.
4. **"Talking back to the brain."** Firm, confident self-statements when symptoms arise, refusing to respond with fear.
5. **Somatic tracking / mindful attention.** Observing sensations with curiosity and safety (shared with PRT). Guided meditations and visualisations come on audio.
6. **Graded exposure / resuming activity.** Returning to feared or avoided activities and dropping protective behaviours. The "triggers" that set off symptoms are deconditioned.
7. **Emotional awareness and expression.** Identifying and feeling anger, sadness, guilt and love, and becoming more assertive in relationships (the EAET core).
8. **Positive psychology.** Gratitude, kindness and joy exercises, and a "plan for the future."

**Freedom From Chronic Pain online course** (Schubiner and Greenham): 30 modules of video, audio and text over self-paced 12-month access. It covers predictive-coding neuroscience, diagnosis and assessment, provocative testing, graded exposure, PRT/somatic tracking, EAET, ISTDP-informed emotion work and IFS (Internal Family Systems). A moderated clinical forum is included, and higher tiers add clinician messaging or a session. A doctor's check is required before starting.

**Clinical model** (Providence clinic and telehealth): one long physician consultation that diagnoses MBS and educates the patient, followed by brief therapy. Historically this was a 4-week group (the 2010 RCT used 3 weekly 2-hour groups). Patients are handed off to EAET, PRT or ISTDP-trained therapists.

**Coursera "Reign of Pain"** (MSU): 7 modules, about 17 h by module estimates. Topics are pain neuroscience, stress and emotions, nociplastic conditions, how doctors assess "neural circuit disorders," and treatments.

## 4. Roots and influences

- **John Sarno's TMS model** is the main precursor. Schubiner presents MBS as a modernised and broadened TMS.
- **Psychodynamic theory**, especially **Davanloo's ISTDP** through Allan Abbass. *Hidden From View* (a clinician guide) is co-written with this network.
- **Pennebaker expressive writing / emotional disclosure research.**
- **Pain neuroscience**: central sensitisation, Melzack's neuromatrix, Apkarian's work on the transition from acute to chronic pain via learning, predictive processing.
- **Behaviourism / classical conditioning** and **exposure therapy** (fear-avoidance models).
- **Mindfulness / MBSR** (meditations; Schubiner taught mindfulness) and **positive psychology**.
- **Collaborators who formalised spin-offs**: Mark Lumley (EAET), Alan Gordon (PRT), the Psychophysiologic Disorders Association / ATNS community.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance

- **Academic base.** The clinic sits inside a teaching hospital (Ascension Providence, Southfield) with an MSU faculty appointment. MSU offers the Coursera course. CME/CE credit is available for clinicians viewing *This Might Hurt*.
- **Spread through derived therapies.** EAET has been tested in the VA (Yarns et al.) and in Sweden (Karolinska, internet EAET for SSD). PRT came from the Boulder trial, and PORT for POTS is under development at VCU (2026). Schubiner's disclosures (JAMA Psychiatry 2022) list co-ownership of Freedom From Chronic Pain, Inc., book royalties, consulting for UnitedHealth Group, Karuna Labs and Curable Health, and personal fees from OVID Dx.
- **Classification.** He is pushing for an operational ICD-11 "chronic primary pain" diagnosis (J Pain 2024).
- **Guidelines.** The branded program is not named in NICE, CDC or ACP guidelines. Those guidelines endorse generic psychological therapies (CBT, ACT, mindfulness) for chronic primary pain, and EAET/PRT are not yet guideline-listed.
- **Insurance.** The book, online course and Schubiner's private telehealth are self-pay; the site says he does not accept insurance. EAET or PRT from licensed clinicians may be covered as ordinary psychotherapy.

## 6. Evidence base

**No trial of the branded self-help book or online course was found.** A PubMed search for "Unlearn Your Pain" returned no trial of the book. The trials below test **clinician-delivered or internet components** derived from Schubiner's model, and almost all have Schubiner as co-author (allegiance; he discloses book royalties and co-ownership of Freedom From Chronic Pain, Inc.).

**Gaps.** There are no trials of the book, workbook or Freedom From Chronic Pain course as sold. Nearly all trials involve the developers. Many are small, use waitlist controls and have short follow-up. In the largest EAET trial (fibromyalgia) the primary outcome was null against education. The evidence is concentrated on musculoskeletal pain, fibromyalgia, IBS and SSD. There are no trials for ME/CFS, long COVID, MCS or anxiety/depression as primary indications, despite claims in the book's subtitle. A 2020 meta-analysis of short-term psychodynamic therapy for functional somatic disorders (Abbass et al., *Psychother Psychosom*, PMID 32428905, Schubiner co-author) supports the broader approach.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory

- **2000s:** a single hospital clinic in Michigan.
- **2010:** first edition of the book (self-published) and the first RCT (JGIM). Later self-published editions and the workbook (now 4th edition) plus *Unlearn Your Anxiety & Depression* sustained steady sales in the Sarno/TMS community. Exact sales are not public.
- **2012–2019:** grew with the Psychophysiologic Disorders Association (PPDA) and TMS forums (TMS Wiki, r/TMSmindbody, where Schubiner's book is a standard recommendation alongside Sarno and Ozanich). EAET trials published 2016–2020.
- **2021–2022:** a sharp rise in attention after the PRT JAMA Psychiatry trial (wide media coverage) and the documentary *This Might Hurt* (c. 2022, year not verified; directed by Kent Bassett and Marion Cunningham, following Schubiner's patients; CME screenings). Many podcast appearances.
- **2021–2025:** the Freedom From Chronic Pain online program existed by 2021 (Schubiner disclosed co-ownership of Freedom From Chronic Pain, Inc. in the 2021/2022 JAMA Psychiatry paper; the exact launch year is not stated on the site). The Coursera MOOC (MSU; ~9.8k enrolled learners and a 5.0 rating from 40 reviews by Oct 2026; launch year not verified). The ICD-11 primary-pain papers were published (J Pain 2024).
- **2026:** trade re-launch of *Unlearn Your Pain* (Penguin Random House / The Open Field, Maria Shriver imprint, 26 May 2026) with a foreword by journalist Kate Murphy. A POTS adaptation (PORT) appeared.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms

- **Over-generalisation.** One brain mechanism is extended from back pain to fatigue, POTS, bladder, skin, anxiety and depression, beyond what the trials show. ME/CFS and long-COVID patient communities object to "it's your brain" framings in general.
- **Diagnostic risk.** "Ruling in" primary pain from history features (88% of back pain called primary) risks missing secondary disease. The program's answer is to require medical evaluation first.
- **Conflicts and allegiance.** Schubiner co-authors nearly all supporting trials and earns from books, courses and consultancies. Replication of EAET without Schubiner is emerging (Yarns et al., JAMA Netw Open 2024, N=126, VA) but still involves the Lumley group. Note that Lumley et al. 2017 declared no conflicts for Schubiner, whereas later papers list book royalties and the course company.
- **Sarno legacy.** The repressed-rage / TMS framing remains contested in mainstream pain medicine. Trials report mixed results on whether emotional processing, rather than reattribution, is the active ingredient.
- **Patient blame.** Critics argue that emotion-based explanations can feel blaming if symptoms persist. Proponents stress that symptoms are "real" and that the model is not psychiatric labelling.

## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
- **Protective nervous system "stuck" in protection mode.** Symptoms are real and physical, but a protective brain/nervous system keeps generating them after the original trigger is gone. The free course says the brain's "timing" is off.
- **Neuroplastic symptoms.** The response is learned and over-sensitive, so it can be unlearned ("retrained"). This framing borrows directly from the neuroplastic-pain / PRT / Schubiner model and extends it from pain to fatigue, post-exertional malaise, orthostatic intolerance and so on.
- **Danger appraisal and attention.** Fear of symptoms, hypervigilance and symptom-tracking are treated as things that keep the loop going.
- **Suppressed emotion and pressure.** Phase 4 of the course covers anger, emotional safety and parts ("IFS") work, and the "4 Ps" are taught as personality pressures. This is a Sarno/Schubiner-style psychodynamic layer.
- **No biomedical model of secondary dysfunction** (unlike ANS Rewire). The site does not discuss mitochondria, immunity or infection.

## 2. One mechanism or many?
**Single unified mechanism.** ME/CFS, long COVID, POTS, MCAS, fibromyalgia and chronic pain are all treated as expressions of the same neuroplastic, protective-nervous-system process. Content is not split by diagnosis, and recovery interviews mix diagnoses freely. The only boundaries are eligibility rules: participants must have had a medical work-up with normal tests, and primary mental-health conditions are excluded.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
**Brain Retraining 101 (paid).** Self-paced, with lessons released relative to enrollment date. There is a "Start Here" module (including working with your doctor) followed by five phases:
1. **Safety in Your Body** (7 items): neuroscience basics; a daily safety practice.
2. **Safety with Symptoms** (10 items): somatic tracking; "interviewing" symptoms; handling "dips"; changing symptom vocabulary; "The Seven Fs"; sleep.
3. **Safety with Activity** (9 items): graded expansion via "The Ladder"; adding joy; handling symptoms during activity.
4. **Safety around Emotions** (12 items): anger; journaling options; an IFS-style parts overview; "The 4 Ps."
5. **Full Recovery and Beyond** (9 items): what full recovery means; a relapse/symptom-return protocol; building life after recovery.

Extras: 7 guided meditations (4–6 min each, including self-compassion) and an "Optional Learning" section with 6 guest lessons plus Agle's own "Simplifying Recovery" lesson. The "Start Here" module has 7 lessons. Most lessons run 10–25 min. Each phase ends with a recap, a companion worksheet and a private check-in.

**Support.** Four weekly group calls with coaches (Björn Borgers, Lalita Holmes, Elizabeth Babcock, Kate Van Boss, Devon Carter), two monthly calls with Agle, and a 1:1 coach match. Duration is open-ended because billing is monthly.

**Free starter course (5 emails).**
- Myth-busting.
- How symptoms arise.
- Keeping an "evidence list" that symptoms can change.
- Interviewing a symptom.
- Celebrating small wins to build safety.
- First practice: noticing one sensation with curiosity rather than alarm.

**Techniques surfaced in the interview cheat sheets.** Breathwork, yoga nidra, somatic tracking, hourly nervous-system "resets," gradual movement rebuilding, "I'm Safe" tapping, mindfulness, boundary-setting and emotional processing, reducing symptom monitoring, joy and meaning. The cheat sheets also point readers to the Curable app, Sarno's *Healing Back Pain* and Gordon's *The Way Out*.

**Interview archive as an intervention.** The roughly 300 recovery stories are framed as hope- and belief-building, which in practice is a form of expectancy and social-modelling intervention.

## 4. Roots and influences
- Sarno TMS and its successors: Schubiner's mind-body syndrome, and Alan Gordon's Pain Reprocessing Therapy (somatic tracking, "safety" reappraisal).
- Neuroplastic pain science (predictive processing, central sensitisation).
- Limbic-retraining culture (DNRS, Gupta, Lightning Process) via guests' stories, though the course does not name these methods.
- Internal Family Systems.
- Mindfulness, self-compassion and yoga nidra.
- Graded activity (CBT/GET lineage, reframed as "safety with activity").
- Social-work psychoeducation.
- Paul Garner's long-COVID recovery narrative.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
No integration into clinical guidelines, health systems or payers. Delivered as a direct-to-consumer subscription by a non-medical LLC with lay and peer coaches. Not reimbursable. NICE (2021) advises against GET- or Lightning-Process-type approaches for ME/CFS, which places this style of programme outside guideline care for ME/CFS.

## 6. Evidence base
- **No trials.** No RCT, cohort or pre-post outcome study of Brain Retraining 101 was found. PubMed ("Raelan Agle"; "brain retraining" with chronic fatigue or long COVID) and ClinicalTrials.gov returned nothing relevant (Oct 2026). The course page offers six testimonials and a claim that "hundreds" have improved.
- **Cefai S. "Recovery Report: What 75 YouTube Interviews Say About Recovery from Chronic Fatigue Syndrome and/or Related Illnesses."** Goldsmiths, University of London, July 2026. The author is Dr Sarah Cefai. The report is hosted on recoveryispossible.info (Goldsmiths branding, research.gold.ac.uk repository links) and promoted on raelanagle.com/research.
  - **Design:** Qualitative thematic and narrative analysis (cultural-studies framing) of 75 interviews from Agle's channel; mostly women.
  - **Reported findings:** 77% described themselves as fully recovered; 95% said mind-body ideas helped; 84% said nervous-system regulation helped. These figures are as summarised on raelanagle.com/research; the recoveryispossible.info landing page frames the study as qualitative and does not headline them. The researcher's further "100% researched/learning" figure could not be verified and was removed. No single method worked for everyone.
  - **Caveats:** Not peer-reviewed and not a clinical study. The sample was selected on recovery by the program's founder. Recovery and diagnosis are self-reported and unverified. The report states that it does not represent the CFS population and does not validate techniques. Funding and conflict-of-interest information are not stated.
- **Indirect evidence only.** Trials of related generic techniques, such as PRT for back pain (Ashar et al., 2022) and EAET for fibromyalgia, do not test this programme or ME/CFS/long COVID populations.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **Framing of ME/CFS.** The channel presents ME/CFS and long COVID as neuroplastic, retrainable conditions. Much of the ME/CFS patient and advocacy community rejects this framing, arguing that it echoes discredited psychogenic models and that graded activity risks post-exertional malaise relapse.
- **Survivorship bias.** The recovery-interview format shows only successes, and the Goldsmiths analysis inherits that selection.
- **Business model.** The open-ended $197/month subscription with lay coaches and no outcome data raises consumer-protection concerns.
- **Credentials.** The "neuroplasticity research" framing is not supported by programme-specific data, and the founder's credentials are in social work rather than medicine or neuroscience.
- **Cross-promotion.** Episode notes include affiliate-style codes for guests' paid programmes, such as 10% off Mickel Therapy with code RAELAN. Financial ties to promoted methods are not otherwise disclosed.
- **Balancing points.** The programme explicitly asks for medical evaluation first, positions itself as complementary to medical care, and excludes primary psychiatric conditions.

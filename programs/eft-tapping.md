## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
- **Original (Callahan/Craig) theory: energy meridians.** Craig's premise: "the cause of all negative emotions is a disruption in the body's energy system." Tapping acupuncture-meridian endpoints while you hold a distressing thought "clears" the disruption. Callahan's TFT used diagnosed, problem-specific point sequences ("algorithms"); Craig dropped them for a single universal sequence. Later Craig material, such as the "Unseen Therapist" and Optimal EFT, takes a spiritual turn.
- **Modern Tapping Solution / Clinical EFT framing: stress neurobiology.** The language has shifted from meridians to brain and body terms:
  - Stimulating acupoints is said to send a calming or "safety" signal to the amygdala, damping the fight-or-flight response.
  - Cortisol and stress biochemistry are said to fall. The site cites a 24% drop in Church et al. 2012 and a 43% drop in Stapleton et al. 2020.
  - fMRI work is cited showing reduced activity in pain and craving circuits.
  - Gene expression and epigenetic changes are claimed in pilot studies.
  - Feinstein (2025, *Front Psychol*) argues that acupoint stimulation produces deactivating signals to the amygdala, which then pair with exposure to the target memory and so drive memory reconsolidation and counter-conditioning.
- **Pain.** Chronic pain is presented as heavily shaped by stress, emotion and unresolved past events. Tapping on the sensation, and on the emotions, beliefs and memories linked to it, is said to lower the threat response and pain amplification. This is close to Sarno and neuroplastic-pain ideas.
- **Academic description.** In the Clinical EFT literature, EFT is "exposure plus cognitive restructuring plus somatic (acupressure) stimulation." Critics accept the first two components and dispute the third.

## 2. One mechanism or many?
**One unified mechanism.** Every target, whether a phobia, PTSD, pain, cravings or money beliefs, is treated as a stress/emotional charge attached to a specific memory, thought or sensation. The same tapping sequence applies to all. Craig called EFT a "universal healing aid" ("try it on everything"). The only differentiation is in the *content* of the setup statements and in the protocol emphasis:
- trauma: Tell the Story or Movie technique
- pain: tapping on sensation, then "chasing the pain" as it moves, then emotional contributors
- cravings: tapping while exposed to the food cue

Named illnesses are not given distinct pathophysiology. Clinical EFT guidelines do set a dose by severity (5 sessions for subclinical PTSD, 10 for PTSD) and advise referral when a patient doesn't respond.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
**The basic recipe** (Tapping Solution version):
1. Choose a specific target, such as a sensation, worry, memory or craving.
2. Rate its intensity from 0 to 10 (SUDS).
3. Say a setup statement three times while tapping the side of the hand (karate-chop point): "Even though [problem], I deeply and completely accept myself" (or "...I acknowledge how I feel").
4. Tap each of 8 further points about 5–7 times while repeating a reminder phrase: eyebrow, side of eye, under eye, under nose, chin, collarbone, under arm, top of head.
5. Re-rate the intensity and repeat rounds until it is low.
6. Optionally finish with a "positive" round.

Craig's original "Basic Recipe" also included finger points and the "9-gamut procedure" (eye movements, humming, counting), which were later dropped as optional.

**Specific techniques:**
- **Movie Technique / Tell the Story.** The person narrates a traumatic memory in small segments and taps whenever distress rises (graded exposure).
- **Tearless Trauma.** Work at a distance from the memory.
- **"Chasing the pain."** Tap on a physical sensation and follow it as it moves or changes.
- **Surrogate tapping.** Tapping on behalf of another person.
- **Optimal EFT / "Unseen Therapist."** Craig's later practice of inviting an inner, spiritual healing presence, with little or no tapping.

**Clinical EFT** (EFT Universe / EFT International certification) is a manualized version with 48 practices. Typical studies use:
- PTSD: 6–10 one-hour sessions.
- Chronic pain: a 6-week group program, live or self-paced online (Stapleton trials).

**Tapping Solution app.** Guided audio "tapping meditations" of about 5–20 minutes in categories such as anxiety, sleep, pain and relief, and weight. Also offers multi-day challenges, quick-relief sessions and a "Tapping Solution for Pain Relief" series. The pain book (2015) offers a 21-day-style program combining tapping on the sensation, on emotions (anger, fear, guilt) and on past traumas, plus belief work.

**NeuroTap Health.** Self-guided 10–20 minute audio sessions taken when distress or pain rises. It has two tracks, behavioral health (anxiety/depression) and chronic MSK pain, with outcomes tracked using Medicare's questionnaires. It is positioned as an adjunct between therapy visits and is not monitored in real time.

## 4. Roots and influences
- **Acupuncture / TCM meridian theory**, through Applied Kinesiology (George Goodheart) and John Diamond's Behavioral Kinesiology, which shaped Callahan.
- **Roger Callahan's Thought Field Therapy** (late 1970s–1980s), the direct parent.
- **NLP.** Craig trained in NLP and attributes some EFT elements to it, for example affirmation-style statements.
- **Exposure therapy and cognitive restructuring** (behaviorism, CBT). EFT includes imaginal exposure and reframing.
- **New Thought / affirmations.** "I deeply and completely accept myself" and later spiritualist elements in Optimal EFT.
- **Energy psychology movement.** Fred Gallo coined the term; ACEP formed about 1999. David Feinstein and Donna Eden.
- **Self-help / life coaching.** Hay House, Jack Canfield and Joseph Mercola promoted EFT.
- **Pain:** overlap with Sarno-style emotion-pain links.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- **Guidelines.** EFT is not recommended in NICE, APA or VA/DoD PTSD/depression guidelines. A UK-commissioned network meta-analysis for children and young people found no good evidence for EFT in PTSD (Mavranezouli et al. 2020, *J Child Psychol Psychiatry*). Proponents published their own "clinical guidelines" (Church et al. 2017 *Perm J*; Church et al. 2018 *Healthcare*), but these come from the same authors who promote and earn income from the method.
- **Health-system use.** There are pockets of use and research in NHS primary care and community settings in the UK, mainly Staffordshire University (Boath), and in some VA programs (Church's veteran trials recruited through VA clinicians). Some holistic-nursing literature promotes it (Rancour 2017).
- **Medicare (2026).** NeuroTap Health, founded and led by Nick Ortner, says Original Medicare beneficiaries with anxiety, depression or chronic MSK pain can use guided tapping at USD 0 cost through the **CMS ACCESS Model**. ACCESS is a 10-year CMMI model that began 5 July 2026. It pays participating organizations recurring *outcome-aligned* payments and allows technology-enabled care. "NeuroTap Health, Inc." appears on CMS's ACCESS accepted-participants list for the MSK and BH tracks, verified 2026-10-12. This is probably EFT's most significant mainstream financing to date. Caveats:
  - CMS pays for outcomes, not for EFT specifically.
  - NeuroTap cites only uncontrolled, within-session app data: 16.6M rated sessions from 868k users, with within-session d=1.21–1.66. The relevant manuscript, with MGH/Harvard co-authors, is under review. Its advisors include Tony Robbins and Maurizio Fava.
- **Private insurance.** No EFT-specific codes. A licensed therapist can bill standard psychotherapy codes while using EFT.

## 6. Evidence base
*The technique (EFT) has a substantial trial literature. The branded Tapping Solution app and NeuroTap have no published controlled trials. Most EFT trials are by a small group of proponents (Church, Stapleton, Feinstein, Boath) who disclose income from the method.*

**Pain and somatic RCTs and clinical trials (generic EFT)**
- **Stapleton et al. 2025, *Eur J Pain* 29:e4740** (PMID 39425257). RCT, N=147 adults with chronic pain, 90% female. A 6-week EFT program, either live group (the abstract says "in-person"; its significance statement says "online in-person") or self-paced online, against waitlist; per-protocol analysis, consistent with ITT. Pain severity and interference were lower than waitlist after treatment and stayed lower at 6 months. Somatic symptoms improved after treatment only. QoL improved. No effects on anxiety, depression or wellbeing. Live and self-paced versions did not differ. Single-site, proponent lab, waitlist control.
- **Stapleton et al. 2022, *Complement Ther Clin Pract*** (PMID 35933806). Uncontrolled trial, N=24 with chronic pain, 6-week online group EFT, resting-state fMRI before and after. Pain severity fell 21% and interference 26%. Medial prefrontal connectivity with PCC and thalamus decreased.
- **Bougea et al. 2013, *Explore* 9:91–99** (PMID 23452711). RCT, N=35 with frequent tension-type headache, EFT twice daily for 2 months vs standard care. Within-group reductions in headache frequency and intensity and in perceived stress. No change in cortisol. Independent group at the University of Athens.
- **Çuvadar, Guksu & Ateş 2026, *J Integr Complement Med* 32:453–464** (PMID 41313614). RCT, N=64 with endometriosis pelvic pain. Two 45–60 min EFT sessions, one per month, plus self-practised affirmations at least twice weekly, vs a breathing-exercise control. Pain VAS fell from 7.3 to 4.7 by month 2, with a significant group × time effect; SF-36 improved. Independent group in Turkey; no blinding.
- **Other independent trials.** A growing number of small, mostly nurse-led trials from Turkey, China and Indonesia test EFT for perioperative, mammography, IUD-insertion, dysmenorrhea and cancer pain and anxiety (e.g. Kaplan & Çelik 2025 *Support Care Cancer*; Lin et al. 2025 *Complement Ther Med*). A 2026 meta-analysis covers surgical pain and anxiety (Kara Söylemez & Uzun, *Explore*, PMID 42497566). These are typically unblinded with weak controls.
- **Fibromyalgia.** Brattberg 2008 (*Integrative Medicine*, Sweden) ran an online self-administered EFT RCT (N≈86) and reported improvements. It is not PubMed-indexed and was not verified this session.

**Stress, PTSD, anxiety and depression**
- **Church, Yount & Brooks 2012, *J Nerv Ment Dis* 200:891–896** (PMID 22986277). RCT, N=83 non-clinical adults, one 1-hour session of EFT vs supportive interview vs no treatment. Cortisol fell 24% with EFT vs 14% in each control, and distress also fell.
- **Stapleton et al. 2020, *Psychol Trauma* 12:869–877** (PMID 32162958). Replication, N=53. Cortisol fell 43% with EFT vs 20% with psychoeducation. EFT did not differ significantly from no treatment, and subjective distress did not improve. This is the source of the site's "43%" claim.
- **Church et al. 2013, *J Nerv Ment Dis* 201:153–160** (PMID 23364126). RCT, N=59 veterans with PTSD, 6 sessions of EFT vs standard-of-care waitlist. 90% of the EFT group no longer met PTSD criteria, vs 4% on waitlist. Single proponent lab; self-report PCL.
- **Church, Sparks & Clond 2016, *Explore*** (PMID 27543343). Pilot RCT, N=21 subclinical veterans.
- **Church et al. 2018, *Am J Health Promot*** (PMID 27520015). Pilot RCT, N=16 veterans, with gene-expression measures.
- **Meta-analyses (mostly by proponents):**
  - anxiety: Clond 2016 (14 RCTs, n=658; pre-post d=1.23 vs 0.41 for controls; PMID 26894319)
  - depression: Nelms & Castel 2016 (PMID 27843054); Seok & Kim 2024, independent, Korea Institute of Oriental Medicine (18 RCTs, g≈1.27; PMID 39518619)
  - PTSD: Sebastian & Nelms 2017 (PMID 27889444); Stapleton et al. 2023 (6 RCTs; g 1.38–2.51 vs inactive controls, −0.15 to 0.79 vs active controls; PMID 37637920); Chen et al. 2025, *Eur Arch Psychiatry Clin Neurosci* (PMID 40301160)
- **Is tapping an active ingredient?**
  - Church, Stapleton, Yang & Gallo 2018 (*J Nerv Ment Dis*, PMID 30273275): 3 dismantling studies, d≈0.47 favoring acupoints. A later corrigendum has been reported but was not verified in the fact-check.
  - Spielmans, Rosen & Spence-Sing 2020 (PMID 32740560) re-analysed the same 3 studies and found no specific effect (d=−0.38, p=0.12). They noted non-clinical samples, controls that were not bona fide therapies, and allegiance bias. The two sides exchanged further replies in 2022.
  - Gilomen & Lee 2015 (*J Behav Ther Exp Psychiatry*, PMID 25863484): an independent meta-analysis of 18 RCTs (n=921), mostly against waitlist. It found a moderate overall EFT effect (g=−0.66; −0.51 after removing outliers, with high heterogeneity) but concluded that, given the methodological shortcomings, it could not tell whether the effect comes from acupoint stimulation or from elements shared with other therapies.
- **Independent overviews.**
  - Ashoorion et al. 2026 (*J Mil Veteran Fam Health*, PMID 42534037) rated EFT for veterans' PTSD as moderate-quality evidence of benefit but a "very limited" evidence base.
  - Pfund, Boness & Tolin 2024 (*Front Psychol* commentary, PMID 38464621) and Boness, Pfund & Tolin 2024 (*J Psychother Integr*, PMID 39006869) rated the EFT meta-analyses "critically low" quality on AMSTAR-2 and called EFT an "unsinkable rubber duck."

**Gaps**
- No trials in ME/CFS, long COVID, POTS, MCS, IBS, FND or CRPS.
- Few comparisons against active, credible treatments such as CBT, prolonged exposure or EMDR, where effects are similar or smaller.
- Very few blinded or sham-controlled designs.
- Investigator allegiance is high.
- App usage data is uncontrolled.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory
- **1990s–2000s.** Craig's free EFT manual and emofree.com spread EFT online. Wikipedia dates the *EFT Handbook* to the late 1990s. By the mid-2000s Craig reported very large manual download numbers (unverified). Mercola promoted EFT heavily around 2005–2010.
- **2008–2013.** *The Tapping Solution* film (2008). Annual Tapping World Summit from 2010, an online event with hundreds of thousands of registrants according to company marketing (unverified). Online courses (2012) and the Tapping Solution Foundation (2013).
- **2013–2016.** Nick Ortner's *The Tapping Solution* (Hay House, 2013) became a NYT bestseller, followed by Jessica Ortner's weight-loss book (2014) and *The Tapping Solution for Pain Relief* (2015). EFT trials multiplied, and Clinical EFT certification grew (EFT Universe, AAMET → EFT International).
- **2018–2026.** The app launched in 2018. The company now claims 4M+ users, 32M+ completed sessions, 40k+ reviews (4.8 stars) and 190+ countries. The app page still says "over 17 million" meditations played; the two figures may count different things, so they do not show a growth trend. The "190+ countries" figure was not re-verified. PubMed output on EFT is rising (more than 40 pain/cortisol-related records, several 2025–26 meta-analyses), and new nurse-led trials are coming from Turkey and China. A new book, *REWIRED* (Ortners), is in pre-order in 2026. NeuroTap Health (Medicare ACCESS) launched in 2026.
- **Gary Craig** retired in 2025 per his site, which now lists his dates as "1940–2026" (death not independently confirmed in a fact-check search). Gabriëlle Rutten is his designated successor for "Official EFT."
- Reddit (r/EFTtapping) and Google Trends could not be checked this session. Based on general knowledge, interest in "EFT tapping" has been steady to rising since about 2015, with seasonal peaks.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **Implausible original theory.** There is no evidence that meridians exist or that point location matters (dismantling and sham-point studies). EFT appears in lists of fringe or pseudoscientific therapies: Lilienfeld 2003; Norcross et al. 2006 Delphi poll ("possibly to probably discredited"); Bakker 2013; McCaslin 2009.
- **Proponent-dominated literature.** Authors often disclose income from EFT. Critics cite allegiance effects, waitlist controls, non-clinical samples and the re-analysis dispute described above.
- **Commercial creep.** The app and books extend tapping to money, weight, relationships and "abundance," framed under one universal mechanism. The site cites "98 RCTs / 400+ studies" without separating them by quality.
- **Medicare inclusion** under an outcomes model, with evidence the company itself describes as uncontrolled, may draw scrutiny.
- **Craig's later turn.** His spiritual "Unseen Therapist" phase is a departure from the evidence-oriented Clinical EFT branch.
- **Possible benefits.** EFT is low-risk and cheap, and its exposure and cognitive components are plausible active ingredients. Self-administered EFT might deliver those benefits at scale.

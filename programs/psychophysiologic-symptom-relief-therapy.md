# Psychophysiologic Symptom Relief Therapy (PSRT)

*Compiled 2026-10-12 from the BIDMC press release (2021), the PSRT papers (Pain Reports 2021; J Pain Res 2023; Mayo Clin Proc Innov Qual Outcomes 2023; Front Pain Res 2024, read in full text via Europe PMC), ClinicalTrials.gov records (NCT02117921, NCT04039139, NCT04689646, NCT04854772), and an S4ME forum thread. Content paraphrased.*

> **Naming note.** The user's "PSPR (psychophysiological symptom reduction)" is taken here to mean **PSRT**, the Boston group program. No program called "PSPR" was found. PSRT is an academic research protocol, not a branded consumer product. In earlier trial registrations it was called "Mind Body Syndrome (MBS) Therapy," borrowing Howard Schubiner's term. *Please confirm this mapping.* (Fact-check note: "PSPR" may also refer loosely to the broader "psychophysiologic disorder (PPD)" approach promoted by the Psychophysiologic Disorders Association/Schubiner; PSRT is the only named program with "psychophysiologic symptom" in its title found in PubMed.)

## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
- **Core claim.** Nonspecific symptoms (pain, fatigue, dyspnea, brain fog, GI upset) are "psychophysiologic." They are somatic expressions of central nervous system processes driven by stress, repressed emotions, and "underlying conflicts and aversive affective states." Once set off, they are kept going by ongoing stressors.
- **Sarno lineage.** The model draws explicitly on John Sarno: pain without an organic cause reflects psychological processes, including repressed feelings such as hostility or rage. Real peripheral changes, such as muscle spasm, are allowed as part of the stress response.
- **Classical conditioning.** Neutral cues such as bending, sitting, walking or exercise become linked to symptoms through a "classical conditioning-like" process. This produces muscle tension, fear of movement (kinesiophobia) and avoidance, which keep the condition chronic. In long COVID, the symptoms that follow exertion are read as conditioned: activity without "knowing the underlying process" strengthens the link.
- **Fear and pain anxiety as mediator.** The 2023 secondary analysis found that the drop in pain-related anxiety (PASS-20) statistically mediated the drop in disability.
- **Long COVID framing.** The authors stop short of dismissing biological hypotheses such as microclots, mitochondrial dysfunction or capillary flow. They argue such findings would be compatible with a psychophysiologic process; their example is that embarrassment changes microcirculation. They name ongoing replicating virus with inflammation as the finding that would *falsify* their model. They also draw parallels with soldiers' syndromes from the Civil War era and with PTSD.
- **Exclusion of "organic" disease.** Candidates are screened by physicians. Patients with tumour, infection, fracture, ankylosing spondylitis or organ injury are excluded. In the long COVID study, so were patients aged over 60, those with ICU-level COVID, and those with myocarditis or lung injury.

## 2. One mechanism or many?
**A single unified mechanism, applied with condition-specific triggers.** The same three-part account (stress and repressed emotion, then a conditioned symptom response, then fear and avoidance) is offered for back pain and long COVID. The authors say it extends to fibromyalgia, IBS, headache, CRPS and other unexplained syndromes. What changes between conditions is only the *trigger set* targeted in desensitization: bending and sitting for back pain; walking, stairs and exercise for long COVID fatigue and dyspnea. The authors also note that the optimal content may differ between individuals. There are no subtype mechanisms. The key distinction is **organic versus psychophysiologic**, decided by medical screening and not by diagnosis label.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
**Structure (pilot, back pain):** one-on-one overview, then 12 weeks and **38 hours** in total. (The abstract says 36 hours.)
- **Weeks 1–4, the PSRT core:** two 2-hour group sessions a week, 16 hours over 8 sessions. Delivered by Donnino and Howard. Built around four components:
  1. **Psychophysiologic education.** Participants review their own pain history and learn the clinical features of psychophysiologic syndromes. The explicit goal is acceptance that the pain has psychological origins. Readings: Sarno, Schubiner and an anxiety workbook.
  2. **Desensitization / return to activity.** Participants use visual motor imagery of pain-triggering activities, such as picturing bending to pick up heavy books. That symptoms appear during imagery is used as *proof* that they are psychophysiologic. Imagery is repeated "with the knowledge that they were safe" until it no longer brings on pain. Graded return to pre-onset activity follows. In the long COVID cohort, activity was encouraged from week 2.
  3. **Emotional awareness and expression.** Expressive writing and journaling, discouraging emotional repression, and identifying stressors and conflicts. The approach is similar to EAET.
  4. **Stress reduction.** Introduced through the MBSR phase.
- **Weeks 5–12: a standard MBSR course** following the UMass curriculum guide (2017). Weekly 2-hour sessions plus a 6-hour retreat day and an orientation, 22 hours in total. It adds awareness of breath, body scan and sitting meditation, with a cognitive component and reminders of the PSRT elements. No new PSRT content is introduced in this phase.
- **Long COVID version:** 13 weeks and about 44 hours, delivered virtually. One session was taught by a former patient who had recovered using the approach. Detailed protocols are in each paper's supplementary appendix.
- **Self-care between sessions:** reading, journaling, visualization practice, graded activity and daily mindfulness practice.

## 4. Roots and influences
- **Sarno's TMS / mindbody syndrome:** the primary basis. *Healing Back Pain* is a core text.
- **Howard Schubiner's "Mind Body Syndrome"** (*Unlearn Your Pain*): Schubiner consulted on the protocol and trained the instructors, and the program's early name comes from his term.
- **Lumley & Schubiner's EAET:** source of the emotional awareness and expression component.
- **Kabat-Zinn's MBSR** (UMass, 1979): adopted wholesale as the second phase.
- **Pain science and education:** Moseley and Butler's *Explaining Pain* and fear-avoidance models.
- **Behaviorism / classical conditioning:** the explanatory frame for why triggers persist.
- **Exposure therapy:** imaginal and graded exposure, as in CBT for anxiety.
- **Pain Reprocessing Therapy (Ashar et al. 2022):** a contemporaneous sibling approach. The authors cite it as convergent but distinct; PSRT puts more weight on emotion and stress, and PRT on safety reappraisal.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- Housed at a major Harvard teaching hospital, BIDMC. Collaborators at Brigham & Women's Pain Management Center lend academic legitimacy.
- **No clinical service.** No dissemination, training program, guideline (NICE, ACP, VA/DoD) or insurer coverage was found. The 2021 BIDMC release said PSRT was not available to the public.
- **Insurance:** not reimbursable as PSRT. The MBSR component is generally self-pay elsewhere. Psychotherapy-style delivery could in principle be billed under standard CPT codes, but no such billing was found.

**Gaps and critiques**
- **Tiny samples.** The randomized PSRT arm had only 11 people.
- **Single site, same two instructors, developer-run trials.** There are no independent replications.
- **Selection.** Participants had to be "willing to consider a mind–body intervention," and the long COVID study was advertised as mind–body.
- **Blinding and expectation.** Only partial blinding: PSRT participants knew their treatment and all outcomes were self-reported. PSRT's explicit goal of persuading patients that symptoms are psychological raises the risk of response bias and demand effects.
- **Unequal dose.** PSRT got 38 hours and MBSR 22.
- **Effect over time.** Group differences narrowed by 26 weeks, with p values near 0.04.
- **Long COVID cohort.** Uncontrolled, with no follow-up beyond 13 weeks.
- **What the results cannot show.** The authors themselves concede that benefit would not prove the psychophysiologic premise.
- **Pending results.** The 126-person RCT is the decisive test and is not yet reported.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory
- **2014:** First BIDMC registration (NCT02117921). No public profile.
- **2019–2020:** Pilot RCT conducted.
- **Sept 2021:** Pilot published; BIDMC press release syndicated on Newswise. This is the peak media moment. (Uptake in Sarno/TMS communities is likely but was not verified here because the web-search budget ran out.)
- **2022–2023:** Long COVID preprint (Oct 2022) and paper (2023) drew attention, mostly critical, in long COVID and ME/CFS patient spaces such as S4ME. The mediation paper was published in 2023.
- **2024:** Rationale paper. **2026:** large RCT completed; an S4ME thread revisited the pilot (mid-2026).
- **Citation metrics (Europe PMC, Oct 2026; not re-verified by fact-check):** pilot ≈16 citations, long COVID paper ≈7, mediation paper ≈1.
- **Reach.** No consumer channel exists. Direct reach is roughly 100–150 enrolled participants across all trials (11 + 23 + ~2/3 of 126 + up to ~20 in the 2014 pilot), of whom only about 34 PSRT recipients have published outcomes. Indirect reach comes through citations in reviews of Sarno-lineage therapies alongside PRT and EAET.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **Long COVID patient groups.** These communities, along with ME/CFS-oriented critics, object to framing post-COVID fatigue and dyspnea as conditioned or psychophysiologic. They object in particular to encouraging activity, given post-exertional malaise. They also question the use of uncontrolled, self-reported outcomes. S4ME commenters called the back-pain pilot methodologically weak (partial blinding, expectation-driven measures, shrinking effect).
- **Psychogenic claims.** Critics of Sarno-derived approaches question claims that "repressed emotion" causes pain, and the use of symptom induction during imagery as "proof" of psychogenesis.
- **Funding and conflicts.** Funding comes from private tech-investor philanthropy (D'Angelo; O'Shaughnessy), not NIH. Authors report no competing interests, and no commercial product exists.
- **Lineage overlap.** PSRT overlaps heavily with Schubiner's PRT/EAET lineage. Its distinct contribution (Sarno education plus imagery desensitization plus EAET-style writing plus full MBSR) has not been dismantled experimentally.

## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
Yoga therapy has no single canonical disease model. IAYT defines it as applying yoga teachings and practices to help individuals move toward better health and well-being. Mechanisms are layered, with traditional and biomedical framings side by side:

- **Traditional framing.** The *kosha* (five sheaths: body, breath/energy, mind, wisdom, bliss) and *prana* (vital energy) models, from Viniyoga/Desikachar and the Krishnamacharya lineage. Illness is imbalance across these layers. Remedial-yoga writers such as Iyengar also made untested claims about specific asanas acting on specific organs.
- **Autonomic / stress regulation.** Slow breathing, extended exhalation and relaxation (savasana, yoga nidra) are said to shift the body from sympathetic dominance to parasympathetic/vagal tone, lower HPA-axis activation and cortisol, and dampen stress reactivity. This is the most common contemporary scientific framing. Some writers (e.g. Streeter's GABA/vagal hypothesis) add polyvagal or GABA-ergic language.
- **Musculoskeletal / sensorimotor.** Stretching, strengthening, posture and body-awareness retraining. In LBP trials this framing is close to therapeutic exercise.
- **Attention, interoception and acceptance.** Mindful movement builds interoceptive awareness, reduces catastrophizing and fear of movement, and increases acceptance. Examples are the coping components of Carson's "Yoga of Awareness" and pain re-appraisal.
- **Trauma / body-memory (TCTSY).** Following van der Kolk's *The Body Keeps the Score* (2014), trauma is described as held in the body as dysregulated arousal and loss of felt bodily agency. Yoga is said to rebuild interoception, choice, tolerance of sensation and affect regulation.
- **Inflammation.** Some reviews cite reductions in IL-6 and CRP as possible mediators. Mechanistic evidence is limited.

## 2. One mechanism or many?
**Transdiagnostic, with condition-specific tailoring.** The underlying model (mind-body imbalance, stress and autonomic regulation, body awareness) is the same across conditions. Practice is individualized:

- **LBP:** spine-focused asanas and core work (Viniyoga, Iyengar, hatha protocols).
- **Anxiety and depression:** breathing and relaxation emphasis; sometimes energizing practices for depression.
- **PTSD:** trauma-sensitive adaptations, including choice-based language, no hands-on adjustments and present-moment focus.
- **Fibromyalgia:** gentle poses plus coping skills.

Yoga therapy does not claim that the named illnesses share one brain cause, unlike ANS-retraining programs. The "central neurological" element enters through the stress/autonomic and interoception framings.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
- **Components:** asana (postures), pranayama (breath regulation: slow, diaphragmatic, alternate-nostril, extended exhalation), relaxation (savasana, yoga nidra/iRest), meditation and mindfulness, chanting, and yogic philosophy and lifestyle (diet, ethics, *svadhyaya* self-study).
- **Yoga-therapy process (IAYT model):** intake assessment covering structure, breath, mind and lifestyle; an individualized home practice, often 15–30 min/day; follow-up sessions to progress or modify.
- **Trial protocols (dose examples):**
  - Sherman 2011: Viniyoga, 12 weekly 75-min classes plus home practice.
  - Saper 2017: 12 weekly 75-min hatha classes plus 30 min/day home practice, then a maintenance phase.
  - Tilbrook 2011 ("Yoga for Healthy Lower Backs"): 12-class, gradually progressing program over 3 months, delivered by 12 teachers (PubMed abstract). Secondary summaries describe 75-min weekly classes with home practice; the Iyengar-style label is not in the abstract.
  - Yoga of Awareness (fibromyalgia): 8 weekly 120-min classes of gentle poses, meditation, breathing, yoga-based coping instruction and group discussion.
- **TCTSY:** 10-week, 1-hour weekly class (as in van der Kolk 2014). Teaching is invitational with choice-based language; there are no physical assists. It focuses on noticing sensations, making choices, taking effective action and rhythm. Facilitators train through a 300-hour TCTSY-F certification.
- **Practitioner training standard (IAYT):** at least 800 hours over at least 2 years, on top of 200-hr yoga teacher training, with a supervised practicum (IAYT accreditation standard; the 2012 guidelines specified at least 150 practicum hours).

## 4. Roots and influences
- Hindu yoga traditions: Patanjali's *Yoga Sutras* (about 2nd c. BCE–5th c. CE) and medieval hatha yoga texts such as the *Hatha Yoga Pradipika*, which itself makes healing claims.
- Modern postural yoga: Krishnamacharya; Iyengar's remedial yoga, which Newcombe likens to Ling's Swedish medical gymnastics; Desikachar's Viniyoga; Swami Kuvalayananda's Kaivalyadhama research institute (1924), the first lab studies of yoga physiology.
- Western relaxation and stress science: Benson's Relaxation Response, Selye.
- Mindfulness (MBSR includes hatha yoga), Ornish lifestyle medicine, polyvagal and trauma theory (TCTSY), and exercise/physiotherapy science.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- **Guidelines:**
  - ACP 2017 chronic LBP guideline (Qaseem et al., Ann Intern Med, PMID 28192789): recommends nonpharmacologic first-line treatments, including tai chi, yoga, motor-control exercise, MBSR and others (strong recommendation, low-quality evidence).
  - NICE NG59 (2016, LBP): recommends group exercise programs, mind-body forms among them (yoga not singled out as superior).
  - EULAR 2016 fibromyalgia (Macfarlane et al., Ann Rheum Dis 2017, PMID 27377815): weak recommendation for meditative movement therapies (qigong, yoga, tai chi); exercise was the only strong recommendation.
- **VA/military:** yoga is among the VHA-approved complementary and integrative health approaches (VHA Directive 1137, 2017) and part of the Whole Health program.
- **Hospitals:** widely offered in cancer centres and integrative medicine programs (e.g. Boston Medical Center group classes after Saper's trials).
- **Insurance:** yoga therapists are unlicensed (IAYT's C-IAYT is a voluntary credential), and there is no dedicated billing code. Coverage is therefore mostly indirect: VA, HSA/FSA, employer wellness programs, Medicare Advantage fitness benefits, or PT/OT billing yoga-informed therapeutic exercise (CPT 97110/97112/97530). A UK economic evaluation alongside Tilbrook (Chuang et al., Spine 2012, PMID 22433499) found £13,606 per QALY from the NHS perspective (72% probability of cost-effectiveness at £20,000/QALY) and yoga dominant from a societal perspective.

## 6. Evidence base
Trials test generic yoga protocols (Viniyoga, Iyengar, hatha, Yoga of Awareness), not an IAYT-branded program. IAYT certifies practitioners and does not run a trial program. The exception is TCTSY, a named protocol with its own RCT.

| Study | Design / N | Key result |
|---|---|---|
| Sherman KJ et al. 2011, *Arch Intern Med* 171:2019–26 (PMID 22025101) | RCT, 228 adults with chronic LBP: 12 wk Viniyoga vs conventional stretching vs self-care book | Yoga beat the book on function (RMDQ −2.5) and pain bothersomeness (−1.1) at 12 wk; function still better at 26 wk. **Yoga was not superior to stretching.** |
| Tilbrook HE et al. 2011, *Ann Intern Med* 155:569–78 (PMID 22041945) | Multicentre UK RCT, 313 adults with chronic/recurrent LBP: 12-class "Yoga for Healthy Lower Backs" vs usual care (both got an education booklet) | Better back function: RMDQ −2.17 at 3 mo (primary), −1.48 at 6 mo, −1.57 at 12 mo. **Back pain and general health similar between groups.** Only 60% attended adequately. Adverse events (mostly increased pain) 12/156 yoga vs 2/157 usual care. |
| Saper RB et al. 2017, *Ann Intern Med* 167:85–94 (PMID 28631003; NCT01343927) | 3-arm noninferiority RCT, 320 low-income, racially diverse adults: 12 wk yoga vs 15 PT visits vs education, plus 40-wk maintenance | Yoga noninferior to PT for function and pain. **Neither was superior to education** on primary outcomes. Yoga/PT reduced pain-medication use by about 21–22 points vs education. Gains held at 1 yr. |
| Carson JW et al. 2010, *Pain* 151:530–9 (PMID 20946990) | Pilot RCT, 53 women with fibromyalgia: 8-wk Yoga of Awareness vs waitlist | Improved FIQR symptoms, pain, fatigue, mood, catastrophizing and acceptance. Waitlist control only. |
| van der Kolk BA et al. 2014, *J Clin Psychiatry* 75:e559–65 (PMID 25004196; NCT00839813) | RCT, 64 women with chronic treatment-resistant PTSD: 10-wk trauma-sensitive yoga (TCTSY) vs women's health education | 52% of the yoga group no longer met PTSD criteria vs 21% of controls. Both groups improved; the control group relapsed after mid-treatment. Investigators were the program's developers (Emerson is a co-author). |
| Cramer H et al. 2013, *Clin J Pain* 29:450–60 (PMID 23246998) | Meta-analysis, 10 RCTs, n=967, chronic LBP | Short-term pain SMD −0.48 and disability SMD −0.59; strong short-term evidence, moderate long-term. |
| Wieland LS et al. 2022, Cochrane CD010671.pub3 (PMID 36398843) | Systematic review, 21 RCTs, n=2,223, chronic LBP | Compared with no exercise: small, **clinically unimportant** gains in function (RMDQ −1.69) and pain (−4.5/100); more adverse events (mainly increased back pain). Compared with other back exercise: little or no difference. No sham-yoga trials; all at high risk of performance and detection bias. |
| Cramer H et al. 2018, *Depress Anxiety* 35:830–43 (PMID 29697885) | Meta-analysis, 8 RCTs, n=319, anxiety | Small effect vs no treatment (SMD −0.43); large effect vs active comparators (SMD −0.86, few small trials). **No effect in DSM-diagnosed anxiety disorders**; effects only in people with elevated anxiety or non-DSM diagnoses. |
| Simon NM et al. 2021, *JAMA Psychiatry* 78:13–20 (PMID 32805013) | 3-arm RCT, 226 adults with GAD: 12 × 120-min group Kundalini yoga vs CBT vs stress education | Yoga beat stress education on acute response, but **yoga was not noninferior to CBT**; authors concluded CBT should remain first line. (Response rates 54.2% yoga, 70.8% CBT, 33.0% education, per abstract.) |

**Gaps and critiques:**
- No blinding or sham-controlled trials.
- Benefits for LBP are roughly equal to stretching/PT, which suggests nonspecific exercise and attention effects.
- Many small, low-quality trials, especially from India.
- Mechanistic mediators (HRV, cortisol, IL-6) are inconsistently measured.
- Essentially no trials in ME/CFS, long COVID, POTS, MCS or EHS.
- IBS evidence is limited to small trials: a systematic review (Schumann et al., Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol 2016, PMID 27112106; 6 RCTs, n=273) found benefits but made no recommendation because of major methodological flaws.
- In the largest psychiatric RCT (Simon 2021, GAD), yoga was inferior to CBT.
- Several leading investigators (Saper, Sherman, Cramer) have no commercial stake. The TCTSY trial was run by the program's developers.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory
- **US prevalence (NHIS):** adult yoga use 5.1% (2002), 6.1% (2007), 9.5% (2012) (NCHS NHSR 79) and 14.3% (2017, NCHS Data Brief 325). Yoga in America 2016 (Yoga Journal/Yoga Alliance) estimated 36.7M US practitioners. Back pain and stress are among the most common health reasons for practising.
- **Professionalization:** IAYT went from 185 directory members in 10 countries (1991) to more than 5,000 members in 50+ countries. It adopted training standards in 2012, announced its first 12 accredited programs in 2014 (24 by 2015, nearly 70 now) and began credentialing C-IAYTs in 2016.
- **Research volume:** PubMed records with "yoga" in title/abstract grew from 8 (1995) and 61 (2005) to 445 (2015), 637 (2020), 847 (2024) and 949 (2025) (E-utilities count, 2026-10-13). Saper 2017 and Cochrane 2017/2022 drew wide media coverage.
- **Culture:** van der Kolk's *The Body Keeps the Score* (2014; 248 weeks on the NYT paperback nonfiction list by 2023, #1 continuously from February 2021 for 34+ weeks) popularized trauma-sensitive yoga. Free YouTube yoga ("Yoga with Adriene", about 13M subscribers in 2025) drives large-scale self-practice. The COVID-19 pandemic accelerated online classes (2020–21). International Day of Yoga (UN, since 2015) and India's AYUSH promotion give global visibility.
- **Trend:** general-yoga Google interest peaked around 2019–2020 and has plateaued since (judgement; Google Trends not retrievable by automated query); clinical and trauma-informed yoga continues to grow modestly.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **Small, nonspecific effects:** Cochrane 2022 judged the LBP benefits clinically unimportant compared with no exercise and no different from other exercise.
- **Overclaiming:** organ-specific claims from Iyengar-era marketing, "detox" language, and claims for serious diseases lack evidence (Broad, *The Science of Yoga*, 2012; Jain).
- **Injury risk:** small but real musculoskeletal injuries, with rare reports of cervical artery dissection in extreme neck positions. Adverse events were more frequent than with no exercise in Cochrane 2022.
- **Regulation:** yoga therapy is unlicensed, so training and competence vary widely, and a tension exists between the yoga-teacher and allied-health professions.
- **Cultural appropriation and spiritual content:** critics question secularized "medical yoga" on one side and religious content in clinical settings on the other.
- **Trauma-sensitive yoga:** a 2019 systematic review (Nguyen-Feng et al.) found the evidence hampered by weak methodology. A 2021 meta-analysis of RCTs in women (Kysar-Moon et al., *Int J Yoga Therap*, PMID 33543258) found marginally significant to no effects on PTSD and depression.

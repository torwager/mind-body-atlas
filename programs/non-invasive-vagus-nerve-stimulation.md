## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)

- **Afferent vagal signalling to the brainstem.** About 80% of cervical vagal fibres are afferent. Stimulation through the skin at the neck (cervical) or the ear's auricular branch (tragus/cymba conchae) is proposed to drive the nucleus tractus solitarius. The NTS then projects to the locus coeruleus, raphe, parabrachial nucleus, hypothalamus, amygdala and insula.
- **Headache-specific mechanism (gammaCore).** nVNS is thought to suppress trigeminal nociceptive transmission and cortical spreading depression, and to lower glutamate in the trigeminal nucleus caudalis (animal data). In humans, fMRI shows that nVNS modulates the trigeminal-autonomic reflex (Möller 2020, *Neurology*). It also inhibits the nociceptive withdrawal reflex in healthy subjects (De Icco 2018).
- **Autonomic rebalancing.** Stimulation is said to raise parasympathetic ("vagal") tone and dampen sympathetic overdrive, with HRV (RMSSD) as the marketed biomarker. This is the main pitch for POTS, anxiety, sleep and "stress."
- **Cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway (Tracey's "inflammatory reflex").** Vagal efferent activity, through the splenic route and α7 nicotinic receptors, inhibits TNF/IL-6 release from macrophages. This is the basis for claims in long COVID, fibromyalgia, the COVID EUA and Nurosym's IL-6 claims. In POTS, Stavrakis' group also proposes reduced autoantibodies and inflammation.
- **Polyvagal "safety" framing.** The consumer brands, and especially the vibration wearables (Apollo's "safety signals," Sensate's "toning the vagus nerve"), borrow polyvagal-theory language: a nervous system "stuck" in fight-or-flight that can be shifted to a "rest-and-digest" state.
- **Caveat.** Whether low-intensity transcutaneous stimulation engages vagal fibres reliably is debated. No validated biomarker of target engagement exists. HRV and pupil responses are inconsistent across studies (Ingram 2026 review; Balan 2026).

## 2. One mechanism or many?

- **gammaCore (Rx).** Differentiated by indication. Each FDA clearance rests on condition-specific pathophysiology (trigeminovascular and trigeminal-autonomic) and condition-specific dosing. Trials separate episodic from chronic cluster headache, and the device worked in the first but not the second.
- **Consumer devices.** Essentially one unified mechanism. Low vagal tone, sympathetic dominance and inflammation are presented as the shared driver of stress, poor sleep, anxiety, gut symptoms, fatigue and "post-viral" symptoms. The marketing does not distinguish named illnesses, partly because "general wellness" regulatory status forbids disease claims. Nurosym hints at post-viral/long COVID ("persistent tiredness," "post-viral GI symptoms") without naming diseases. Patient communities fill in the conditions.
- **Academic taVNS research.** Uses condition-specific hypotheses (e.g., autoimmunity/inflammation in POTS, central sensitisation in fibromyalgia), but all rest on the same vagal-afferent and anti-inflammatory core.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies

| Device | Site and type | Typical dose (manufacturer or trial) |
|---|---|---|
| gammaCore / gammaCore Sapphire | Cervical, handheld; 5 kHz bursts at 25 Hz, user-adjusted intensity | Acute: one to several 2-min stimulations at onset. Prevention: 2 × 2-min stimulations twice daily (morning and night). Rx-only, with a refillable or rechargeable dose allotment. |
| Truvaga 350 / Plus | Same cervical platform as gammaCore, sold as wellness | 2-min sessions, morning and/or evening. Plus is rated for about 30,000 sessions over 3 years. |
| Pulsetto Lite / FIT | Neck band with bilateral cervical electrodes plus gel; app modes (Stress, Sleep, Calm, Focus, Release) | About 4-min sessions (up to about 10–20 min in some modes), 1–3×/day. Company pilot used twice daily for 4 weeks. |
| Nurosym ("AVNT" protocol) | Ear-clip auricular (tragus) electrode, wired controller | Trials used 30–60 min/day (e.g., 30 min 2×/day for 10–28 days). |
| Generic taVNS (TENS with ear clip) | Cymba or tragus | Research protocols range from 15 min to 4 h/day. |
| Apollo Neuro (margin) | Wrist or ankle vibration, app-selected "Vibes" | Worn continuously or in sessions; AI-timed during sleep. |
| Sensate (margin) | Chest-worn infrasonic vibration plus headphone soundscapes | 10–30 min sessions. |

Accompanying self-care. The consumer apps bundle breathing exercises, HRV tracking and sleep routines. Online "vagus nerve" content pairs the devices with free vagal manoeuvres (slow exhale breathing, humming/gargling, cold-water face immersion, singing). Long COVID and POTS users often combine devices with pacing, compression, fluids and salt, and HRV biofeedback.

## 4. Roots and influences

- **Implanted VNS.** Corning's carotid compression in the 1880s. Zabara's 1985 proposal for epilepsy, with the first human implant in 1988 (Cyberonics) and FDA approvals for epilepsy (1997) and treatment-resistant depression (2005). Vivistim paired VNS for stroke rehab (2021).
- **Auricular stimulation.** Auricular acupuncture/auriculotherapy (Nogier) and electroacupuncture; the auricular branch of the vagus (Arnold's nerve) as the target.
- **TENS and gate-control pain science** (Melzack & Wall, 1965), plus 1970s neuromodulation.
- **Biofeedback.** HRV biofeedback and resonance breathing (Lehrer, Gevirtz).
- **Polyvagal theory** (Porges, 1994 onward). It supplies the consumer "safety signalling / ventral vagal" vocabulary, especially for Apollo and Sensate.
- **Cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway** (Kevin Tracey, 2000 onward; also SetPoint Medical).
- **Selye/HPA stress physiology.** The "rest-and-digest vs fight-or-flight" framing.
- **Yogic breathing and pranayama.** Cited informally as "natural vagal toning."

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance

- **FDA (gammaCore).** De Novo clearance for acute episodic cluster headache (Apr 2017). 510(k) clearances followed for acute migraine (Jan 2018), adjunctive cluster prevention (Nov 2018), adult migraine prevention (Q1 2020) and hemicrania continua / paroxysmal hemicrania (2021). The migraine indications were extended to adolescents aged 12–17 (Feb 2021). A COVID-19 EUA was issued for gammaCore Sapphire CV (July 2020).
- **Veterans Affairs.** This is the main US channel. Rx gammaCore sales to the VA were US$4.7M in Q1 2025 (+22%), versus US$0.29M (−33%) from US commercial. 195 VA facilities had purchased it by Sept 2025, up from 166 a year earlier. Patients are referred through VA Prosthetics.
- **US commercial insurance.** Largely not covered; many US payers class it as investigational. US commercial Rx revenue is shrinking as cash-pay patients migrate to the cheaper Truvaga wellness version (Q2 2025 filing).
- **UK.** NHS England began funding gammaCore for treatment-refractory cluster headache in responders in April 2019 (Innovation & Technology Payment). NICE medtech guidance MTG46 (3 Dec 2019) supports its use alongside standard care, noting likely savings when a free 3-month trial screens for responders. From April 2021 it was rolled out nationally under the MedTech Funding Mandate (NHS estimated about 11,000 eligible patients). Selected NHS Scotland boards also fund it. Current 2026 MFM status was not confirmed: NICE and NHS England pages return 403 to automated fetches.
- **Guidelines.**
  - The IHS evidence-based guideline (Yuan et al., *Cephalalgia* 2025) gives a weak recommendation for gammaCore Sapphire in migraine prevention, and no recommendation for nVNS in acute migraine.
  - The Ontario Health HTA (2025) judged nVNS likely cost-effective for cluster prevention (about C$27k/QALY) but not for migraine prevention (about C$952k/QALY).
- **Long COVID, POTS, ME/CFS, fibromyalgia.** No guideline endorsement. Use is in research clinics (Mayo, Oklahoma, Leeds, German and Austrian post-COVID centres) and among self-purchasing patients.
- **Consumer devices.** Marketed as FDA-exempt "general wellness" products with no disease claims. They are not reimbursed, but several advertise HSA/FSA eligibility.

## 6. Evidence base

**Branded gammaCore (cervical nVNS), headache. Most trials were electroCore-sponsored or had company-affiliated authors.**

| Trial | Design, N | Key result |
|---|---|---|
| ACT1: Silberstein et al., *Headache* 2016;56:1317 | Double-blind sham RCT, acute cluster, n=150 (ITT 133) | Primary endpoint missed overall (26.7% vs 15.1%, p=.10). Episodic subgroup positive (34.2% vs 10.6%, p=.008); chronic subgroup not. |
| ACT2: Goadsby et al., *Cephalalgia* 2018;38:959 | Double-blind sham RCT, acute cluster, n=92 | Overall not significant (14% vs 12%). Episodic 48% vs 6% pain-free at 15 min (p<.01); chronic not. |
| PREVA: Gaul et al., *Cephalalgia* 2016;36:534 | Open-label RCT, adjunctive prevention, chronic cluster, n=97 | −5.9 vs −2.1 attacks/week (p=.02); ≥50% response 40% vs 8%. No sham. |
| EVENT: Silberstein et al., *Neurology* 2016;87:529 | Sham pilot RCT, chronic migraine prevention, n=59 | Safe; headache-day difference not significant (p=.56). |
| PRESTO: Tassorelli et al., *Neurology* 2018;91:e364 | Double-blind sham RCT, acute migraine, n=248 | Primary (pain-free at 120 min) missed: 30.4% vs 19.7%, p=.067. Significant at 30 and 60 min. |
| PREMIUM: Diener et al., *Cephalalgia* 2019;39:1475 | Double-blind sham RCT, episodic migraine prevention, ITT n=332 | Not superior to sham (−2.26 vs −1.80 days/month, p=.15). Positive only in a post hoc adherent subgroup. |
| PREMIUM II: Najib et al., *Cephalalgia* 2022;42:560 | Double-blind sham RCT, migraine prevention, stopped early for COVID, mITT n=113 | Primary missed (−3.12 vs −2.29 days, p=.23); ≥50% responder rate 45% vs 27% (p=.048). |
| Natelson et al., *Life Sci* 2021 | Double-blind sham RCT (gammaCore), 10 weeks, Gulf War Illness widespread pain, n=27 | Pain improved slightly in both arms; active nVNS no better than sham for pain or migraine; no further gains in open-label phase. |

**Syntheses.**
- Song et al., *Front Neurol* 2023 and Qian et al., *Front Neurol* 2026 (meta-analyses of migraine RCTs).
- de Coo et al., *Cephalalgia* 2019 (cluster meta-analysis): benefit in episodic but not chronic cluster headache.
- Pellow et al., *Scand J Pain* 2025 (systematic review of nVNS and HRV biofeedback in chronic pain; 9 nVNS studies, 6 in headache): nVNS reduces headache frequency, but evidence for other chronic pain (fibromyalgia, low back pain) is insufficient.
- Ontario Health HTA 2025: low to very-low certainty for most outcomes.

**Long COVID, POTS, fibromyalgia (generic or other-brand t/taVNS).**

| Study | Design, N | Key result |
|---|---|---|
| Stavrakis et al., *JACC Clin Electrophysiol* 2024;10:346 | Double-blind sham RCT, tragus stimulation 1 h/day × 2 months, POTS, n=26; NIH-funded, independent | Smaller orthostatic HR rise (17.6 vs 31.7 bpm, p=.01); lower autoantibodies and inflammatory markers reported. |
| Zheng et al., *Front Neurol* 2024 | Single-arm pilot (Casa Colina), auricular tVNS (device brand not named in abstract) 30 min 2×/day × 10 days, long COVID women, n=24 | Improvements in cognition, anxiety, depression and sleep. No control. |
| Gierthmuehlen et al., *Neurol Ther* 2026 (COVIVA) | Single-blind 3-arm RCT (sham vs sub-threshold vs above-threshold taVNS, tVNS-L device, 25 Hz), 4 h/day × 4 weeks, long COVID fatigue, n=45 | High adherence; all arms improved on MFI-20; **active not superior to sham**. |
| Ganesh et al. (Mayo), *J Prim Care Community Health* 2026 | Pilot RCT, handheld cervical tcVNS 2 min 3×/day vs no-VNS (no sham) control × 12 weeks, long COVID, n=18 (8 vs 10) | Trends on PHQ-9, PROMIS-F and COMPASS-31; not significant. |
| Lommano et al., *Joint Bone Spine* 2025/26 | Single-arm pilot, Nurosym 30 min 2×/day × 28 days, fibromyalgia, n=25 | COMPASS-31, FIQR (69→62), painDETECT and CSI improved. No control. |
| Balan et al., *J Clin Med* 2026 (systematic review, long COVID taVNS) | 5 studies, n=154 | Very-low certainty. In the best-powered RCT (Percin et al.), sham beat active on fatigue despite an HRV increase. |

**Gaps and critiques.**
- Headache efficacy is real but modest and inconsistent. Most of the RCT base is industry-sponsored, and several primary endpoints failed.
- Blinding is hard because active stimulation produces sensation and muscle twitch. Sham devices in the electroCore trials delivered low-frequency stimulation, which some argue may itself be active.
- Consumer brands (Pulsetto, Truvaga, Apollo, Sensate) cite company pilots, mostly unpublished or uncontrolled. These are not trials of disease outcomes.
- No RCT exists for ME/CFS. Long COVID sham-controlled trials so far are null.
- One nVNS (gammaCore) Parkinson's paper (Mondal et al., *NPJ Parkinsons Dis* 2021) was retracted in 2022; a re-analysed version was later published in *Front Aging Neurosci* 2023.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory

- **Before 2017.** Low visibility: a niche headache device in Europe (CE mark) and US trials.
- **2017–2018.** FDA clearances for cluster and migraine. electroCore IPO in 2018 raised about US$78M.
- **2020.** COVID EUA and a pandemic-era surge of interest in "vagus nerve" and inflammation.
- **2021–2023.** Long COVID and POTS patient communities adopt tVNS. Pulsetto is founded (2021). Wellness and social-media content on "vagal toning" grows sharply, #vagusnerve passed 64 million TikTok views by June 2022 (*NYT*; later press reports 75 million), and "vagus nerve exercise" content is reported at 185 million+ views. Truvaga launches (Dec 2022).
- **2024–2025.** Consumer growth outpaces Rx:
  - Truvaga sales rose 187% year on year in Q1 2025.
  - electroCore's FY2025 revenue was US$32.0M (+27%), with general-wellness sales $5.5M (+97%). It guided to about 30% growth for 2026 (March 2026 release).
  - Reddit: subreddit sizes could not be retrieved (Reddit returns 403 to automated requests, and there are no Wayback captures of r/VagusNerve). GummySearch (2025) listed r/tVNS at about 960 members and r/Nurosym at about 880, so the device-specific communities are small.
  - Pulsetto claims over 330,000 customers and 20 million stimulations (company figures).
  - Apollo claims over 150,000 customers.
  - Academic output on taVNS in long COVID and fibromyalgia rose steeply (about 55 PubMed hits for tVNS × long COVID/POTS/fibromyalgia/CFS, most since 2023).
- **2026.** Continued consumer sales. The research narrative is cooling as sham-controlled long COVID trials return null results.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms

- **Wellness loophole.** Consumer devices make implicit disease claims (post-viral fatigue, inflammation, anxiety) under "general wellness" status, without disease-endpoint trials. Their marketing statistics (e.g., "56% less stress," "78% IL-6 reduction") are company-reported, with no published methods.
- **Target engagement.** Whether consumer-grade neck bands or ear clips at comfortable intensities actually activate vagal afferents is uncertain.
- **Overextended polyvagal claims.** Applying polyvagal-theory language to vibration devices that do not stimulate the vagus electrically is a further step removed.
- **Industry dominance.** electroCore sponsored or co-authored the headache trial base. Several pivotal trials missed primary endpoints, and approvals relied on secondary or subgroup results.
- **Cost-effectiveness.** Poor for migraine prevention (Ontario HTA).
- **Patient-community risk.** In long COVID and POTS, patients sometimes buy multiple devices on the strength of anecdote, while controlled trials (Gierthmuehlen 2026; Percin et al.) are null against sham.

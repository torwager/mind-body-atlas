## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
- **Disorder of gut–brain interaction.** IBS is framed in the Rome IV sense: symptoms are real and arise from miscommunication along a bidirectional gut–brain axis, not from a "purely psychological" cause.
- **Visceral hypersensitivity / signal amplification.** Gut nerves become oversensitive. Normal digestive activity then sends signals that the brain reads as pain or urgency, and an overactive gut–brain loop amplifies them (the site uses an "alarm" analogy). The site does not use the term "central sensitisation."
- **Stress and arousal as amplifiers.** Stress, anxiety and the threat response worsen gut signalling and motility. Symptom-focused anxiety, catastrophising and avoidance are named as amplifiers, and these are the CBT-informed targets.
- **How hypnosis is said to work.** Focused attention, deep relaxation and gut-specific imagery and suggestions are said to "calm" or normalise gut signalling and visceral sensitivity directly. CBT is said to work instead on the thoughts and behaviours around symptoms. The mechanistic claims are generic to the GDH literature (Whorwell): reduced visceral sensitivity, changes in central pain processing, and normalised motility. The company presents no app-specific mechanistic data.
- Dietary triggers (FODMAPs) are acknowledged as a parallel, peripheral route. Company abstracts report similar outcomes in users who identify food triggers and users who identify stress triggers.

## 2. One mechanism or many?
**Mostly unified, with condition-specific emphasis.** The programme is disorder-specific: it is built for IBS/DGBI, not a transdiagnostic "one illness" model like ANS Rewire or DNRS. Within that scope it uses a single gut–brain-axis/visceral-hypersensitivity model, with the same protocol for IBS-D, IBS-C and IBS-M. There are no subtype-specific mechanisms beyond some symptom-focused content. Mindset Health's sister apps apply the same general recipe (hypnosis/CBT plus a brain-body explanatory model) to other conditions: hot flashes (Evia, with Gary Elkins), anxiety/depression (Claria, with Michael Yapko) and back pain (Relio, "pain reprocessing", with NeuRA). Each uses a condition-specific rationale rather than one cross-disorder mechanism.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
- **Core:** 42 daily gut-directed hypnotherapy audio sessions (~15 min each) over 6 weeks. The sessions use relaxation induction, focused attention, gut-focused imagery (e.g. calming, soothing and regulating the gut) and post-hypnotic suggestions. The script is adapted from Peters' Monash GDH work, which descends from Whorwell's Manchester protocol (7–12 weekly face-to-face sessions), compressed into daily self-guided audio.
- **CBT-informed psychoeducation:** short daily lessons on the gut–brain axis, stress, symptom-related thoughts and avoidance, diet and gut microbiota. Mahana's IBS-CBT content is being integrated after the 2025 acquisition.
- **Breathing:** diaphragmatic breathing exercises and a "flare-up toolkit." A 2026 Mayo-led DDW abstract reports added benefit in users with more severe baseline symptoms.
- **Monitoring:** a 30-second daily check-in, plus weekly validated symptom measures (GI symptom VAS, IBS-SSS-type items, pain, bloating) and anxiety/depression scales. Progress can be shared with a clinician.
- **Clinical safety layer:** users are screened and escalated to medical follow-up when needed (red flags). Diagnosis stays with the user's own clinicians.
- **Dose:** ~15 min/day for 6 weeks, then maintenance listening with continued access.

## 4. Roots and influences
- **Hypnosis:** Mesmer, then Erickson-style clinical hypnosis, then **Whorwell's gut-directed hypnotherapy** (Manchester, 1984 *Lancet* RCT). Palsson's North Carolina scripted protocol (2000s) showed that scripted and audio delivery can work.
- **Monash University FODMAP/IBS group** (Gibson, Muir, Halmos), including Peters et al. 2016's GDH-vs-low-FODMAP RCT.
- **CBT for IBS** (Lackner; Drossman; Rome Foundation "brain-gut behaviour therapies").
- **Relaxation Response / diaphragmatic breathing.**
- **Rome Foundation DGBI framework**: the gut–brain axis and visceral hypersensitivity.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- **Guidelines.** Gut-directed hypnotherapy for IBS is supported by ACG (2021, conditional), BSG (2021) and Rome Foundation guidance. The 2025 AGA Institute IBS quality indicators (Hung KW et al., *Gastroenterology* 2025, PMID 39818650) list brain-gut behaviour therapies as integral to IBS care and acknowledge digital delivery as a scalable option. The vendor's claim that Nerva in particular is endorsed is its own framing; the guidelines endorse the technique.
- **Clinicians.** The company reports 20,000+ referring clinicians worldwide, including 8,000+ in the US. Researchers at Monash, Mayo Clinic, Mount Sinai and Baylor have published or presented analyses of Nerva data.
- **Payers.**
  - US: from 5 July 2026, participation in the CMS Innovation Center **ACCESS Model** gives eligible Original Medicare beneficiaries no-cost access, and referring Part B clinicians can bill a co-management payment. US patients aged 65+ without coverage can get company-funded access. The company markets to Medicare Advantage plans, employers and brokers, but did not name specific plans.
  - Australia: **HIF** private health fund since March 2025. The company proposes dietitian-led Chronic Disease Management Plan (CDMP) pathways.
  - Otherwise: self-pay, HSA/FSA.
- **Regulatory.** Not FDA-cleared; the site states it has not been evaluated by FDA, TGA or MHRA. Mahana's IBS app was FDA-authorised as a prescription digital therapeutic. Nerva acquired "key assets" of Mahana Therapeutics (announced 8 May 2025; Mahana's prior venture funding, reported elsewhere as >US$80M, was not verified here) and is folding Mahana's CBT content into Nerva. Terms were undisclosed, and the page does not say whether the FDA authorisation transferred.
- **PHTI.** Nerva's site quotes a March 2026 PHTI report saying Nerva had the strongest evidence for IBS. The public summary of PHTI's *Virtual GI Care Solutions* assessment (released 3 Mar 2026; v1.2, 6 Oct 2026) names only Cylinder, Digbi, Ayble, Oshi and Salvo, so the quote could not be confirmed. It may come from the full report.

## 6. Evidence base
**Trials of the branded app**

|---|---|---|---|

**Conference abstracts (not full peer-reviewed papers)**, all real-world and uncontrolled:
- ACG 2022: compliance.
- *Am J Gastroenterol* 2023 supplement (n≈18k): anxiety and depression fell from moderate to mild.
- JPGN Reports 2024, ESPGHAN abstract: adolescents.
- DDW cost/productivity abstract (n>17k): estimated 6-month costs fell from US$1,259 to $502.
- JGH 2026 supplement abstracts (n=76k, 26k, 132k): comorbid anxiety, trigger type, age ≥65.
- DDW 2026 (Mayo, Mount Sinai, Monash; n up to 194,879 over 2019–2025): 45% of adherent vs 34% of non-adherent users had clinically meaningful pain reduction.

**Foundational (generic GDH, not the app)**
- Peters SL et al., *Aliment Pharmacol Ther* 2016;44:447–59 (PMID 27397586). RCT, n=74: GDH, low-FODMAP diet or both. Overall symptoms improved about equally in all three (≈72% responders), with no additive effect. GDH was better for trait anxiety and depression.
- The broader face-to-face GDH literature (Whorwell 1984 onward; Palsson; Lindfors 2012; meta-analyses) supports GDH for IBS at moderate certainty.

**Gaps and critiques**
- Only one RCT. It was run by the content developers (Peters co-developed Nerva). Conflicts of interest were not detailed in the abstract.
- The control shared the same app shell and psychoeducation. This isolates the hypnosis component but does not test against waitlist, usual care or face-to-face GDH. Participants were not blind to condition.
- The 63% responder rate in the control arm shows large non-specific/placebo and psychoeducation effects.
- 90% of participants were female. Outcomes were self-reported.
- Real-world completion is low: 9% in 2019–20, and 31% persistence in 2022.
- The real-world analyses are uncontrolled, often company-funded, completer-biased and based on self-reported IBS without confirmed diagnosis.
- An independent commentary (Hasan, Ballou, Keefer, Vasant, *Neurogastroenterol Motil* 2023;35(4):e14554, PMID 36847206) welcomed app delivery but stressed the low adherence in Peters 2023 and called for controlled comparison with face-to-face GDH.
- A 2025 network meta-analysis of behavioural therapies for IBS (Thakur, Khasawneh, Moayyedi, Black, Ford, *Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol* 2025;10:1075-88, PMID 41077057) gives the wider context for GDH and digital therapies. Other digital GDH trials concern different products, not Nerva: Berry et al. 2023 (*Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol*, PMID 37391055) tested Regulora vs muscle relaxation, and Wang et al. 2025 (*Clin Transl Gastroenterol*, PMID 39835688) tested a Mayo-owned bloating protocol.
- No head-to-head comparison with other digital therapeutics (e.g. Mahana Parallel, Zemedy, Cara Care) or with in-person therapy.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory
- **2019:** Mindset Health founded in Melbourne; app sold from about June 2019. By April 2020, ~2.8k users with self-reported IBS had started it.
- **2022:** ~15k users in nine months (Jan – Sep 2022). The ACG 2022 abstract was presented that year.
- **2023–24:** First peer-reviewed papers (NGM 2023; *Digital Health* 2024). RCT published online June 2024.
- **2025:** RCT in print in *AJG* (Feb). HIF fund partnership in Australia (Mar). Mahana assets acquired (May). The cumulative real-world dataset reached ~195k users (2019–2025).
- **2026:** PHTI report (Mar). Multiple DDW and JGH abstracts. CMS ACCESS Model participation (Jul). 300,000+ users and 20,000+ referring clinicians (Sep 2026). App Store rating 4.6 from ~32.6k ratings. Mindset Health has expanded to Evia, Claria and Relio (350k+ users across apps, "30M+ covered lives").
- **Not verified this session:** Reddit/forum sentiment, YouTube/podcast reach and Google Trends. Searches were exhausted, and reddit.com and archive.org could not be fetched. Nerva is commonly discussed on r/IBS alongside the Calm Gut app and Zemedy, but this impression was not verified here.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **Mild controversy.** Gut-directed hypnotherapy is a guideline-endorsed, mainstream therapy, and Nerva makes no cross-disorder or "cure" claims.
- **Marketing outruns the evidence in places.** Examples: "backed by 17 studies," of which most are uncontrolled abstracts; responder percentages quoted without the 63% active-control rate; and a PHTI quote not visible in PHTI's public summary.
- **Low adherence and attrition** in real-world use, and selection bias in completer analyses.
- **Independence.** The trial and analyses were authored by the content developers or company-funded advisers.
- **Regulation.** It is sold direct-to-consumer as a wellness or "behavioural" programme without FDA review, while the company pursues reimbursement.
- **Possible missed diagnoses.** Users with self-reported IBS may have undiagnosed organic disease (coeliac, IBD, colorectal cancer). The company says it screens and escalates.

## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
- **The "downward spiral" / allostatic model of pain and addiction.** Chronic pain and long-term opioid use are thought to reinforce each other through the stress and reward systems of the brain:
  - Pain and negative affect drive opioid use (negative reinforcement).
  - Repeated opioid exposure produces **hedonic dysregulation**: an allostatic shift in which drug-related cues gain salience (attentional bias, cue-reactivity, craving) while natural rewards lose value (anhedonia, blunted positive affect).
  - This shift then sustains pain, distress and misuse.
- **Three interlocking targets:**
  1. *Attention:* automatic attentional bias toward pain and drug cues, and habitual "autopilot" behaviour.
  2. *Appraisal:* catastrophizing and threat appraisal of pain. Garland calls this the "attention–appraisal–emotion interface."
  3. *Reward:* deficient positive affect and natural-reward responsiveness, and craving.
- **Interoception and autonomic regulation.** Mindfulness is proposed to shift processing from evaluative/affective to sensory-discriminative ("mindful reinterpretation of pain sensations"). It is also proposed to increase interoceptive awareness and heart-rate-variability-indexed self-regulation, and to engage frontal-midline theta. These are presented as mechanistic biomarkers (Hudak et al. 2021, *Neuropsychopharmacology*; Garland et al. 2020, *Am Psychol*).
- **Positive psychological processes.** Savoring is held to "restructure reward processing" from drug back toward natural rewards (EEG LPP/P300 evidence: Garland et al. 2019 *Sci Adv*; 2025 *JAMA Psychiatry*). Positive affect, meaning and self-transcendence are treated as mediators of reduced misuse risk.
- **Framing.** MORE stays within mainstream pain and addiction neuroscience. It does **not** claim pain is wholly "brain-generated" or that structural pathology is irrelevant, and it is used alongside medications (opioid therapy, methadone, buprenorphine).

## 2. One mechanism or many? (does it differentiate between named illnesses)
**Mostly unified, with condition-specific emphasis.** MORE is marketed as transdiagnostic ("complex, overlapping health and mental health conditions"): one set of neurocognitive targets (attention, appraisal, reward/hedonic regulation, stress reactivity) spans addiction, pain and distress. The emphasis does vary:
- **Pain:** reinterpretation of sensations and catastrophizing.
- **Opioid misuse:** craving, cue-reactivity and natural-reward deficits.
- **PTSD symptoms:** reappraisal-mediated emotion regulation (Parisi et al. 2023).

It does not address the fatigue or "sensitivity" syndromes in this survey (ME/CFS, POTS, MCS/EHS, long COVID, "chronic Lyme", mould) and makes no claims about them. Fibromyalgia appears only as a registered trial (University of Fribourg, NCT04451564).

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies (concrete; components, structure, duration, dose)
**Standard dose:** 8 weekly 2-hour groups of about 6–12 patients, led by a trained clinician (social worker, psychologist, nurse, etc.). Patients are asked for about 15 min/day of audio-guided home practice and brief "3-minute" micro-practices before opioid dosing or when craving or pain spikes.

Three core skill sets:
1. **Mindfulness**
   - Mindful breathing.
   - Body scan.
   - "Mindfulness of pain": deconstructing pain into heat, tightness, tingling and similar sensations, the "zooming in/out" technique that underlies *mindful reinterpretation of pain sensations*.
   - Mindful awareness of craving: urge surfing and looking for the "choice point."
   - Recognising automatic habits ("autopilot").
   - "STOP"-style brief practices before taking opioids.
2. **Reappraisal (cognitive)**
   - Mindful reappraisal: decentring from thoughts, then reframing stressors and pain to find meaning, growth or benefit. This draws on CBT restructuring, but is entered from a mindful, decentred stance.
3. **Savoring**
   - Deliberately attending to the pleasant sensory features of natural rewards (nature, connection, accomplishment) and amplifying the positive affect they produce.
   - Ends with self-transcendent practice (sense of connectedness).

**Other session content:**
- Psychoeducation on the pain–addiction downward spiral and on safe opioid use.
- Mindful decision-making.
- Plans for coping with relapse and with stress.

**Variants:**
- One MORE: a single 2-h session.
- Brief MORE: 4–5 sessions for primary care.
- MORE-VR: virtual-reality delivery.
- Zoom MORE plus wearable-triggered just-in-time practice.
- An integrated MORE + physical therapy protocol.
- Adjuncts to methadone/buprenorphine treatment and to ketamine-assisted therapy (trials registered).

## 4. Roots and influences (prior practices/traditions it draws on)
- **Buddhist insight meditation, via MBSR/MBCT.** Mindful breathing, body scan and decentring come from Kabat-Zinn's MBSR and from Segal, Williams and Teasdale's MBCT, including the "3-minute breathing space."
- **Mindfulness-Based Relapse Prevention** (Bowen, Marlatt): urge surfing and relapse prevention.
- **CBT (Beck/Ellis):** cognitive reappraisal and restructuring.
- **Positive psychology** (Fredrickson's broaden-and-build; Bryant and Veroff on savoring): savoring and positive affect. Garland's mindful positive-reappraisal model is built on broaden-and-build.
- **Allostasis theory and reward-deficiency models of addiction** (Koob and Le Moal): the hedonic dysregulation construct.
- **Pain science:** fear-avoidance and catastrophizing models, and sensory–affective dissociation of pain.
- **Hypnosis:** Garland is an ASCH consultant and has tested hypnotic suggestion alongside mindfulness (2017 *JGIM*). It is not a core MORE component.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- **Health systems.** Essentia Health (MN/WI/ND) has offered MORE as an insurance-reimbursable service via group medical visits since January 2021. The developer's site lists training at Johns Hopkins, Mount Sinai, Kaiser Permanente, the Philadelphia VAMC and the Ralph H. Johnson VAMC (Charleston). Garland also held an affiliation with the VA Salt Lake City Whole Health Flagship site, and military medical centers take part in DoD-funded trials. NIH, DoD and Department of Defense–funded trials have embedded it in primary care, methadone clinics, military pain care and stepped-care SMART trials, e.g. NCT04172038 (N=850, Military Health System) and NCT03859713 (Optimize, N=749).
- **VA.** A developer blog post (1 Oct 2025) says MORE was "recognized" by the U.S. VA, citing the VA Whole Health "Meditation" professional-resources page and a VA literature review. When checked (Oct 2026), that VA page did not mention MORE by name, so the claim could not be independently confirmed. MORE is **not** named in VA/DoD clinical practice guidelines, NICE guidance or CDC opioid guidelines. The 2022 CDC guideline endorses mindfulness/CBT-type nonpharmacologic therapies generically.
- **Insurance.** There is no dedicated billing code. Reimbursement is indirect, through generic psychotherapy or HBAI codes, depending on the clinician's licensure and the setting.
- **Economics.** Wilson & Garland (2026, *Value Health*) estimated an incremental cost-effectiveness ratio of about USD 116 per averted case of opioid misuse and a 12:1 benefit-to-cost ratio, based on the JAMA IM 2022 trial. This analysis is co-authored by the developer.

## 6. Evidence base
Randomized trials **of the branded program** (all verified via PubMed/Europe PMC; ★ = developer not an author):

|---|---|---|
| Garland EL, Gaylord SA, Boettiger CA, Howard MO. *J Psychoactive Drugs* 2010;42(2):177-92 | Pilot RCT, alcohol-dependent adults in a therapeutic community, mindfulness training (MORE precursor) vs support group; N=53 | Reduced stress and thought suppression; better physiological recovery from alcohol cues; altered attentional bias. |
| Garland EL et al. *J Consult Clin Psychol* 2014;82(3):448-59 (NCT01505101) | Stage-1 RCT, opioid-treated chronic pain, MORE vs support group, 8 wk; N=115 | Greater reductions in pain severity (p=.038) and interference (p=.003), maintained at 3 mo. | Li W, Garland EL, McGovern P et al. *Psychol Addict Behav* 2017;31(4):393-402 | Stage-1 RCT, internet gaming disorder; N=30 | Reduced gaming disorder symptoms and craving vs support group. |

**Synthesis.** Parisi A, Roberts RL, Hanley AW, Garland EL. *Mindfulness* 2022;13:2396-2412 (PROSPERO CRD42022319006) pooled 8 RCTs (N=816). It found small-to-moderate effects on addictive behaviour (SMC −0.54), craving (−0.42), opioid dose (MC −17.95 mg), chronic pain (SMC −0.60) and psychiatric symptoms (−0.34). The authors concluded MORE "should now be disseminated broadly". Garland (2024, *Br J Clin Pharmacol*) cites 13 completed RCTs with >1,300 patients.

**Gaps and critiques:**
- Nearly every trial includes Garland or his close collaborators (Hanley, Cooperman, Froeliger), so the meta-analysis is developer-authored. The Wexler 2024 sciatica trial is the main fully independent RCT, and its primary outcome (disability) was null.
- Comparators are mostly supportive groups, which control for time and attention but not for an active, credible skills-based treatment. The exceptions are group CBT (2016) and TAU.
- Self-report outcomes are not blinded, and attrition is substantial (≈35–40%).
- The "nearly three times as effective as standard therapy" claim (the site ties it to the JAMA IM 2022 misuse result, where remission was 45.0% vs 24.4%, i.e. about 1.8-fold, OR 2.06) and the percentage reductions on the developer's site (e.g. craving −50%, pain −24%) are promotional summaries, not pooled estimates.
- No trials in the fatigue/sensitivity syndromes covered in this review.

Registered but not yet reported (as of Oct 2026): fibromyalgia (Fribourg), heroin addiction (Mount Sinai, N=157, completed), and several large military SMART trials.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory
- **2008–2013, origin.** The pilot ran in 2008 (Garland's doctoral and postdoctoral work at UNC/FSU). The treatment manual was published by NASW Press in 2013 as *Mindfulness-Oriented Recovery Enhancement for Addiction, Stress, and Pain*.
- **2014–2019, growth.** NIH-funded trials ran at the University of Utah, and publications with MORE in the title rose from 1/yr (2012) to 6 (2019). Live 2-day trainings cost USD 500 (2015); about 475 clinicians had been trained by ~2020.
- **2020–2022, scale-up.** The JAMA IM RCT was published in March 2022 and widely reported in the medical press. Essentia began billable delivery in 2021. In 2021 Garland was named "most prolific mindfulness researcher" in a bibliometric analysis (developer blog). NIH HEAL Initiative funding followed.
- **2023–2026, consolidation.** Nature Mental Health (2023), AJP and JAMA Psychiatry (2024) and JAMA Psychiatry (2025) papers were published, and the Guilford manual followed in July 2024. More than 1,100 clinicians have been trained. Coverage on CNN/Sanjay Gupta's *Chasing Life* came in Aug 2025, and the developer cites a VA mention in Oct 2025. In 2026 an online certification institute with a companion app and an economic evaluation followed. Europe PMC lists 58 MORE-titled papers (8 in 2026) and 352 full-text mentions; ClinicalTrials.gov lists 38 registered studies (counts from the original compilation, not re-run during fact-check), several with N=300–850.
- **Consumer/social media.** Visibility is minimal. There is no subreddit and no app-store consumer product, and MORE is rarely discussed on r/ChronicPain or TMS/neuroplastic-pain forums compared with PRT or Curable. Its reach is through clinicians and trials, not self-help.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- No public controversy. The main scholarly caveats are developer allegiance (most trials, the meta-analysis and the cost-effectiveness study are from the developer's group); active-but-nonspecific comparators; attrition; and mixed findings for some primary outcomes (misuse not different overall in the AJP 2024 trial, the JAMA Psychiatry 2024 primary effects significant only after adjustment, and the independent sciatica trial null on disability).
- Marketing language ("nearly three times as effective") goes beyond what the trials show, and the VA "recognition" claim could not be verified on the VA page cited.
- Its scope is narrower than the other programs in this review: MORE is an add-on for pain, opioid misuse and distress, and is not a cure model for functional syndromes.

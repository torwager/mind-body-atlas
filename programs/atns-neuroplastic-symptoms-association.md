## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)

**Core construct.** "Neuroplastic symptoms" (earlier: psychophysiologic disorders, PPD; Clarke's "stress illness"). These are real physical symptoms generated by learned nerve pathways in the brain, with no tissue damage or disease behind them. The site's split: "all pain is made in the brain," but *structural* pain comes from injury or disease, while *neuroplastic* pain is a danger signal the brain has learned and keeps firing. Phantom-limb pain is the standard example.

**Causal chain (current site and Clarke's course):**
- **Triggers.** Stress, strong or unrecognised emotion and trauma use the same "alarm system" as physical threat.
- **Perpetuation.** When stress persists, the brain learns to stay on alert and the signal gets "stuck," continuing after tissues have healed.
- **Predisposing factors.** Strong emphasis on adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) and personality habits they produce: perfectionism, people-pleasing, self-criticism, putting others first, low self-esteem. These keep the nervous system under chronic strain. Several quiz items screen for these, e.g., "Are people who caused stress for you as a child still active in your life?"
- **Fear loop.** Fear of symptoms and hypervigilance maintain the alarm.
- **Older psychodynamic layer.** The 2012–2020 PPDA "Treatment Outline" says symptoms can be a somatic expression of emotions that cannot be tolerated or expressed, protecting the person from them. Fear is described as serving "psychological homeostasis" and distracting from painful unconscious emotions. This is Sarno's TMS idea, minus his oxygen-deprivation hypothesis.
- **Autonomic framing.** Patients are taught that the ANS operates outside conscious control (blushing, a "knot" in the stomach) and cannot distinguish physical from psychological threat.

**Explicit break with Sarno.** A PPDA blog by the executive director says Sarno's mild-ischaemia hypothesis was never supported. It credits fMRI and RCT work with showing that "learned neural pathways" are the cause, and that these can be "unlearned" through neuroplasticity.

**Diagnostic heuristics.** Symptoms count as "likely neuroplastic" if they:
- outlast normal healing
- move around or come and go
- are set off by stress, weather, people or situations
- go with normal or discordant tests
- fail body-directed treatment
- occur several at once

The quiz treats a lack of clear medical explanation as "good evidence" of neuroplasticity. Positive criteria (the Schubiner/Clarke primary-pain approach, Schubiner et al. 2024 *J Pain*) are also used.

## 2. One mechanism or many?

**Essentially one mechanism for everything.** A Dec 2025 blog is titled "Many Symptoms, One Brain." It argues from ATNS survey co-occurrence data (e.g., back/neck pain and migraine co-occur at 12% vs 5% expected) that multiple symptoms "are usually from one underlying cause that is treatable."

Condition pages differ only in descriptive detail:
- **ME/CFS.** A nervous system "locked in a protective, energy-conserving alarm," often after infection or prolonged stress. Post-exertional crashes are framed as part of the alarm/threat state, not deconditioning.
- **Long COVID.** Over 200 symptoms. Recommends medical evaluation first. If tests are clear or treatment fails, the brain "likely" plays a role. Pacing is acknowledged as helpful but insufficient.
- **"Chronic Lyme."** States that persistent infection lacks evidence and prolonged antibiotics do not help, so residual symptoms are likely neuroplastic.
- **Extended list.** "Alternative diagnoses" (adrenal fatigue, MCS, EMF sensitivity, MCAS, SIBO, candida, leaky gut, CCI), cardiology (POTS, IST), musculoskeletal, GU, GI and mental-health conditions all appear in one list.

Note that the list also includes conditions with established structural or neurological pathology (small fiber neuropathy, trigeminal and post-herpetic neuralgia, EDS). Every condition page recommends the same two therapies (PRT and EAET) and the same self-assessment. A standing caveat runs throughout: rule out structural or organ disease first, and neuroplastic symptoms can coexist with documented pathology.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies

ATNS does not deliver therapy itself; it curates and refers.

**3.1 Recommended clinician therapies**
- **Pain Reprocessing Therapy (PRT):** reappraise sensations as safe and reduce the threat response.
- **Emotional Awareness and Expression Therapy (EAET):** recognise and express suppressed emotion and stress.
- Directory clinicians also use Schubiner's "Unlearn Your Pain," Psychophysiologic Symptom Relief Therapy (PSRT, the Donnino/Schechter protocol), ISTDP-informed work, Somatic Experiencing, and coaching.
- CBT, mindfulness, nerve blocks and surgery are described as offering "limited" relief because they don't address the learned signals.

**3.2 Generic recovery steps (current site)**
1. Accept the diagnosis: the cause is a learned pathway, not damage.
2. Reduce the threat response and gradually return to avoided activities.
3. Notice how symptoms track with stress and emotion.
4. Address perfectionism, self-criticism and people-pleasing; build self-compassion.

**3.3 PPDA Treatment Outline (2012–c.2020, professional protocol, 8 sections)**
- (I) Education on brain pathways and the ANS; link symptom onset to life stressors and trauma; normalise and validate; explain incidental imaging findings (e.g., asymptomatic disc disease); point out inconsistencies, such as symptoms absent on vacation.
- (II) Reframe symptoms as signals to look inward ("What am I feeling right now?").
- (III) Reduce fear: graded re-engagement with avoided activities, positive self-talk, "outcome independence," mindfulness so the person doesn't "buy in" to fear thoughts.
- (IV–VI) Identify, tolerate and express difficult emotions (anger, sadness, helplessness): childhood exploration, confronting defences, dream interpretation, guided imagery, journaling and free-writing, Somatic Experiencing techniques, role play, Gestalt empty-chair work, free association, expressing emotion toward the therapist.
- (VII–VIII) Identify and change self-defeating patterns.

**3.4 Self-treatment course (free; Clarke; 12 chapters, about 30 minutes of reading)**
- Science of chronic pain and illness.
- Three-part self-diagnosis.
- Evidence.
- Diagnosing current stresses, mental-health conditions and ACEs.
- Treating the long-term impact of ACEs: personality habits, low self-esteem, unrecognised emotions, triggers.
- Then onward to books (Schubiner's *Unlearn Your Pain*, Clarke's *They Can't Find Anything Wrong!*), apps (Curable, Nervana) and practitioners.

**3.5 Community and maintenance**
- Rewiring Project forum.
- Monthly members-only expert Q&As and "After Hours" themed events.
- Webinar library.
- Local chapters and special-interest groups.
- Annual conference: 2026 has 40+ sessions and up to 17.75 CE/CME hours. Pre-conference workshops cover challenging cases, people-pleasing, and "teaching the brain safety through the body" with movement and graded motor imagery.

## 4. Roots and influences

- **Sarno TMS.** Several co-founders and board members trained with or were treated by Sarno: Schechter, Sherman, Sommer Anderson, Schubiner. PPDA ran a Sarno Memorial Fund in 2017. The PPD/NS label was adopted to distance the movement from the TMS label.
- **Psychodynamic theory.** Repressed or intolerable emotion, defences, childhood messages, dream interpretation, free association. Davanloo ISTDP enters via Allan Abbass (co-author with Schubiner of *Hidden From View*).
- **Psychosomatic medicine and Selye-type stress theory.** Clarke's "stress illness" from GI practice; the ACE literature.
- **CBT and fear-avoidance.** Graded exposure, self-talk. Third-wave/mindfulness elements.
- **Pain neuroscience.** Central sensitisation, predictive processing / "brain-generated danger signal," fMRI work on chronification.
- **Somatic Experiencing; Gestalt therapy.**
- **Internet patient communities.** TMSWiki (Munger) and its forum.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance

- **Academic footprint:**
  - Clarke: OHSU emeritus; Visiting Professor at Royal Children's Hospital Brisbane and Oxford; clinical advisor to the UK Stress Illness Recovery Practitioners Association (SIRPA).
  - Schubiner: Michigan State clinical professor.
  - Former board member Ira Rashbaum was chief of TMS/Mind-Body Medicine at NYU Langone Rusk Rehabilitation, an institutional TMS clinic.
  - Former board member Rebecca Kennedy led the Kaiser Permanente Northwest long COVID group using the PPD approach before leaving in 2023.
- **Training and professional texts:** Conferences are accredited for CE/CME. Clarke led two professional textbooks: *Psychophysiologic Disorders* (2019) and *A Diagnostic Guide for Psychophysiologic Disorders* (2022).
- **Guidelines:** None. No CMS, NICE or national guideline recognises "neuroplastic symptoms" or "PPD" as a diagnosis, or recommends ATNS. ATNS lobbies "Federal health decision-makers" as part of its stated mission.
- **Insurance:** Membership, conferences and courses are not reimbursable. Psychotherapy from licensed directory clinicians can be billed under standard psychotherapy codes; 49 listings accept insurance.

## 6. Evidence base

**No trials of ATNS/PPDA itself** (membership, course, quiz or directory). Its Self-Treatment Course and quiz have no published validation. The trials below test related therapies delivered by clinicians affiliated with the movement, several of them PPDA co-founders or board members. All DOIs were checked via Crossref.

**Gaps:**
- No trials in ME/CFS, POTS, MCS, EHS, "chronic Lyme," EDS or MCAS, all of which the site lists.
- Long COVID evidence is a single uncontrolled series.
- Most trials are run by the therapies' developers.
- Comparators are often weak (waitlist, usual care, open-label placebo), apart from the CBT comparisons.
- ATNS's "1 in 3 adults / 80 million Americans" figure comes from its own commercial-panel surveys with XandY (2023–2026 white papers, not peer reviewed), which classify "likely neuroplastic" with the ATNS quiz.
- The "$600 billion" cost figure is an extrapolation from Barsky et al. 2005 on somatisation costs.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory

- **2011–2012.** Founded March 2011; website launched March 2012; first teaching conference Oct 2012 at the New York Academy of Medicine (~250 practitioners from 11 states and 4 countries).
- **2013–2016.** Low-level activity: blog, directory (Drupal-era clinician pages), training workshops (2016).
- **2017.** Research fundraising for the Boulder PRT trial exceeded its goal (~500 international donors); Sarno memorial fund after Sarno's death.
- **2019–2022.** Clarke-edited textbooks; virtual annual conferences with CE/CME (2021, 2023 recordings sold); professional online courses (PPD Diagnosis & Treatment, Challenging Cases, Spanish-language version). The JAMA Psychiatry PRT paper (Sept 2021) and wide media coverage of "pain reprocessing" brought the field mainstream attention.
- **2023.** Long COVID content; first national survey; "PPD Relief Alliance" membership.
- **2024.** Rebrand to Symptomatic/ATNS (autumn 2024).
- **2026.** Rebuilt as neuroplastic.org with condition pages, three podcasts, a 2026 national survey and an in-person Dallas conference (Oct 15–17, 2026).
- **Current self-reported reach:** 30k people/month via site, email and community; "thousands" through courses and conferences; board clinicians claim more than 10,000 patients treated since the 1980s.
- **Finances (IRS 990, EIN 45-0678253):** total revenue $3.6k (2014), $232 (2016), $107k (2017, PRT-trial appeal), $100k (2020), $167k (2021), $335k (2022), $165k (2023). ProPublica lists an IRS exemption ruling date of March 2018, later than the 2011 founding; the reason is not stated publicly. A small organisation by budget.
- **Not retrievable:** r/TMSHealing size (Reddit blocks automated and fetch-tool access), Google Trends (no API access) and social follower counts (Facebook/Instagram @neuroplasticorg require login). Checked Reddit JSON, old.reddit and web search.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms

- **Diagnosis by exclusion and over-inclusion.** The quiz treats "no clear cause found" as evidence of neuroplastic illness. The condition list includes EDS (a genetic connective-tissue disorder), small fiber neuropathy, trigeminal and post-herpetic neuralgia, POTS, MCAS, interstitial cystitis, ME/CFS and long COVID. This risks missed organic disease, and patient groups (especially ME/CFS and long COVID) object to it as psychologisation, even though ATNS repeatedly says symptoms are "real" and not "in your head."
- **Overstated efficacy.** "Most patients are able to fully recover" goes beyond the trial data, which come mostly from back pain and fibromyalgia. Uncontrolled long COVID data are presented alongside RCTs.
- **Psychodynamic and ACE emphasis.** Screening and treatment content focuses on childhood adversity and personality traits (perfectionism, people-pleasing), which can feel like blame. The site explicitly says "not blame."
- **Conflicts of interest.** Board members author and sell books, courses, programmes and therapy (Schubiner, Schechter, Clarke, Gordon/PRT Institute), and ATNS promotes their products and member discounts. Trials are mainly by developers.
- **Self-produced prevalence statistics.** The survey white papers are used in press materials without peer review.

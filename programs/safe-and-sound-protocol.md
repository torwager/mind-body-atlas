## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
- **Polyvagal Theory.** The autonomic nervous system is said to have three hierarchical states:
  - ventral vagal "social engagement" (safe)
  - sympathetic fight/flight
  - dorsal vagal shutdown

  "Neuroception," a subconscious threat-detection process, chooses the state based on cues of safety or danger.
- **The "integrated social engagement system."** Cranial nerves V, VII, IX, X and XI link control of the face, head, larynx and middle-ear muscles with vagal regulation of the heart. People in chronic defensive states are said to lose middle-ear muscle tone, so they hear low-frequency (predator-like) background sound too much and hear the human voice poorly. This shows up as auditory hypersensitivity and poor social engagement.
- **"Neural exercise."** Music is computer-filtered to emphasise the frequency band of human prosody, and the filter shifts dynamically. This is said to exercise the stapedius and tensor tympani and so recruit the neural regulation of the social engagement system. That in turn is said to raise ventral vagal tone (indexed by respiratory sinus arrhythmia, RSA) and make "felt safety" more accessible.
- **Disorders as the result.** Symptoms are reframed as the outcome of a nervous system stuck in defense. Chronic sympathetic or dorsal states are said to drive anxiety, trauma symptoms, GI dysfunction, sensory overwhelm and fatigue. Third parties extend this to fibromyalgia, MCS, ME/CFS and POTS.

## 2. One mechanism or many?
**A single unified mechanism.** Autonomic state regulation via the social engagement system is applied across all conditions. Unyte does not offer condition-specific mechanisms or protocols. Variation comes from **titration**, not diagnosis:
- Choice of pathway (Connect, Core or Balance).
- Pace: sessions can be as short as one minute.
- Playlist.

The provider tunes these to the client's "autonomic response," and trauma and sensory clients are advised to go slowly. The newer Rest & Restore Protocol (Gorry & Porges) adds a second claimed mechanism, "homeostatic entrainment," aimed at sleep, GI comfort and fatigue. It too is undifferentiated by diagnosis.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
- **SSP Connect:** unfiltered full-spectrum versions of the playlists. Used to build familiarity and assess listening sensitivity before Core.
- **SSP Core:** the patented, dynamically progressing filter. Classically **5 hours** in total; the original LPP was 5 consecutive days × ~45–60 min. Clinicians now commonly spread it over weeks in short sessions, e.g. 5–30 min per day.
- **SSP Balance:** lighter, non-progressive filtering. Used after Core for maintenance or extension.
- **Playlists:** eight, including:
  - Original: 1970s–present pop
  - Wonder: for children
  - Classical Calm and Classical Flow: mainly Mozart
  - Groove and Groove Instrumental
  - Freely: ambient
  - Visions: hip-hop/chant fusion
- **Delivery context:**
  - Over-ear headphones (not noise-cancelling, per training materials) in a quiet setting.
  - The provider co-regulates: social presence and attention to signs of dysregulation (fatigue, irritability, emotional release, sound sensitivity).
  - Often paired with psychotherapy (EMDR, IFS, Somatic Experiencing), OT sensory integration, or Polyvagal-informed coaching.
- **Remote/home listening:** through the Unyte app, under provider supervision. The protocol can be repeated but is not meant for continuous use.
- **Measurement:** Unyte assessments (GAD-7, PHQ-9, PCL-5, caregiver scales) tracked in MyUnyte.
- **No self-care curriculum** in the sense of limbic-retraining programs. The intervention is passive listening plus the therapeutic relationship.

## 4. Roots and influences
- **Polyvagal Theory** (Porges 1995, 2011). Its roots are vagal-tone/RSA psychophysiology, Hughlings Jackson's hierarchical dissolution, and comparative evolutionary neuroanatomy.
- **Auditory/middle-ear physiology:** Borg & Counter's model of middle-ear muscle function.
- **Earlier sound-based therapies:**
  - Alfred Tomatis's "Tomatis Method" (1950s onward; filtered Mozart, "electronic ear").
  - Guy Bérard's Auditory Integration Training.
  - iLs's own Focus System, which draws on Tomatis-lineage work.
- **Biofeedback and psychophysiology** (RSA/HRV as an index of regulation).
- **Clinical uptake** through trauma therapy and somatics: Somatic Experiencing, sensorimotor psychotherapy, Deb Dana's polyvagal-informed therapy.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- **Practice:** Mainly private-practice OTs, psychotherapists, counsellors, SLPs and coaches. Some residential addiction and trauma centres (e.g., Caron Treatment Centers is running an RCT). Isolated hospital use, e.g. in paediatric FND at Westmead, Sydney (Kozlowska group).
- **Not in clinical guidelines.** No NICE, APA, AAN, CDC or other guideline mention was found.
- **Regulatory status:** Unyte explicitly disclaims medical-device status and FDA evaluation. Some trial registrations nonetheless describe SSP as a "non-invasive acoustic vagus nerve stimulator."
- **Insurance:** No CPT code or payer coverage. Providers sometimes bill the session under existing psychotherapy or OT codes (indirect).

## 6. Evidence base

### Trials of the precursor (Listening Project Protocol)
- **Porges SW, Bazhenova OV, Bal E, et al. (2014).** *Front Pediatr* 2:80. doi:10.3389/fped.2014.00080. PMID 25136545.
  - Design: two sequential "randomized" controlled trials in children with ASD. Assignment was "sequential by presentation," i.e. quasi-random.
  - Trial I (73 enrolled; 36 vs 28 analysed): filtered music vs headphones only.
  - Trial II (102 enrolled; roughly 80 analysed; 2:1 allocation): filtered vs unfiltered music.
  - Dose: 5 daily ~45-min sessions.
  - Outcome: parent-report questionnaire.
  - Trial I: filtered music improved hearing sensitivity, spontaneous speech, listening and behavioural organisation versus headphones-only.
  - Trial II: against unfiltered music, only hearing sensitivity and emotional control remained significant. Other benefits looked attributable to music or context.
  - Caveats: developer-run with the COI declared "none" although the algorithm was later commercialised; parents only partly blinded; no pre-registration; high attrition in Trial II.
- **Porges SW, Macellaio M, Stanfill SD, et al. (2013).** *Int J Psychophysiol* 88:261–70. PMID 23201146.
  - Mainly a cross-sectional study of RSA and auditory processing in ASD vs typically developing children.
  - In a subset of ASD children, auditory processing and RSA improved after the listening intervention. Uncontrolled.

### Published studies of the branded SSP
- **Kawai H, Kishimoto M, et al. (2023).** *IJERPH* 20:4862. PMID 36981773.
  - Uncontrolled pilot; n = 6 adults with ASD.
  - Only the SRS-2 Social Awareness (family-report) subscale improved. No change in the main self-report outcomes.
  - Unyte-iLs lent equipment and paid the author's training fee.
- **Rajabalee N, Kozlowska K, … Porges SW, et al. (2022).** *Harv Rev Psychiatry* 30:303–16. PMID 35616609.
  - Single case: a 10-year-old with FND unresponsive to standard care improved after SSP within a multidisciplinary programme.
  - Porges is a co-author. This is the only peer-reviewed report in a target "functional" syndrome.
- **Systematic review: Vincent V et al. (2025).** *Occup Ther Int* (PMID 40519630). Covered 8 studies across 6 sound-based interventions in children and concluded the evidence is preliminary, heterogeneous and in need of RCTs.

### Registered but unpublished, or terminated
- **NCT04999852** (Indiana University / Spencer Psychology).
  - Non-randomized, n = 45: SSP + psychotherapy (32) vs psychotherapy as usual (13), in PTSD/anxiety.
  - Posted results: PCL-5 change −9.9 (SD 11.1) vs −2.3 (SD 12.2); GAD-7 −1.9 vs −0.8.
  - Differential attrition: only 19/32 SSP participants completed, vs 12/13 controls.
  - Not randomized; groups self-selected; no peer-reviewed paper located.
- **NCT03083977**, "Processed music on chronic pain" (Porges, Indiana): **terminated at n = 8** (pandemic recruitment). No results.
- **NCT03101826 / NCT03871751** (Prader-Willi syndrome): terminated at n = 5 and n = 10.
- **NCT05249270** (online parent-report survey, n = 132): terminated.
- **Ongoing RCTs:**
  - NCT06701578: HealthPartners; depression/anxiety; filtered vs unfiltered music; quadruple-masked; n = 120 planned; listed as *not yet recruiting* (Oct 2026).
  - NCT06925867: Ohio State; SSP-filtered vs unfiltered music added to Cognitive Processing Therapy for PTSD; triple-masked; n = 100; recruiting.
  - NCT07219056: Caron; trauma plus substance use disorder; sham-controlled, quadruple-masked; n = 100; recruiting.

### Grey literature
- **Cook et al. (2021)**, *Proceedings of IMPRS* (Indiana University student research abstract; doi:10.18060/25904). Non-randomized, n = 17 children in three groups (4/4/9). Reported a smaller SSP response in children on non-stimulant neurotransmitter-altering medication. Not peer-reviewed.
- **Gitler et al. (2025)**, *Med Int (Lond)* (PMID 40386190). Narrative review that promotes SSP alongside HRV biofeedback as "vagal neuromodulation." It contains no new trial data.

### Company "real-world evidence"
Unyte reports clinical-to-non-clinical shifts of 61% for anxiety, 54% for depression and 63% for trauma symptoms, plus survey figures such as "85% reported anxiety reduction." No denominator, control group or peer review is given.

### Gaps
- No controlled trial in chronic pain, fibromyalgia, ME/CFS, long COVID, POTS, IBS or MCS.
- The key unfiltered-music control has been tested only in the 2014 ASD paper.
- Little independent replication.
- Autonomic mechanism (RSA change) not demonstrated in adequately controlled designs.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory
- **2000s–2015:** LPP was a laboratory research protocol (UIC, then UNC) with negligible public reach.
- **2016–2017:** Commercial launch through iLs.
- **2018–2019:** Growth through trauma-therapy networks, e.g. a 2019 "SSP Gathering" and SSP case-study request pages (Wayback captures from 2018–2019).
- **2020–2021:** Remote delivery via app during the pandemic. Polyvagal Theory went mainstream in therapy culture (Deb Dana books, social media, the "nervous system regulation" trend), driving uptake. Bundled into PESI and Psychotherapy Networker trainings.
- **2022–2025:**
  - The pathways expanded to Connect, Core and Balance, and eight playlists.
  - Unyte reports 10,000+ certified providers in 50+ countries and "hundreds of thousands" served.
  - Spin-off Rest & Restore Protocol (Gorry & Porges).
  - Porges's newer "Sonic Augmentation Technology" was evaluated in a feasibility study (Dale et al., 2026, *Front Psychiatry*).
  - SSP is frequently recommended in chronic-illness and limbic-retraining communities (Primal Trust, MCS/long-COVID forums) as an adjunct.
- **2023–2026 headwinds:** Scholarly critiques of Polyvagal Theory: Grossman 2023 (*Biol Psychol*), and Grossman + 38 co-authors 2026 (*Clin Neuropsychiatry*), "Why the Polyvagal Theory is untenable." Porges published a rebuttal (2026).
- **Not quantified:** Google Trends, subreddit sizes and YouTube metrics were not measured, because the search budget was exhausted.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **Contested theory.** The mechanism rests on Polyvagal Theory. Its core physiological premises are disputed by many autonomic physiologists: the claimed uniqueness of mammalian myelinated vagal pathways from the nucleus ambiguus; the dorsal-vagal "shutdown" in humans; RSA as an index of vagal "tone" and of a safety state. The 2026 39-author critique calls the theory untenable.
- **Marketing claims beyond the data.** Marketing language such as "evidence-based," "stimulates the vagus nerve" and percentages of improvement outpaces the evidence: one quasi-randomized parent-report trial of a precursor in children with autism.
- **Developer conflicts.** Developer-led research with commercial links; equipment and training supplied free to investigators.
- **Cost and access.** Provider-gated subscriptions make clients dependent on certified practitioners.
- **Adverse reactions.** Practitioner materials acknowledge possible "dysregulation" reactions (emotional flooding, fatigue, sound sensitivity), managed by titration. Systematic adverse-event data are absent.
- **Off-label spread.** Use in chronic-illness communities extends claims to conditions never studied.

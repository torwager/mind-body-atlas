## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
CFS Health gives very little explicit pathophysiology. It presents itself as a **health-and-lifestyle rehabilitation program, not a medical one**. The implicit model, pieced together from the FAQ, book copy, podcast titles and the founder's story, has five parts:

- **Overexertion and the boom–bust trigger.** Illness starts with an infection (Morrison's own case was glandular fever) combined with "pushing through" without rest. The body eventually goes into "shut down."
- **What keeps people ill.** The illness is perpetuated by the **push–crash cycle**: overdoing it on good days, which causes post-exertional malaise and setbacks. Symptoms reflect a depleted "baseline" capacity.
- **Nervous-system dysregulation.** This is the secondary framing that has grown since about 2023. People are described as "stuck in fight or flight, constantly wired, exhausted or shut down" (Ep. 193, May 2026). The program promises to "calm and reset the nervous system" to reduce fatigue and pain. It is presented as general autonomic or stress-system language, without a specific polyvagal or limbic-circuit theory.
- **Psychological load.** Fear, anxiety, guilt, shame, loss of identity, "secondary depression" and non-acceptance are treated as obstacles to recovery. They are not described as the cause.
- **Health-building logic.** Symptoms fall "as a by-product" as overall health (energy stability, sleep, nutrition, conditioning, mindset) rises.

The book page explicitly sets the approach apart from **"brain retraining"** and quick-fix symptom relief. It describes CFS Health as rehabilitating body and brain to restore "body wisdom." This puts it closer to **pacing plus graded rehabilitation plus coaching** than to DNRS/Gupta-style limbic retraining, even though it now uses nervous-system language.

## 2. One mechanism or many?
The program uses **one generic model applied to all conditions**, with ME/CFS as the default case. Long COVID and post-viral fatigue are handled within the same CFS framework (the FAQ discusses post-viral fatigue symptoms such as PEM and brain fog rather than naming long COVID). Fibromyalgia is named alongside it, and the podcast addresses "chronic health conditions" broadly. There are no condition-specific mechanisms or modules. Individualisation comes through where a member sits on CFS Health's "3 stages of recovery" and through baseline level (bed-bound to working). Women's health and return-to-work modules exist, but these address life domains, not separate mechanisms.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
**Structure.** Members apply, then have a sales call, then an introductory call in month 1 to set priorities. The program is self-paced, with modules assigned according to recovery stage. The minimum is 6 months; the typical stay is 9–15 months. Members are told to expect physical change within about 2 months.

**Core components:**
- **Baseline and anti-push–crash pacing.** Find a stable daily activity baseline (the free "Find your baseline" training), stop boom–bust, then expand gradually. This is the foundation module.
- **"RPE Method".** Movement is paced by rating of perceived exertion so that members can "move without crashing" (book).
- **Restorative movement.** This is a video-module topic, not a separately named program on the current Program Info page. It is overseen by the in-house exercise physiologist Steph Ryan (BSc Sport & Exercise Science, Master of Clinical Exercise Physiology, PhD candidate; AEP accreditation not verified). A **progressive movement / reconditioning** stage follows once members are ready. This is in effect graded activity anchored to symptoms and PEM.
- **Nervous-system calming.** Relaxation-based regulation to reduce symptoms and pain.
- **Mindset work.** Acceptance (letting go of anger and frustration was the first 6 weeks of Morrison's early 1:1 cases), "brain cognition" techniques for anxiety and worry, reworking unhelpful thoughts, belief that recovery is possible, guilt and shame, and identity.
- **Lifestyle.** Nutrition e-books (stable blood sugar and energy), sleep, boundaries and relationships, and a setback strategy.
- **Gradual reintegration.** A phased return to work and social life.
- **Optional supplements.** Videos by the resident integrative medical doctor; nothing is prescribed. Members are told not to stop medication without consulting a doctor.
- **Support and accountability.** Weekly group Zoom calls, 1:1 Momentum Coach check-ins, 20+ peer "mentors," a Facebook group (with a "no negative venting" rule), a library of more than 4,000 member Q&As, a mandatory weekly "win" post, and a printed planner and tracker.
- **Lived-experience coaching.** More than 90% of staff are said to have had and recovered from CFS.

## 4. Roots and influences
- **Clinical and medical:** pacing and energy-envelope management, combined with graded exercise and activity principles. Morrison credits his Melbourne paediatrician (Dr Lionel Lubitz: "can't do too much, can't do too little") and a 4-week inpatient CFS rehabilitation program he attended as a teenager.
- **Sports and fitness:** exercise science and personal-training practice (RPE scales, progressive overload).
- **Coaching:** the life-coaching and positive-psychology tradition (mindset, accountability, "wins," the motto "luck = Labor Under Correct Knowledge").
- **Psychological:** generic CBT-style thought work and acceptance (ACT-like).
- **Recent additions:** nervous-system and stress-regulation language and integrative-medicine supplement advice.
- **Non-sources:** the program does not claim lineage from meditation traditions, Sarno or DNRS-style limbic retraining, and explicitly sets itself apart from "brain retraining."

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- No adoption by health systems, and no endorsements from guideline bodies were found.
- The emphasis on "progressive movement / reconditioning" runs against current NICE NG206 (2021) guidance, which advises against graded exercise therapy for ME/CFS. However, the program frames its exercise as symptom-titrated and secondary to establishing a baseline.
- It is not insurance-reimbursable. It is paid directly by card through Stripe, with payment plans.

## 6. Evidence base
- **Branded program:** No peer-reviewed trials, observational cohorts or registered trials were identified, and the provider does not claim any. A fact-check PubMed query found nothing. ClinicalTrials.gov was not searched exhaustively. No publication is cited anywhere on the site.
- **Internal data:**
  - A 2024 self-reported member survey: 92% improved health and well-being, 97% improved mindset, 92% would recommend. There is no denominator, response rate, standardized measures or follow-up.
  - Outcome charts on the program page are images with no methods.
  - "Thousands" or "5,500+" recoveries are asserted without definition.
- **Related generic evidence:**
  - Pacing and activity management for ME/CFS has mixed and low-quality evidence; the PACE trial reported no benefit of adaptive pacing over usual care.
  - Graded exercise and CBT have contested trial evidence (PACE and the reanalyses) and have been downgraded by NICE 2021.
  - None of this tests the CFS Health package.
- **Gaps:** Outcomes are entirely self-reported by the vendor. Results are subject to survivorship and selection bias, because members self-select after a sales call and long-stay members are the ones surveyed.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **Sales model.** High-ticket pricing is hidden behind sales calls. There is a 6-month lock-in with no early cancellation. Trustpilot reviewers complain of a "great salesman" founder, an unorganised library of "over 100 videos," and coaching that isn't worth the price. Several 1-star reviews from 2026 call it a "scam" or the founder a "con man", and one compares the company to Belle Gibson. These are unverified reviewer claims, and the Trustpilot profile is unclaimed and not invited. A refund dispute involves the delivery charge on a physical product.
- **Patient-community concerns.** In ME/CFS patient communities (e.g. Science for ME), coaching programs that combine "recovery" testimonials with mindset work and progressive exercise draw generic criticism. This criticism concerns implied blame, risk of PEM-driven deterioration from reconditioning, and survivorship-based marketing. A specific CFS Health thread could not be verified this session.
- **Facebook group rule.** The "no negative venting" rule may suppress reports of harm.
- **Mismatch between marketing and substance.** Nervous-system marketing language has been layered onto what is essentially a pacing, reconditioning and coaching program.

## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
- **"Threat physiology."** Chronic mental and physical symptoms are physiological, not "just psychological." They result from a sustained fight-or-flight state, which Hanscom calls an "adrenalized" nervous system. The site's FAQ goes further: nearly all chronic mental and physical illness is said to arise from prolonged stress chemistry. Anxiety is reframed as the felt sensation of that threat state, and as a protective signal.
- **Pain becomes centralised and linked to emotion.** In his 2018 e-book, after about 3 months the original nociceptive circuits are said to quiet down, while emotional circuits take over and form "new pain centres" independent of the tissue. Pain and emotional reactivity become physiologically coupled, so everyday stressors (work, relationships, money) trigger flares without a new physical cause. This draws on Apkarian/Baliki-type findings of a shift toward emotional circuitry.
- **The trapped animal / the "unescapable."** His central analogy is a hand held over a hot burner that cannot be withdrawn. Inescapable pain or threat produces anger and anxiety, which feed back into further sympathetic activation. Repetitive unwanted thoughts (RUTs) act as ongoing internal threats that sustain the loop, and attempts to suppress them strengthen them.
- **Immune/inflammatory and cellular layer (later writing).** In *Calm Your Body, Heal Your Mind* and the mission page, prolonged threat physiology is said to cause mitochondrial dysfunction and inflammation in the brain and body, glial/immune signalling, and reduced blood flow to "thinking" (prefrontal) regions. Four environmental factors are said to shift immune and inflammatory tone: hope, sense of control, positive mood and social connection. These are cited from the psychoneuroimmunology literature.
- **Life stress and loss as triggers.** Many patients are said to have had major personal losses when their pain began, consistent with Holmes–Rahe life-event stress. Hanscom says he would not do elective spine surgery in the face of severe life losses, and that pain often resolved as the loss was worked through, even with structural pathology present.
- **Structure vs. function.** Most chronic symptoms are framed as physiological rather than structural. Surgery is reserved for clear structural lesions such as deformity, instability or neurological compression, and he cites the poor results of fusion for non-specific pain (Carragee 2006; Franklin 1994).

## 2. One mechanism or many?
**Single unified mechanism, with condition-specific emphasis.** Hanscom treats mental pain (anxiety, anger, OCD, depression) and physical pain as "the same thing" at the level of the nervous system. The Pre-Course explicitly teaches that mental and physical pain are equivalent. One threat-physiology model is applied to a long symptom list spanning musculoskeletal, neurological, visceral (IBS, POTS) and psychiatric categories. The only real differentiation is triage of **structural** problems for which surgery or other medical care is indicated, a distinction that reflects his spine-surgery background. He does not offer separate mechanisms for ME/CFS, MCS, POTS and similar conditions; these simply appear on the list of symptoms the model covers.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
**Organising idea:** "Direct your Own Care." The patient takes ownership of a multi-pronged, self-paced plan, and clinicians act as advisors. In the earlier book version, this was coordinated with the physician as a structured care pathway.

Core components across the books and the course:
1. **Expressive writing as the first and non-negotiable step.** Write down whatever thoughts are present, however negative, then **immediately destroy the paper** without rereading or analysing it. Do this daily, typically 10–30 min, and indefinitely in the early phase. Hanscom calls it the one common factor among all his patients who became pain-free. It also has variants such as positive writing and "write and rip."
2. **Calming and "rewiring" the nervous system.** Active meditation and relaxation exercises, slow breathing and body awareness, and "separating" from repetitive thoughts rather than fighting them. Attention is shifted through the senses ("active meditation": focusing on a physical sensation for 5–10 s at a time) and visualisation.
3. **Sleep first.** Sleep is restored aggressively, including with sleep medications when needed. Inadequate sleep is said to block all other progress.
4. **Pain education / neuroplasticity.** Patients learn how the brain processes and amplifies pain, and that symptoms are real and physiological but reversible.
5. **Anger, forgiveness and the "victim" role.** Anger is identified as hyperactivated anxiety that keeps the threat state on. Forgiveness exercises follow, and patients are taught to let go of the "victim role" and "dissolve the ego" (cognitive distortions).
6. **Medication management.** Anxiety and nerve-pain medications are used judiciously, opioids are tapered, and the sequence of medications is optimised.
7. **Physical reconditioning.** Physical therapy and gradual return to activity once the nervous system is calmer.
8. **Play, creativity, joy, purpose and social connection.** This is described as the stage that rewires circuitry toward safety ("thrive"), distinct from hedonistic escape.
9. **Stress management, CBT-style reframing, and addressing life losses.** Patients work through grief and life stressors.

The four-part framework in *Calm Your Body, Heal Your Mind*:
- (a) separate from negative thoughts through mindfulness, meditation and expressive writing;
- (b) turn down anger;
- (c) dissolve the ego and its cognitive distortions;
- (d) nurture creativity and joy.

**Online course structure:**
- The **Pre-Course** ($20) covers an overview, starter tools and the "two core aspects of healing."
- **The DOC Journey** is a guided sequence of short video lessons with "rest stops" for practice. It suggests starting at about 10 min/day and has no fixed endpoint, since it is an ongoing subscription.
- It also includes monthly webinars and an app.
- **Group coaching** runs Tue/Thu 12–1 pm PT on Zoom, led by Hanscom. Each session includes a relaxation exercise, a short lesson, Q&A and small-group breakouts. It is explicitly education/peer coaching, not medical advice.

## 4. Roots and influences
- **Pennebaker expressive writing** (1986 onward). This is the signature technique, although Hanscom's "destroy immediately" version differs from Pennebaker's 15–20 min × 3–4 day protocol.
- **CBT and pain psychology.** Hanscom co-authored a 2015 review arguing for early, widespread CBT for chronic low back pain. David Burns' *Feeling Good* is on his recommended reading list.
- **Mindfulness/meditation** (MBSR-style), plus **Selye stress theory** and Holmes–Rahe life-event stress.
- **Neuroplastic pain science:** Apkarian/Baliki work on the shift of chronic back pain to emotional circuits, and central sensitisation.
- **Psychoneuroimmunology**, and later **cell danger response/mitochondrial** framing (Naviaux-type) together with **polyvagal-style** safety/threat language. His podcast and discussion group have featured polyvagal and immunology researchers.
- **Sarno/TMS tradition.** Hanscom interviews and features TMS/PPD practitioners such as Georgie Oldfield (SIRPA). His approach shares the "structural findings are often incidental" stance, but he puts less emphasis on repressed rage and more on physiology.
- **Viktor Frankl, Paul Brand, Jim Doty:** meaning and compassion themes, listed among his recommended books.
- **Life coaching / positive psychology:** play, joy and "thrive" language.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- **Within his surgical practice:** at Swedish Medical Center, Hanscom required non-operative "structured care" before elective spine surgery. This covered sleep, medication, expressive writing and PT, and is reported anecdotally to have greatly reduced surgery rates in his own practice. It was never published as an outcome study.
- **Professional roles:** chair of the Scoliosis Research Society non-operative care committee (2019); lead on an SRS non-operative care standards manual; consultant to Spine Zone (2021–); co-director of a spine fellowship; consultant to Premera.
- **Guideline and resource mentions:** listed in the American Chronic Pain Association 2020 Resource Guide; Washington State Bree Collaborative pain materials (per his site; neither listing was independently verified). These are resource listings, not endorsements of efficacy.
- **No adoption of the DOC Journey course** by any health system, payer, NICE or CMS could be found.
- **Insurance:** not reimbursable. Payment is by monthly subscription through Kajabi checkout. If a clinician delivers CBT or PT along DOC lines, those services may be covered as usual care.

## 6. Evidence base
- **No peer-reviewed trial of Back in Control / DOC.** PubMed and Europe PMC author searches ("Hanscom DA", "Hanscom D" with pain/polyvagal/cytokine terms) returned only surgical case reports, anatomical studies and technical notes (e.g., Cureus, World Neurosurg, Spine, 2012–2018), plus one review. A re-check of PubMed ("Hanscom D[au]", 32 records, Oct 2026) confirmed this. No ClinicalTrials.gov registration for the program was found.
- **Hanscom's relevant publication:** Hanscom DA, Brox JI, Bunnage R. *Defining the role of cognitive behavioral therapy in treating chronic low back pain: an overview.* Global Spine J 2015;5(6):496–504. PMID 26682100; doi:10.1055/s-0035-1567836. This is a **narrative review, not a trial**. It argues that "CBT" should be defined by the problem it targets, and recommends early, widespread CBT for chronic low back pain.
- **Founder-reported data:** his own recovery (17 symptoms resolved), plus "hundreds" and later ">1,000" patients described as pain-free. There is no denominator, no comparison group and no standardised outcome.
- **Related generic evidence (not of the branded program):**
  - Expressive-writing trials in chronic pain show small, mostly psychological and short-lived effects. See, e.g., Salikka T, Kylmä J, Fröjd S, et al. *Therapeutic writing interventions for adults with chronic pain: experiences and health effects. A systematic review with a narrative synthesis.* Eur J Pain 2026 (PMID 41699922; PMC12910190), which reports modest psychological benefit and fleeting physical effects.
  - CBT and mindfulness for chronic low back pain have multiple RCTs and meta-analyses (e.g., Cherkin 2016, JAMA), but these test different protocols.
  - Hanscom's distinctive "write and immediately destroy" variant has not, to my knowledge, been tested head-to-head against standard Pennebaker writing.
- **Gaps:** there is no independent evaluation, and the claims for non-pain conditions (POTS, IBS, PTSD, tinnitus) rest on theory and anecdote alone.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **Sweeping causal claims.** The claim that nearly all chronic mental and physical illness stems from prolonged stress physiology goes far beyond the evidence. It risks implying that symptoms are self-generated, and it overlaps with critiques of TMS/"mindbody" framings for ME/CFS, POTS and similar conditions.
- **The signature technique is not validated.** "Write and destroy" is presented as essential, with no trial of the variant, and the cited support ("300+ papers") refers to standard expressive writing.
- **Founder-led, testimonial-based marketing** with no published outcome data, despite Hanscom's medical credentials. This is a common critique of surgeon- and physician-author programs.
- **Credibility asset vs. conflict of interest.** As a former spine surgeon who argues against unnecessary spine surgery, he is credible to many patients. Yet he markets his own subscription product.
- No regulatory actions or major media controversies were found. Searches were limited by the exhausted web-search quota.

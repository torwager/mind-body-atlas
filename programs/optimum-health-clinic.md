## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
- **Maladaptive Stress Response (MSR).** This is the clinic's core construct. ME/CFS is framed as the stress response (the nervous system and HPA axis) getting stuck "on" after a trigger. Arroll's 2013 *Medical Hypotheses* paper sets it out in terms of **allostatic overload**: repeated or prolonged activation of stress-response systems, including inflammatory cytokines, perpetuates the illness. ME/CFS is therefore a "maladaptive stress disorder." The aim of the psychology programme is to "switch off the stress response" so that the body can stay in a "healing state" (the "Healing Zone").
- **Psychological and emotional perpetuators.** These include emotional style, suppressed emotion, the inner critic, self-sabotage, and "boom-and-bust" driving behaviour. Earlier OHC materials described characteristic driven, achieving, helping or perfectionist styles. Howard's later books, *It's Not Your Fault* and *Decode Your Trauma*, push further toward a developmental-trauma framing, citing Porges (polyvagal theory) and Levine (Somatic Experiencing).
- **Multi-system physiology (the "Clinical Roadmap").** Symptoms are taken to interact across body systems:
  - Mitochondrial or energy metabolism "at the centre" of fatigue
  - Immune dysfunction that is not visible on standard tests
  - Gut dysfunction (dysbiosis, SIBO)
  - Endocrine and "adrenal" dysfunction (Adrenal Stress Index)
  - Histamine, hormones, toxins and infections
- **Integral framing.** Howard & Arroll (2011) applied Ken Wilber's "4 Quadrants" integral model to ME/CFS: biological, psychological, cultural and social perspectives taken together.

## 2. One mechanism or many?
**Mostly unified, with person-specific emphasis.**
- The *psychological* model is a single stress-response and nervous-system account applied to all fatigue conditions. The site does not separate ME/CFS, fibromyalgia, long COVID or POTS mechanistically.
- Differentiation comes through *individual* biochemical profiling rather than diagnostic categories. Functional tests pick out "root causes" per client, such as gut, adrenal, mitochondrial, histamine or mycotoxin problems, and the D.R.I.V.E. nutrition protocol tailors supplements accordingly. This is personalised (functional medicine), not disorder-specific.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
**Psychology department ("Therapeutic Coaching™")**
- A proprietary blend of NLP, hypnotherapy, life coaching, EFT, mindfulness and developmental psychology.
- **3 pillars:**
  1. Nervous-system regulation ("Healing Zone"): breathing, relaxation, mindfulness.
  2. Emotional healing: EFT, psychotherapy and coaching techniques.
  3. Behavioural change ("Integration"): habits, self-sabotage, and the "Wheel of Balanced Recovery."
- **90 Day RESET Programme**, week by week:
  1. Mindset of Change
  2. What creates a Maladaptive Stress Response?
  3. "Bouncing the Boundaries" (activity limits, pacing)
  4. Mindfulness and self-awareness bootcamp
  5. **STOP process**, a pattern-interrupt for stress-response triggers
  6. **PRESENCE process**, including body scans
  7. Pause week
  8. Emotional style
  9. Opening to emotions (Wheel of Emotions)
  10. EFT for calming the system
  11. Parts work (inner critic, inner child)
  12. Self-coaching
- Bonus material in the 90 Day programme: an Enneagram module, *Decode Your Trauma*, and Trauma Super Conference interviews.
- The self-paced RESET course has the same 12 modules plus interviews with Porges and Levine.
- Dose: 25+ hours of video, 12 weekly one-hour group Zoom calls, optional 1:1s, and the audio programmes "Learn to Relax" and "The Sleep Inducer." All psychology practitioners have personally had ME/CFS.
- In the 2012 BMJ Open study, the psychology arm ran for 3 months: 3–13 hours of contact (group, telephone, 1:1) plus about 6 hours of CDs and online material.

**Nutrition department ("Therapeutic Nutrition")**
- **Functional tests:** stool analysis, SIBO, organic acids, Adrenal Stress Index, nutrient and hormone panels, mycotoxins, histamine, kryptopyrroluria, co-infection panels, immune status, toxic metals, food intolerance, MARCoNS, nutrigenomics.
- **Treatment:** practitioner-grade supplement protocols, plus diet and lifestyle change, following the D.R.I.V.E. steps (Decode, Roadmap, Integrate with psychology, Validate, Engage).
- **Dose:** 1-hour initial consultation, then follow-ups about every 6 weeks.

**Medical department**
- Run by doctors (Dr Anu Arasu and colleagues) linked to London Bioidentical Hormones.
- Covers functional and integrative medicine, gut and hormone balance, and "protocol approaches" once the Roadmap is drawn up.

**Combined programme**
- Psychology and nutrition are phased in sequentially; the clinic notes that doing everything at once raised drop-out.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- Entirely private and self-pay. Not NHS-commissioned.
- The NICE ME/CFS guideline (NG206, 2021) does not endorse proprietary recovery programmes. It warns against therapies that claim to cure ME/CFS and against graded exercise and Lightning Process–type approaches.
- The clinic reported NHS research-ethics approval in 2019 for an RCT. This is ethics approval, not clinical adoption.
- No insurer coverage was identified.

## 6. Evidence base
All of the clinic's empirical outputs were produced in-house: authors were the OHC founder/CEO and its research director, with no external funding. No independent evaluation was found. ClinicalTrials.gov and ISRCTN searches for "Optimum Health Clinic" returned nothing.

**Branded OHC programmes**
- **Arroll MA, Howard A. *BMJ Open* 2012;2(6):e001079.** doi:10.1136/bmjopen-2012-001079; PMID 23166120.
  - Design: preliminary prospective cohort in OHC's private clinic. Clients self-selected into psychology (n=42), nutrition (n=44) or combined (n=52) arms. No randomisation and no control.
  - N: 138 at baseline; only 72 (52%) gave 3-month follow-up, with 33% retention in the psychology arm.
  - Results: statistically significant within-group improvements in fatigue (MFI), SF-36 function and CDC symptom inventory in all arms, reported as percentage changes (e.g., whole-sample general fatigue −8.6%, vitality +22%). No between-group differences after baseline correction.
  - The authors themselves describe the results as statistical, not clinical, significance and call for RCTs. The clinic's research page lists it as 2012.
- **Arroll MA, Attree EA, Marshall CL, Dancey CP. *Psychol Res Behav Manag* 2014;7:213-21.** doi:10.2147/PRBM.S63193; PMID 25214803.
  - Design: non-randomised pilot of OHC's online "Secrets to Recovery" programme (15 modules: psychology, nutrition, EFT, NLP, meditation, pacing) against an online meditation programme.
  - N: 28 analysed (19 vs 9; the control group was older). Self-reported diagnosis.
  - Results: between-group differences only in chance and powerful-others locus of control and one sleep item. **No difference in fatigue or overall symptoms.** Largest effect (partial η² = 0.27, chance locus of control).
  - Conflicts: the lead author worked for the OHC Foundation, and the programme is owned by Howard's company.

**Non-trial OHC publications (no outcome data)**
- Howard & Arroll 2011, *J Integral Theory Pract* 6(4):25-40: integral-medicine model.
- Arroll & Howard 2013, *Psychol Health* 28(3):302-18, PMID 22963543: IPA qualitative study, N=10, on identity change and post-traumatic growth.
- Arroll 2013, *Med Hypotheses* 81(3):506-8, PMID 23850395: allostatic overload hypothesis.
- Arroll & Arroll 2013, *Aust Fam Physician* 42(4):191-3, PMID 23550241: GP management commentary.
- Arroll & Alderman 2012: single nutrition case report in a trade journal.
- Hunter, Paxman & James 2017: cost-of-illness report, steered and funded by OHC.

**Gaps**
- No RCT, no long-term follow-up and no objective outcomes.
- The 2019 RCT has no visible registration or results as of 2026.
- RESET (2019) itself has never been evaluated.
- The component techniques have their own varied evidence: mindfulness has RCT support in other conditions; EFT and NLP have weak evidence.

- **2003–04:** Harley Street clinic opens. **2005:** Therapeutic Coaching training launches. **2006:** CAM award and core psychology programme.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **Credentials and title.** ME patient-advocacy forum users (Science for ME) question Howard's qualifications: an undergraduate psychology degree, no counselling registration, a self-described "therapeutic coach" now styled "Dr." They also note the clinic's use of NLP, EFT and hypnosis, and liken it to the Lightning Process.
- **Ethics of filming.** Filming vulnerable ME patients for the YouTube *In Therapy* series raised concerns.
- **Cost and supplements.** Supplement protocols were described as expensive. The extensive functional test menu (Adrenal Stress Index, kryptopyrroluria, MARCoNS, food intolerance) relies on tests of contested validity.
- **Charity–company structure.** A charity ran alongside a for-profit company that owned the IP, and the charity's income appeared to come mostly from the company. Forum posts also mention two informally resolved ASA complaints (unverified) and Howard's 2018 cryptocurrency venture for charities (Giftcoin).
- **Evidence and conflicts.** All evidence is in-house, non-randomised, with heavy attrition; the only comparison (online meditation) showed no fatigue difference. The research page describes the work as building toward a "high quality evidence base"; a "clinically proven" marketing claim could not be verified in this check (the research page does not use that phrase).
- **Contested framing.** Patient groups object to stress-response and personality framings of ME/CFS as psychologising, contrary to NICE NG206's biomedical framing.

## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)

- **Core claim.** Chronic physical and mental symptoms are adaptations of a dysregulated autonomic nervous system that holds unresolved survival stress ("stored trauma"). The nervous system is said to "govern all other systems" (immune, endocrine, digestive, muscular), so dysregulation spreads to every system.
- **Origin of dysregulation.** The site lists these sources:
  - a difficult early environment (developmental or attachment trauma, adverse childhood experiences)
  - accidents and injuries
  - surgery and anesthesia
  - medical injury or infection
  - decades of chronic stress
  - a culture that rewards "override" and disconnection from the body
- **Perpetuation.** Fight/flight and freeze responses that are overwhelmed and cannot "complete" become "stuck." The body keeps running survival physiology long after the threat has passed. This is the Somatic Experiencing (Levine) model of incomplete defensive responses, illustrated with the "African safari" story of animals discharging stress.
- **Polyvagal framing.** The program is described as "polyvagal-informed." Its content covers ANS 101, "Vagus Nerve 101" and freeze/shutdown as a dorsal-vagal state. Blog posts link "freeze" to autoimmune disease.
- **Developmental attunement.** Regulation is said to be learned in infancy through caregiver attunement. Adults whose attunement was incomplete can still learn it later through neuroplasticity, by becoming "their own attuned caregiver." The Feldenkrais idea of learning like an infant underpins the "relearning how to learn" theme.
- **Capacity / window of tolerance.** The "swimming-pool analogy" holds that the goal is to expand nervous-system capacity, not to suppress symptoms. Most people with chronic symptoms are said to live outside their window of tolerance.
- **Bottom-up vs top-down.** Lyon argues that willpower, mindset, CBT-style reframing or breathwork alone "don't reach the autonomic level." Change must come through interoceptive, sensorimotor experience.
- **"Fluid/chemical trauma."** Following Kathy Kain, toxic or chemical exposure (including in utero and intergenerational exposure) is described as a distinct layer of cellular trauma. Lyon attributes her own lifelong rashes to it.

## 2. One mechanism or many?

**Essentially one mechanism.** Nervous-system dysregulation from stored survival stress or trauma is presented as the common root of chronic illness, pain, anxiety, depression, relational problems and parenting difficulties. Lyon credits her SE mentor Steven Hoskinson with the view that "pretty much ALL chronic and mental illness" connects to dysregulation and unresolved traumatic stress.

Differences between conditions are a matter of emphasis, not separate mechanisms:
- **Autoimmune and chronic "syndromes":** framed through freeze/shutdown physiology.
- **Toxic exposures:** "fluid/chemical trauma" is a special sub-type. The FAQ concedes that some conditions have additional non-trauma causes: toxins, mould, radiation, pharmaceuticals, hormonal problems, nutrient deficiency. For these it recommends adjuncts, including:
  - detox protocols (chelation, oxygen therapy, a zeolite product, "Pure Body"/"Pure Body Extra," which the FAQ says Irene and Seth have used to detox from heavy metals and recommend)
  - morning sunlight for "mitochondrial health"
  - barefoot grounding
  - hot/cold hydrotherapy
  - exercise
- **Mental illness:** the program is said to be likely to help OCD, anxiety and depression, but not to suit psychosis.
- **Exclusions:**
  - severe or longstanding psychiatric disorder
  - suicidality
  - active substance misuse
  - persistent extreme activation or terror
  - living with an abuser
  - younger children (teens 14–18 possibly)

There are no condition-specific protocols for ME/CFS, POTS, MCS, long COVID and similar conditions. The same curriculum applies to all.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies

**Neurosensory exercises.** These are short audio-guided practices, 15–30 min each, meant to be repeated until internalized and woven into daily life (e.g., orienting before starting the car). The practices are:
- **Orienting:** slowly turning the head and eyes to take in the environment.
- **Breath awareness.**
- **Sensing contact:** with the ground or supporting surfaces.
- **Following the biological impulse:** small spontaneous movements or shifts.
- **Self-touch and containment:** holds borrowed from Kain's touch work.
- **Resourcing:** external anchors such as a warm drink, a blanket, music or nature.
- **Titration:** working at the edge of the window of tolerance rather than pushing through.
- **Feldenkrais-style movement explorations:** e.g., "potent posture," "balancing your back," "multi-tasking awareness."
- **"Nervous System First Aid – the 4 Whens":** when to orient, when to resource, when a deep breath helps, and what to do when unsure.

**Education.** Lectures cover:
- ANS physiology and the vagus nerve
- the animal stress-discharge model
- how accumulated stress shows up in the body
- the window of tolerance
- polyvagal theory
- the 5-part "Biology of Stress" video series

## 4. Roots and influences

- **Somatic Experiencing (Peter Levine):** ethology of defensive responses, discharge or completion, titration, pendulation, resourcing. Lyon names it as one of the three pillars.
- **Somatic Practice / touch-based developmental trauma work (Kathy Kain, Stephen Terrell):** containment, self-touch, "fluid/chemical trauma," and attention to early attachment and attunement.
- **Feldenkrais Method:** Awareness Through Movement, learning modelled on infant development, and neuroplastic self-learning.
- **Polyvagal theory (Stephen Porges):** vagal hierarchy, freeze and shutdown, safety.
- **Trauma-somatics literature:** Robert Scaer (quoted on the site), the developmental trauma literature, and ACEs research.
- **Selye-style stress physiology:** taught as ANS/HPA biology.
- **Functional/naturopathic and "terrain" medicine:** detox, grounding, sunlight, hydrotherapy (blog: "Terrain Theory & Naturopathic Musings").
- **Exercise science:** Lyon's academic background.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance

- **Mainstream adoption:** none. SBSM does not appear in NICE, CDC, specialty-society or other clinical guidance, and no health system or employer program delivering it was found.
- **Academic link:** a Mitacs Accelerate grant (Canadian federal/provincial industry–academic matching) funded a postdoctoral evaluation at UVic's Department of Psychology (SMART Lab, Dr. Colette Smart; lead researcher Dr. Jordan Ali).
- **Insurance:** not reimbursable. The site states that it and its staff are "not healthcare professionals or providers." Individual SE sessions with licensed therapists may be partly reimbursable under those therapists' own licenses, but that falls outside this program.

## 6. Evidence base

**Branded-program studies:**

| Study | Design | N | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ali J, Smart CM et al., UVic SMART Lab with Scientuitive. Mitacs-funded, run 2021, results announced by press release 18 Apr 2024 and reposted on irenelyon.com 28 Apr 2026. | Single-arm, within-subject. Three time points: 12 weeks before the program (waitlist-style self-baseline), immediately pre, immediately post. Self-report measures (interoception, self-compassion, loneliness, pain, stress, trauma-related impairment, ACEs) plus cognitive tests. | Not reported | Relative to baseline: higher interoceptive awareness (body trust and safety, less worry about body sensations), higher self-compassion, a trend toward better relationship functioning, and self-reported attention/organization gains. Objective cognitive tests showed no meaningful change (ceiling at baseline). Participants with higher ACEs reported larger falls in perceived stress, physical symptom severity and trauma-related impairment. |

- **Limitations of this study:**
  - no randomization or active/attention control
  - no follow-up beyond post-program
  - self-selected paying participants
  - industry co-funded
  - findings released by the company before peer review
  - described in 2024 as "under scientific review"
  - no peer-reviewed paper found on PubMed as of Oct 2026 (searches on program and investigator terms)
- **No trials** were found of SBSM or the Tune-Up in ME/CFS, long COVID, fibromyalgia, POTS or any other named diagnosis. No ClinicalTrials.gov registration was identified.
- **Related generic evidence (not the branded program):**
  - Somatic Experiencing: a small RCT literature, mostly in PTSD and low back pain, plus a 2021 scoping review (Kuhfuß et al., *Eur J Psychotraumatol*).
  - Feldenkrais: small RCTs and systematic reviews with mixed, low-certainty results for balance and pain.
  - Interoceptive or body-awareness therapies (e.g., MABT, Price) have been tested separately.
  - None of these validate the "stored trauma causes chronic illness" model or polyvagal claims about autoimmunity.
- **Company's stance:** the FAQ argues that accurate data are hard to obtain in living nervous systems and relies mainly on testimonials, with a disclaimer that testimonials "have not been scientifically evaluated."

## 8. Controversies / criticisms

- **Sweeping causal claims.** Statements such as "the nervous system governs everything" and that nearly all chronic and mental illness stems from unresolved traumatic stress go beyond the evidence. Polyvagal theory's physiological claims are themselves contested (e.g., Grossman P, *Biol Psychol* 2023, "Fundamental challenges and likely refutations of the five basic premises of the polyvagal theory").
- **Illness attribution.** Linking autoimmune disease, CFS and fibromyalgia mainly to stored trauma or "freeze" risks implying that patients' illnesses are of their own psychological making. This is a common objection from ME/CFS advocacy communities to trauma- and brain-retraining framings.
- **Pseudoscientific adjuncts.** The FAQ endorses chelation, "oxygen therapy," and a commercial zeolite detox product the founders use and recommend. "Fluid/chemical trauma" and earthing ("electron exchange") have no evidence base. Recommending a branded supplement alongside the program raises a conflict-of-interest concern.
- **Marketing.** Outcomes evidence is mostly testimonials. Participant counts are inconsistent across pages (9,500 vs 25,000 vs 27,000). There are no refunds. The single evaluation was publicized by press release before peer review.
- **Safety guardrails.** The program does screen out acute suicidality, severe psychiatric illness and unsafe living situations, and it warns that the practices can surface memories and emotions. It is self-directed, without clinical supervision.

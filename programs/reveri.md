## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
- **Hypnosis as a distinct brain state.** Reveri's science page rests on Jiang et al. 2017 (fMRI, N = 57, high vs. low hypnotizables), which reports three changes during hypnosis:
  - less activity in the dorsal anterior cingulate / salience ("alarm") network, framed as less worry;
  - more DLPFC–insula connectivity, framed as stronger mind–body control;
  - less DLPFC–default mode network connectivity, framed as less self-consciousness and self-doubt.
- **Focused attention, dissociation and suggestibility.** Spiegel's model has three components: absorption (narrowed, focused attention), dissociation (setting aside peripheral awareness, such as pain sensation) and suggestibility (uncritical acceptance of instructions). In pain, a hypnotic suggestion changes how nociceptive input is processed and appraised, for example by "filtering" the hurt out of the sensation. This is top-down modulation of pain affect and intensity.
- **Stress and arousal.** Hypnosis is framed as lowering physiological arousal and sympathetic tone. The site also cites the Huberman/Spiegel cyclic-sighing study (Balban et al. 2023) for breathing-based downregulation of arousal.
- **Hypnotizability as a trait.** Hypnotizability is framed as a stable cognitive skill, linked to executive flexibility (Faerman & Spiegel 2021, Sci Rep), that predicts who benefits. Users are screened with a remote version of the Hypnotic Induction Profile.
- **Implicit disorder model.** The app does not offer a disease-mechanism theory of chronic illness (no central-sensitization or limbic-impairment narrative). Symptoms are treated as states that attention and suggestion can modulate.

## 2. One mechanism or many?
**Mostly unified, with condition-specific emphasis.** The same induction (focused attention, physical relaxation, a "floating" sensation) is used across goals. Content then branches into goal-specific modules: "Manage Stress and Anxiety", sleep, pain relief, smoking, eating, focus. In practice the pain modules use analgesic or dissociative imagery (e.g., numbness, temperature change, filtering the sensation), and the stress modules use visualization and cognitive restructuring. Named syndromes such as fibromyalgia, ME/CFS, IBS or long COVID are not addressed, and no illness-specific mechanism is offered.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
- **Onboarding.** A hypnotizability test (remote HIP, scored 0–10, about 5–10 min) uses eyelid-closure and arm-levitation-type items. Spiegel's classic eye-roll sign is part of the in-person HIP.
- **Core session.** Guided audio of about 10 min (standard) or 4–5 min (brief):
  - induction (eyes up, slow breath, eyes close, body floating);
  - a goal-specific suggestion with sensorimotor change (e.g., tingling, numbness, lightness);
  - guided imagery (e.g., a "screen" for visualizing a stressor);
  - cognitive restructuring;
  - an exit count.
- **Interactive mode.** The user rates stress or pain 0–10 before and after each session, and taps or speaks to responses that branch the script. The current version presents this as AI-personalized, with notes that carry forward to the next session.
- **Refreshers.** 1–2 min self-hypnosis "breaks" teach users to self-induce, which reflects Spiegel's clinical emphasis that "all hypnosis is self-hypnosis."
- **Dose.** No fixed course; use is on demand and unlimited. The published data show the same per-session relief at session 1 and session 10, with no cumulative trend.
- **Adjuncts.** Content includes cyclic-sighing and breathing exercises.

## 4. Roots and influences
- **Clinical hypnosis.** The lineage runs from Mesmer and Braid through Erickson. More directly, it comes from Herbert Spiegel (David's father), who developed the Hypnotic Induction Profile and the eye-roll sign. The father–son book *Trance and Treatment* (1978; 2nd ed. 2004) is the foundation.
- **Spiegel's own clinical trials**, including:
  - supportive-expressive group therapy with self-hypnosis for metastatic breast cancer pain (1980s);
  - self-hypnotic relaxation during invasive procedures (Lang et al., Lancet 2000);
  - the brief single-session smoking protocol.
- **Neuroimaging of hypnosis** (Stanford fMRI work).
- **Relaxation-response and breathwork traditions.**
- **Consumer meditation apps.** Board and advisors have Headspace backgrounds, per the About page.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- **The app** is a direct-to-consumer wellness subscription with no FDA clearance. No payer coverage, employer or health-system deployment, or clinical-guideline mention was found on the company site, ClinicalTrials.gov or PubMed. A broader news search for employer deals was not possible.
- **Hypnosis in general** has mainstream footing:
  - Gut-directed hypnotherapy is guideline-endorsed for IBS (e.g., by the ACG and British Society of Gastroenterology).
  - Procedural hypnosis is used in some hospitals.
  - Clinical hypnosis delivered within psychotherapy can be billed under standard psychotherapy codes.
  - Those endorsements are for clinician-delivered or other hypnotherapy, not Reveri.

## 6. Evidence base
**App-specific (mostly observational and co-founder involved; item 3 is independent):**
1. Tran N, Saperia C, Neri E, Jo B, ... Maldonado J, Spiegel D. Effects of app delivered self hypnosis on stress management. *npj Digital Medicine* 2025 (published online 18 Dec 2025); DOI 10.1038/s41746-025-02182-0; PMID 41413254.
   - **Design:** retrospective, uncontrolled analysis of in-app ratings, Nov 2021–Jan 2025.
   - **Sample:** 282,893 sessions by 84,395 users.
   - **Result:** single-item 0–10 stress rating fell about 1.5 points pre-to-post in each of the first 10 sessions (d ≈ 0.71–0.78). Effects were larger with interactive and standard-length sessions, higher hypnotizability, older age, and paying members.
   - **No cumulative improvement** across sessions.
   - **Limitations the authors acknowledge:** no control group, a single-item outcome, an unvalidated remote HIP, an unknown clinical vs. non-clinical mix, and self-selected users. Before a Dec 2023 software update, missing pre/post scores defaulted to 10 and 1.
   - **Conflicts:** Spiegel is co-founder and scientific advisor of Reveri, and one author consulted for Reveri. Two senior authors without Reveri ties oversaw the analysis.
2. Saperia C, Bharti S, Kim B, Jo B, Neri E, Markovits J, ... Maldonado J, Lev-Ari S, Spiegel D. Effect of app based hypnosis on pain reduction in a large user cohort. *Research Square* preprint, posted 9 Sept 2026; DOI 10.21203/rs.3.rs-10731340/v1. **Not peer reviewed.**
   - **Sample:** 19,063 users and 59,147 sessions of the "Manage Pain" module, Dec 2021–Jul 2025. Of the 20,376 module users, 1,313 had no pain ratings and were excluded.
   - **Result:** 0–10 pain rating fell 1.16–1.3 points per session over the first 10 sessions (effect sizes about 0.53–0.62).
   - **Session-1 breakdown:** 22.5% had >50% reduction; 29.1% had 30–50%; 18.7% had no change; 6.6% got worse.
   - **No cumulative change** across sessions.
   - **Design:** uncontrolled, immediate pre-post self-report. Expectancy, demand and regression effects cannot be excluded.
3. Caggiano L, Karayannis NV, Collins C, Grooms D, Simon JE. A brief hypnosis intervention improves single-limb dynamic balance in people with chronic ankle instability: a crossover experimental pilot study. *Int J Clin Exp Hypn* 2025;73(2):181–197 (PMID 40020194; NCT06390072). This independent study (Ohio University) used an 8-min Reveri track, "Hypnosis Prep for Workout".
   - **Design:** randomized crossover pilot comparing the track with an 8-min ankle-education video. N = 24, unblinded.
   - **Result:** a modest improvement in lateral dynamic balance only. This is a single-session performance outcome, not a pain or symptom outcome.
4. Stanford open pilot NCT03931772, "Online Automated Self-Hypnosis Program" (PI Spiegel).
   - **Design:** non-randomized, single-group trial with smoking-cessation and pain arms, delivered via Amazon Alexa or the Reveri app.
   - **Status:** N = 152 enrolled; completed Feb 2022. No results posted, and no publication found.
5. Spiegel D. Tranceformation: digital dissemination of hypnosis. *Neuron* 2024;112(3):340–341 (PMID 38262415). A commentary arguing for app-based delivery; not a trial.

**Generic hypnosis evidence the brand cites (not Reveri):**
- Lang EV, Benotsch EG, Fick LJ, ... Spiegel D. Adjunctive non-pharmacological analgesia for invasive medical procedures: a randomised trial. *Lancet* 2000;355:1486–1490 (PMID 10801169).
  - N = 241 RCT comparing self-hypnotic relaxation, structured attention and standard care.
  - With hypnosis, pain stayed flat during the procedure (it rose with standard care), drug use was lower, and procedures were shorter.
- Jiang H, White MP, Greicius MD, Waelde LC, Spiegel D. Brain activity and functional connectivity associated with hypnosis. *Cereb Cortex* 2017 (PMID 27469596). fMRI, N = 57.
- Am J Med 2021 commentary, "Hypnosis: the most effective treatment you have yet to prescribe" (PMID 33171103).
- The broader hypnosis literature includes meta-analyses for pain (e.g., Thompson et al. 2019; Milling and colleagues), RCTs of gut-directed hypnotherapy for IBS, and procedural and analgesic hypnosis studies. This supports hypnosis as a technique, not the app.

**Gaps.**
- No randomized or active-controlled trial of Reveri for any chronic condition. The only randomized use is the single-session ankle-balance pilot (N = 24).
- No follow-up beyond the session.
- No clinical populations (fibromyalgia, migraine, chronic low back pain) studied as such.
- "Pain reduction 30–42% across clinical studies" on the site refers to non-app hypnosis studies.
- Internal claims such as "77% feel better in one session" are unsourced.
- ClinicalTrials.gov (checked Oct 2026) lists two Reveri studies: the uncontrolled Stanford pilot (no results posted) and the Ohio ankle-balance crossover (NCT06390072).
- For contrast, an RCT of a *different* self-hypnotic relaxation app (Lang et al. 2021, *Int J Clin Exp Hypn* 69:277–295, PMID 33724898) found modest and inconsistent advantages over a control app. This shows that app-delivered hypnosis does not automatically reproduce in-person effects.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory
- **2017–2021:** Stanford pilot of an Alexa-delivered automated self-hypnosis program (from 2017); company start c. 2020; iOS app released 9 Mar 2021.
- **Feb 2022:** Huberman Lab episode with Spiegel, featuring a Reveri promotion and Huberman's personal endorsement. This was the main amplification event; the stress-module user base begins rising in the Nov 2021–2022 data.
- **2022–2024:** Android release (after Feb 2022), plus podcast endorsements (Tim Ferriss, Rich Roll) and press (the Financial Times is quoted on the site).
- **Aug 2023:** UK-market rebrand (Mother Design) and quit-vaping content (May 2023). Press reported more than 300k registered users.
- **Through Jan 2025:** at least 84,395 users completed at least one stress session, of whom 40.5% were paying members. The pain cohort had at least 19,063 users. Total registered users were probably 300k–1M by 2025–26 (inference from the 2023 press figure).
- **2025–26:** Rebranded around an AI-personalized "Dr. Spiegel's method", alongside the Dec 2025 npj publication and the Sept 2026 pain preprint.
- **App stores (Oct 2026):** iOS 4.5★ from 4,487 ratings; Google Play 4.1★ from 1.75K reviews, 100K+ downloads. Both apps were updated in Oct 2026, so the product is actively maintained.
- **Not available:** Reddit volume (Reddit search and JSON endpoints blocked automated access in Oct 2026) and Google Trends (no programmatic access).

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **Founder-conflicted, uncontrolled evidence.** It is marketed with "science settled" rhetoric, and the large-N retrospective data are pre-post single-item ratings with no comparator.
- **Misattributed effects.** Pain claims rest on generic hypnosis trials and internal data, not app RCTs.
- **Celebrity amplification.** Endorsements from Huberman and Ferriss (including Ferriss's claim that his pain "dropped to nearly zero") are promotional anecdote. Huberman has faced wider criticism for overstating evidence for wellness products.
- **Price.** A high monthly price ($44.95) relative to typical meditation apps.
- **Unequal benefit.** Benefit depends on hypnotizability; roughly a third of people are low-hypnotizable.
- **Little substantive controversy** compared with limbic-retraining programs. The product makes no claims to cure ME/CFS or similar conditions, and it carries wellness disclaimers.

## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
- **Core idea (all variants):** symptoms reflect *dysregulated brain oscillations or network dynamics*. Real-time feedback of EEG lets the brain learn, by operant conditioning, to shift toward more regulated patterns, and the learning is thought to persist through neuroplasticity.
- **Frequency-band / "arousal" model (classic and Othmer):** conditions map onto under- or over-arousal and instability. Examples: excess theta or low SMR/beta in ADHD; low SMR and hyperarousal in insomnia and pain; excess high beta in anxiety. Othmer ILF (infra-low frequency, below 0.1 Hz) claims to train the brain's slowest regulatory rhythms that govern arousal, autonomic and affective state. Protocols are adjusted session by session based on the client's reported state.
- **Chronic pain specifically:** pain is framed as a central-nervous-system state (central sensitisation / thalamocortical dysrhythmia). Up-training SMR or alpha and down-training theta/high-beta over sensorimotor cortex is meant to "gate" pain processing. Newer research protocols target the limbic/affective network: amygdala EEG fingerprint NF (Goldway 2019, Or-Borichev 2025) and pgACC-to-S1 connectivity NF (Anderson 2025).
- **qEEG / LORETA z-score model:** a patient's EEG is compared with a normative database. Deviations (z-scores) in power, coherence or source-localised activity are treated as the dysfunction, and training pushes metrics toward the norm.
- **NeurOptimal ("Dynamical Neurofeedback"):** explicitly *no* diagnosis or protocol. The software detects moment-to-moment "turbulence" in the EEG and briefly interrupts music, on the claim that the brain notices and self-organises. It is presented as non-medical training of a self-regulating system rather than a treatment of a disorder.
- **Consumer meditation headbands (Muse):** EEG (mainly alpha/beta-derived "calm" metrics) is turned into weather sounds to train focused attention during meditation. The mechanism claim is attention/mind-wandering regulation, the mindfulness model.
- **Non-specific mechanisms (critics' view):** expectancy, therapist contact, relaxation and self-efficacy may account for much of the clinical benefit (Thibault & Raz 2017).

## 2. One mechanism or many?
**Mostly unified, with condition-specific emphasis.** The shared premise is that dysregulated brain rhythms or networks are a common pathway across disorders. Clinic practice usually picks electrode site and frequency by symptom cluster: SMR/C4 for hyperarousal and pain, theta/beta at Cz for ADHD, alpha-theta for trauma and addiction, ILF at right-hemisphere sites for arousal and affect. qEEG-guided practice individualises by the person's deviation pattern rather than by diagnosis. NeurOptimal sits at the other extreme: one non-specific algorithm for everyone, with diagnosis treated as irrelevant. None of the brands offers a distinct mechanism for ME/CFS, POTS or MCS; fibromyalgia and migraine are folded into the central-dysregulation/arousal model.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
- **Assessment:** intake symptom checklists. Many clinics do a 19-channel qEEG "brain map" (eyes open/closed) compared with a normative database. Othmer clinicians instead titrate by symptom report and do not require a qEEG.
- **Session:** 1–2 scalp electrodes (or 19 for z-score/LORETA training) plus ear references. The client watches an animation or game, or listens to music, that brightens, moves or plays when target activity is achieved. Sessions last 20–40 min of training; 2–3 sessions/week.
- **Course:** typically 20–40 sessions (Kayiran 2010 used 20 sessions over 4 weeks; Wu 2021 used 8 weeks; NF trials for ADHD used about 30–40). Booster sessions as needed.
- **Protocol families:** SMR (12–15 Hz) up-training with theta/high-beta inhibit (pain, sleep, epilepsy); theta/beta ratio (ADHD); alpha-theta "deep state" (PTSD, addiction; Peniston protocol); ILF (Othmer; frequency titrated to symptoms, often paired with inter-hemispheric "synchrony" training); live z-score/LORETA (BrainMaster, Thatcher); limbic "EFP" NF (research only, e.g. Prism/GrayMatters Health).
- **NeurOptimal:** 33-minute automated session with music; minimal clinician skill needed; home rental model.
- **Home/consumer:** Myndlift (Muse headband, app games, remote clinician-set protocols); Muse app meditation with real-time EEG sonification and sleep tracking. Mendi is often grouped here but uses prefrontal fNIRS, not EEG (mendi.io; headset about US$284–379, no subscription).
- **Self-care framing:** passive-to-active self-regulation. Most clinics combine training with sleep hygiene, psychotherapy or relaxation; in trauma care it is used as an adjunct to psychotherapy (van der Kolk).

## 4. Roots and influences
- **Biofeedback movement of the 1960s–70s:** Kamiya's alpha-state work; Sterman's SMR/epilepsy studies; founding of the Biofeedback Research Society (October 1969; renamed Biofeedback Society of America 1976 and AAPB 1989).
- **Behaviourism / operant conditioning:** the formal learning model.
- **Meditation / contemplative traditions:** the alpha "calm" states and the alpha-theta protocols drew on 1960s–70s EEG studies of Zen and yogic meditators; Muse brands itself as meditation support.
- **Clinical neurophysiology / qEEG and source localisation:** normative databases (E. Roy John, Thatcher); sLORETA (Pascual-Marqui).
- **Systems / dynamical-systems theory:** NeurOptimal's "dynamical" and non-linear framing.
- **Arousal-regulation and stress physiology:** the Othmers' model of autonomic and arousal regulation.
- **Modern pain neuroscience:** central sensitisation and thalamocortical dysrhythmia, used to justify pain protocols.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- **Adoption:**
  - Used in some university and VA research, in integrative pain and psychiatry clinics, and widely in private practice (psychologists, LPCs, chiropractors).
  - Certification is available through BCIA (Board Certified in Neurofeedback, BCN; BCIA's first national exam was in 1980).
  - Not recommended in major pain, fibromyalgia or ADHD guidelines as first-line care. Earlier enthusiasm for ADHD, e.g. the "Level 1 – Best Support" rating of biofeedback for ADHD in the PracticeWise evidence chart circulated by the AAP (2010–12 editions, as cited by Bink et al. 2019), was undercut by the blinded trials described in §6. The 2020 chart edition has no ADHD row; it rates biofeedback Level 2 for anxiety and Level 5 (no support) for autism.
- **FDA status:**
  - Clinical EEG biofeedback devices fall under the FDA Class II "biofeedback device" regulation (21 CFR 882.5050, product code HCC, 510(k)-exempt), intended for relaxation training, not disease treatment.
  - NeurOptimal and Muse disclaim medical treatment and market as wellness/training tools; NeurOptimal states it "does not diagnose, treat, mitigate, prevent or cure any disease."
- **Insurance:**
  - Inconsistent. Some plans pay under biofeedback or psychotherapy codes when it is part of mental-health treatment.
  - Aetna (CPB 0132) explicitly excludes EEG biofeedback for headache and lists biofeedback for ADHD, anxiety, depression, insomnia, PTSD and chronic pain/fibromyalgia as experimental.
  - Most patients pay cash or use FSA/HSA.

## 6. Evidence base
Generic EEG neurofeedback trials (none tests a branded consumer product unless noted):

**Chronic pain, fibromyalgia, headache**
- Kayiran S et al. 2010, *Appl Psychophysiol Biofeedback* 35:293–302. Rater-blind RCT, N=36 fibromyalgia. 20 SMR sessions vs escitalopram; NF superior on pain, mood and QoL (p<.05). Small; active drug comparator; not blinded to participants. EEG band amplitudes did not change (only theta/SMR ratio fell), which weakens the specific-mechanism claim.
- Caro XJ & Winter EF 2011, *Appl Psychophysiol Biofeedback* 36:193–200. Non-randomised pilot, 15 NF vs 63 usual-care fibromyalgia patients. Improved attention, tenderness, pain and fatigue.
- Wu YL et al. 2021, *Pain Manag Nurs* 22:755–763. RCT, N=80 (60 NF vs 20 telephone support). 8-week SMR/alpha NF improved pain severity, pain interference, FIQ and sleep latency.
- Goldway N et al. 2019, *NeuroImage* 186:758–770. Placebo-controlled RCT of amygdala-EEG-fingerprint NF, N=34 fibromyalgia (25 real, 9 sham). No specific effect at end of training; delayed pain benefit at follow-up, mediated by sleep.
- Or-Borichev A et al. 2025, *BMC Med* 23:304. Double-blind RCT, N=47 (amygdala-EFP NF 21, sham 13, TAU 13). Real NF reduced symptom severity (ηp²=.27) and FIQ (ηp²=.33). Sham also reduced pain long-term.
- Anderson L et al. 2025, *Sci Rep* 15:209. Placebo-controlled feasibility RCT, N=30 **fibromyalgia** (15 active, 15 placebo; 12 sessions of pgACC-to-S1 alpha effective-connectivity NF). Safe and feasible; pain and FIQR decreases comparable in active and placebo arms. A private NF practitioner co-author programmed the protocols.
- Arina GA et al. 2022, *Front Hum Neurosci* 16:891323. Sham-controlled crossover of **ILF (Othmer-type)** NF in tension-type headache. Real but not sham sessions lowered headache probability. Small; Moscow group; a psychoeducation session and ongoing emotional support were included for all.
- Patel K et al. 2020, *Eur J Pain* 24:1440–1457. Meta-analysis of 21 studies. Pooled RCT effect on pain d=−0.76 (95% CI −1.31 to −0.20), I²=73%; mostly low-quality evidence.
- Hesam-Shariati N et al. 2022, *Eur J Neurol* 29:921–936. Meta-analysis of 10 RCTs plus 13 non-randomised studies. Clinically meaningful short-term pain reduction, low certainty (GRADE); moderate after removing high-risk-of-bias studies.
- Torres CB (Barbosa-Torres) et al. 2024, *Eur Arch Psychiatry Clin Neurosci* 274:981–999. Systematic review of 17 fibromyalgia NF studies. Mostly SMR protocols; protocol heterogeneity limits conclusions.
- Schuurman BB et al. 2024, *J Clin Med* 13:2813. Scoping review of 32 studies. Probable short-term analgesia; band rationale unclear.

**PTSD / trauma**
- van der Kolk BA et al. 2016, *PLoS One* 11:e0166752. RCT vs waitlist, N=52 chronic PTSD (28 NF, 24 waitlist; 24 sessions of right-temporal T4–P4 training, not alpha-theta). Post-treatment PTSD diagnosis 27% NF vs 68% waitlist. No sham control.
- Askovic M et al. 2023, *Eur J Psychotraumatol* 14:2257435. Review of 10 controlled studies; meta-analysis of 7 RCTs (N=215). SMD −1.76 (95% CI −2.69 to −0.83) for PTSD symptoms; studies small and heterogeneous. First author directs a neurofeedback institute (declared).

**ADHD (the most rigorously tested indication; a cautionary benchmark)**
- Cortese S et al. 2016, *JAACAP* 55:444–455. Meta-analysis of 13 RCTs, N=520. Effects only on unblinded (parent) ratings; null for probably-blinded raters and sham-controlled trials.
- Schönenberg M et al. 2017, *Lancet Psychiatry* 4:673–684. Triple-blind RCT, N=118 adults. NF not superior to sham or to group CBT; all groups improved.
- Neurofeedback Collaborative Group (Arnold LE et al.) 2021, *JAACAP* 60:841–855. Double-blind sham-controlled RCT, N=144 children. Both arms improved; no specific NF effect at end of treatment or at 13 months. The 25-month follow-up (NF Collaborative Group 2023, *JAACAP* 62:435–446; n=120) also found no difference and attributed the large within-group gains to non-specific effects.

**ILF / Othmer and NeurOptimal**
- ILF: Bazzana F et al. 2022, *Front Hum Neurosci* 16:920659 (mixed-studies review of 18 mostly case/uncontrolled studies). Individualisation hampers controlled testing.
- ILF: Carlson J et al. 2025, *Explore* 21:103137. RCT in combat veterans with post-concussion symptoms, N=87 enrolled (36 NF and 38 control completers; control = 15-min health discussions). Improvements in headache, sleep and attention reported, but the abstract reports baseline-to-end changes and gives no clear between-group test.
- A 2022 *Front Hum Neurosci* research topic was dominated by ILF case reports.
- NeurOptimal: no peer-reviewed RCT located in PubMed.
- Muse: research mostly uses it as an EEG/meditation tool. Company-sponsored studies (e.g. 2021 Western University/Interaxon sleep study, cited on the site) are not trials of clinical pain or fatigue outcomes.

**Gaps and critiques**
- Few double-blind sham-controlled trials outside ADHD; where they exist (ADHD), specific effects largely vanish.
- Pain trials are small, often unblinded, with heterogeneous protocols and many investigator/practitioner-led studies.
- No trials in ME/CFS, POTS, MCS, IBS or long COVID located.
- CRED-nf reporting checklist (Ros T et al. 2020, *Brain* 143:1674–1685) was created because of design weaknesses across the field.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory
- **1960s–70s:** Kamiya's alpha discrimination study (1962) and his 1968 *Psychology Today* article popularised an "alpha state" of alert calm. Sterman's SMR work ran from cats (1967) to an epileptic patient (1971). Lubar's hyperkinetic-child report followed in 1976. The alpha-feedback vogue cooled in the late 1970s and 80s, although professional activity continued (biofeedback certification exam 1980; AAPB renamed 1989); no usage data quantify the decline.
- **1990s:** clinical revival (Othmer EEG Spectrum network; Society for the Study of Neuronal Regulation by 1995, later ISNR; BCIA neurofeedback certification).
- **2000s:** NeurOptimal (2000) brought low-skill turnkey and rental systems. LORETA/z-score training grew (2005–10).
- **2010s:** consumer EEG arrived with Muse (2014). The van der Kolk book *The Body Keeps the Score* (2014; #1 *NYT* bestseller, nearly 300 weeks on the list) features neurofeedback among its trauma treatments (publisher description), a major popularity driver for trauma clients. The negative ADHD meta-analysis (2016) and trials (2017, 2021) followed.
- **2020s:**
  - The home/remote model expanded during COVID (Myndlift: 41k+ members, 1.6M sessions; Muse: 500k+ users).
  - TikTok and Instagram "brain map" marketing.
  - Academic limbic-NF fibromyalgia trials (2019, 2025) and pain meta-analyses (2020, 2022).
- **Literature:** PubMed records with "neurofeedback" in title/abstract rose from 20 (2005) to 58 (2010), 149 (2015), 237 (2020) and 340 (2025).
- **Reddit:** r/Neurofeedback is a modest subreddit, with about 6,100 subscribers at the last third-party count (subredditstats.com, Dec 2023); reddit.com blocks automated fetches, so no 2026 count was obtained. Google Trends also blocked automated queries (HTTP 429). Typical discussion mixes strong testimonials (anxiety, sleep, trauma) with complaints about cost, upselling of qEEG maps, and transient or "overtraining" side effects (headache, insomnia, agitation).

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **Placebo / "neuroplacebo":** Thibault, Lifshitz & Raz (*Brain* 2017; *Am Psychol* 2017) argue that clinical benefits largely reflect psychosocial and placebo mechanisms, since sham NF often matches real NF.
- **Brain maps:** normative qEEG brain maps are criticised as over-sold diagnostics, with little validation for prescribing protocols.
- **NeurOptimal's claims:** its "no diagnosis, no side effects, millions of sessions" marketing pairs with a wellness disclaimer that sidesteps FDA efficacy claims.
- **Cost and access:** courses of US$2,000–8,000 are high for evidence that is uncertain outside a few indications.
- **Practitioner quality:** variable; practitioners may be unlicensed or only device-trained.
- **Side effects:** reported side effects (headache, fatigue, sleep disruption, emotional flooding) are under-studied.

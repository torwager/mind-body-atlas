# Specialist physiotherapy for functional motor disorder (Physio4FMD / Nielsen protocol)

## 1. Proposed mechanism(s)
- **Abnormal top-down motor prediction plus self-focused attention.** FND is modelled in a predictive-processing / Bayesian framework (Edwards et al. 2012). An abnormally precise prior expectation of abnormal movement ("my leg is weak"), shaped by illness beliefs and often seeded by a triggering event (injury, panic, illness), combines with attention focused on the body to produce real, involuntary motor symptoms. These are experienced as not self-generated because of an altered sense of agency.
- **Two key drivers in the trial TIDieR description:** (1) symptoms need attention directed to the body, and they diminish when attention is distracted; (2) a non-voluntary expectation of abnormal movement, tied to illness beliefs.
- **Biopsychosocial formulation.** It lists predisposing factors (adverse experiences, prior illness, anxiety/depression), precipitating triggers (injury, physical illness, panic/dissociation at onset), and perpetuating factors. Perpetuating factors include self-focused attention, unhelpful illness beliefs, learned/habitual abnormal movement patterns, deconditioning, reinforcement of symptomatic movement, pain and fatigue.
- **Positive signs as proof of intact hardware.** Inconsistencies used in diagnosis (Hoover's sign, entrainment of tremor, normal movement when distracted) are taken as evidence that the motor system is structurally intact and that the problem is in "software" (control and attention), not damage.
- It is not psychodynamic. Psychological factors are framed as risk factors rather than obligatory causes, consistent with DSM-5's removal of the requirement for a psychological stressor in FND.

## 2. One mechanism or many?
- **Disorder-specific.** The protocol is built only for functional *motor* disorder and makes no cross-disorder claims, for example about ME/CFS, fibromyalgia or chronic pain.
- **Within FMD it is one model with phenotype-specific tactics.** The attention/prediction model applies to all motor phenotypes. Movement-retraining strategies are tailored to weakness, gait, tremor, dystonia, jerks and so on.
- The broader FND school (Stone, Carson, Edwards) uses related predictive-processing language for dissociative seizures and persistent postural-perceptual dizziness (PPPD). Those are treated with other modalities, e.g., CBT for dissociative seizures in the CODES trial.
- Pain, fatigue and dissociative seizures are recognised comorbidities. They are addressed with self-management (pacing, sleep) but are not mechanistically reframed by this protocol.

## 3. Treatments and self-care strategies
**Structure (trial protocol):** neurologist diagnosis with a positive explanation, then 9 one-hour sessions within 3 weeks (often delivered as 5 consecutive days), starting within 6 weeks of the neurology consult, plus a follow-up session. Only a minority of FMD patients are deemed suitable: in the feasibility RCT, 32% of patients screened met inclusion criteria.

1. **Assessment and formulation.** Full history and physical exam. Patient and therapist co-write a biopsychosocial formulation of how symptoms developed: risk factors, trigger, panic/dissociation at onset, self-focused attention, reinforcement of abnormal movement.
2. **Education.** Explains the attention/expectation model and demonstrates the patient's own positive signs, e.g., showing that a "weak" leg works during distraction, to build belief that recovery is possible.
3. **Movement retraining with an external focus of attention (implicit motor learning).** This is a key difference from usual neurorehab, which uses explicit, body-focused practice. Normal movement is elicited within tasks (standing, walking, sit-to-stand) using:
   - distraction and dual tasks;
   - automatic or "rehearsed" movements;
   - altered rhythm/speed and music/metronome;
   - visualisation/motor imagery;
   - novel or unusual movements (walking backwards, sideways, marching);
   - entrainment and redirection for tremor;
   - progressive reshaping toward normal patterns.
4. **Reducing reinforcement of abnormal patterns.** Weaning walking aids, splints and wheelchairs where appropriate. Avoiding compensatory strategies and "symptom-focused" exercises.
5. **Self-management plan (long-term emphasis).** Daily written reflections in the workbook. A relapse/setback plan with "go-to" strategies for symptom flares. Graded return to activity and goal-setting. Advice on pain, fatigue, sleep and concentration (pacing, managing boom–bust).
6. **Therapist training.** A 5-day course, a manual, and per-patient supervisory calls in the trial to ensure fidelity.

Often combined in multidisciplinary FND services with occupational therapy (2020 OT consensus), speech therapy, and psychological therapy (CBT/CODES for dissociative seizures, or psychotherapy for comorbid anxiety, depression or trauma).

## 4. Roots and influences
- **Motor neuroscience:** predictive coding / free-energy accounts of action (Friston; Edwards et al. 2012), sense of agency, and motor learning theory (Wulf's external-focus/implicit learning).
- **History of "hysteria":** Charcot, Janet's dissociation and attention concepts, Babinski. Diagnostic positive signs (Hoover 1908).
- **Prior physical-rehab programs for "psychogenic" movement disorders:** Mayo Clinic motor-retraining protocol (Czarnecki et al. 2012), Norwegian inpatient gait rehabilitation (Jordbru et al. 2014), and behavioural/reinforcement-based rehabilitation of conversion disorder from the 1980s–90s.
- **CBT and illness-perception models:** Leventhal's common-sense model, operant behavioural principles (not reinforcing dysfunction), graded activity.
- It does not draw on contemplative or somatic traditions. Movement work resembles somatic re-education only superficially, since the emphasis is on external focus, not body awareness.

## 5. Mainstream adoption and insurance
- **UK.** Delivered within NHS neurology/neuro-rehab services. The trial ran at 11 NHS sites in England and Scotland. NHS Scotland's FND pathway says therapy should be FND-specific, citing distraction-based physiotherapy. NHS Wales' FND pathway places physiotherapy within community/outpatient tiers. There is no NICE guideline specific to FND.
- **International.** The 2015 consensus is the standard reference for FND physiotherapy worldwide. It is widely cited by FND rehabilitation services in the US, Australia, Canada and Europe (specific institutional adoption not verified here). It is promoted by the FND Society (Nielsen, Edwards, Stone and Carson are founding/board members) and by patient charities (FND Hope, FND Action).
- **Insurance.** Reimbursed as standard physiotherapy (NHS; private insurers; US Medicare/commercial plans under PT CPT codes). Coverage depends on payer medical-necessity criteria, and some older payer policies list neuromuscular re-education only for "structural" neurological diagnoses. No dedicated billing code exists.
- **Cost-effectiveness.** Incremental cost per QALY was £4,133, with an 86% probability of being cost-effective at £20k/QALY. From a societal perspective (productivity) the intervention was dominant.

## 6. Evidence base
Trials of **this specific protocol** (all by the developing group):
- **Nielsen et al. 2015, *J Neurol* 262:674–81.** Prospective uncontrolled cohort of a 5-day programme, n=47 (mean symptom duration 5.5 y; 64% off work). 65% rated themselves "much/very much improved" at end of treatment and 55% at 3 months. SF-36 physical domains, Berg balance and 10-m walk improved; mental health did not change.
- **Nielsen et al. 2017, *JNNP* 88:484–90 (feasibility RCT, NCT02275000).** n=60 (57 analysed), single tertiary centre, vs treatment as usual. At 6 months, 72% vs 18% rated themselves improved. SF-36 PF adjusted difference was 19.8 points (95% CI 10.2–29.5); d=0.46–0.79 across 3 of 8 SF-36 domains. Incremental cost was £12,087/QALY.
- **Nielsen et al. 2024, *Lancet Neurol* 23:675–86 (Physio4FMD phase 3, ISRCTN56136713).** n=355 randomised at 11 sites; 241 in the primary analysis after 89 COVID-related exclusions. The primary outcome, SF-36 PF at 12 months, was **not significantly different** (adjusted mean difference 3.5, 95% CI −2.3 to 9.3; powered for 9 points).
  - The 89 COVID exclusions were imbalanced (27 specialist physio vs 62 TAU), leaving 138 vs 103 analysed.
  - Secondary outcomes favoured the intervention: CGI-I "much improved"/"improved" 59% (81/138) vs 38% (39/102), OR 2.3 (95% CI 1.4–3.9); at 6 months OR 4.7. The authors describe SF-36 mental health, confidence in the diagnosis, and IPQ-R personal control/coherence as *nominal* improvements, without multiplicity correction. At 6 months there were also differences in role-physical, social functioning and EQ-5D.
  - No treatment-related serious adverse events.
  - Full report: **HTA 2025 29(34)**. Economic analysis: **Hunter et al. 2025, *Neurol Clin Pract***.
- **Nielsen et al. 2025, *J Psychosom Res* 190:112056.** Prognostic analysis of the intervention arm. Greater perceived personal control predicted improvement; belief in permanence of symptoms and older age predicted non-improvement. Illness duration, pain and fatigue did *not* predict outcome.

Related trials of **generic physical rehabilitation for FND** (not this protocol):
- **Jordbru et al. 2014, *J Rehabil Med* 46:181–7.** Crossover RCT (n=60) of 3-week inpatient adapted physical activity for psychogenic gait. Large FIM/FMS gains, maintained at 1 year.
- **Czarnecki et al. 2012, *Parkinsonism Relat Disord* 18:247–51.** Mayo 5-day motor-retraining protocol, with a historical control.

**Limitations.**
- All protocol trials were run by the developers.
- Outcomes are mostly self-reported, and the trials were unblinded to participants.
- The control arm also received physiotherapy, and participants waited longer to start it.
- COVID disruption excluded 25% of randomised participants from the primary analysis.
- Many secondary outcomes were tested without multiplicity correction.
- The large feasibility-trial effect (~20 points) shrank to ~3.5 points in the multicentre pragmatic trial.

## 7. Popularity and trajectory
- **2012:** Edwards' Bayesian model (*Brain*), now ~590 Europe PMC citations, lays the conceptual foundation.
- **2014–2015:** Consensus recommendations (JNNP; ~270 citations) and the 5-day cohort study. FND physiotherapy becomes a recognised specialty interest.
- **2016–2017:** Feasibility RCT (~170 citations), with a JNNP editorial. Specialist FND physio services spread across the UK.
- **2018–2022:** Phase 3 trial registered (2018), protocol published (2019), recruitment interrupted by COVID. FND Society founded (2019), with growing international conferences. Patient-facing promotion via neurosymptoms.org, FND Hope and FND Action. FND visibility rises in social media (TikTok/"FND tics" in 2020–21; r/FND subreddit). Manual and workbook (Nielsen & Holt) released openly on SGUL figshare under CC BY 4.0 (April 2024).
- **2024–2025:** Lancet Neurology publication (null primary outcome, positive secondaries), HTA monograph, cost-utility paper. Coverage in the UK clinical and legal press (e.g., Keoghs insurer/legal commentary) and critical coverage in ME/CFS-adjacent blogs.
- **Reach.** Each UK specialist service treats tens to low hundreds of patients a year, so cumulative reach across UK and international services adopting the approach is plausibly 10^4–10^5 patients. This is an estimate; no registry exists.

## 8. Controversies / criticisms
- **Null primary outcome.** Critics (e.g., David Tuller, Virology Blog, April 2025) argue the trial shows no meaningful benefit: both arms remained severely disabled (SF-36 PF ~37/100 at 12 months), and the secondary "improvement" ratings reflect unblinded, subjective reporting and attentive care. FND researchers counter that the SF-36 PF may be a poor outcome measure for FMD and that the active comparator (community physio) narrowed the difference. A parallel argument followed the CODES trial for dissociative seizures. Tuller also sent a letter (May 2025) asking *Neurol Clin Pract* to correct the Hunter et al. claim that Pick et al. (2020) recommended the CGI-I as the primary outcome for FND trials; no correction had been reported at the time.
- **Imbalanced exclusions.** More TAU than intervention participants (62 vs 27) were excluded from the primary analysis because of COVID disruption.
- **Diagnostic contestation.** Patient communities overlapping with ME/CFS and long COVID are wary of the "functional" label and of its use in medicolegal and insurance contexts. Several investigators disclose expert-witness work in FND cases.
- **Generalisability.** Only about a third of FMD clinic patients met suitability criteria in the feasibility RCT (32%). Effects in children, minority groups, and people with severe pain, fatigue or dissociative seizures are unclear.
- **Investigator allegiance.** All protocol-specific trials come from the developers.
